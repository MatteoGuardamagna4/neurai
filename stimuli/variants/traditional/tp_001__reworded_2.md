# Explanation

Target-profit questions ask what volume a business must sell to achieve a specified profit, and the arithmetic is the break-even calculation with a single additional term. Each unit sold contributes its margin, the price less the variable cost, first to the fixed costs and subsequently to profit, so the required quantity equals the fixed costs plus the desired profit, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Taxation alters what that required profit is. A company does not retain the profit it earns; it retains the amount remaining after the tax authority has taken its share. If the tax rate is one quarter, the owners retain 75 cents of every euro of pre-tax profit, so a target expressed after tax is smaller than the profit the business must actually generate. Converting the one into the other requires dividing the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate.

The required volume is therefore:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop incurs fixed costs of EUR 120,000 per year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner requires EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit required = 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = EUR 60,000. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 60,000) / 12 = 15,000 units.

The characteristic error is to add the after-tax target directly to the fixed costs. For the workshop this gives (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units, and at that volume the pre-tax profit is precisely EUR 45,000, of which the tax authority takes one quarter, leaving the owner EUR 33,750 and well short of the target. The profit must first be grossed up, and thereafter treated as though it were an additional fixed cost. Two steps, in this sequence: gross up the target, then divide by the contribution margin. Omitting the first step invariably understates the volume the company must sell.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: Sales volume is determined by the profit the company must earn before tax, since tax is levied on that profit. The figure provided is measured after tax, so it is not yet the figure required.

Hint 2: Convert the target: if tax takes one fifth of the pre-tax profit, the after-tax figure is only four fifths of it. Divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate to obtain the pre-tax profit.

Hint 3: Add that pre-tax profit to the fixed costs and divide the total by the contribution margin per unit, namely the price less the variable cost.

# Worked solution

Step 1: gross up the target. Tax takes 20%, so the after-tax profit is 80% of the pre-tax profit: 128,000 / 0.8 = EUR 160,000 must be earned before tax. Step 2: calculate the contribution margin: 90 - 50 = EUR 40 per unit. Step 3: the contribution must cover the fixed costs and the pre-tax profit: (300,000 + 160,000) / 40 = 11,500 units. Adding the after-tax target directly would give (300,000 + 128,000) / 40 = 10,700 units, which leaves the company short once the tax liability falls due.
