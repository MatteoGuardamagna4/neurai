# Explanation

Every business that sells a product has a threshold below which it incurs a loss and above which it earns a profit; break-even analysis identifies that threshold. Doing so requires three figures that must be kept strictly distinct: the costs that remain constant irrespective of activity, the revenue from a single sale, and the cost added by one additional sale. Rent, a licence and insurance fall into the first category; ingredients and packaging into the third.

The difference between the price and the variable cost is the contribution margin, and it is the only portion of a sale available to meet other expenses. A meal sold at EUR 12 that consumes EUR 4.50 of ingredients does not contribute EUR 12 towards the rent; it contributes EUR 7.50, since the remaining EUR 4.50 has already been expended on the meal.

Dividing the fixed costs by this margin yields the volume at which they are precisely covered:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A bike repair shop incurs EUR 2,400 per month for its workshop, tools and insurance. A standard service is priced at EUR 50 and requires EUR 20 of parts and consumables. Contribution margin = 50 - 20 = EUR 30 per service. Break-even = 2,400 / 30 = 80 services per month.

The most costly error in practice is division by the price. For the repair shop this would give 2,400 / 50 = 48 services, and a shop performing 48 services per month would fall far short of covering its workshop, since the EUR 20 of parts consumed on each job would have been disregarded. The sequence is invariant: subtract first, then divide. Two steps, always in this order: establish what a single sale leaves over, then determine how many such amounts are needed to cover the fixed costs.

# Problem

A food truck pays EUR 3,600 a month for its pitch, licence and insurance. A meal is sold at EUR 12 and costs EUR 4.50 in ingredients and packaging. How many meals must it sell each month to break even? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: Three quantities are relevant, and each serves a distinct function: the amount the truck pays irrespective of sales, the revenue from one meal, and the cost of producing one meal. Identify them before calculating.

Hint 2: Only a portion of the EUR 12 is available for the pitch and the licence, since EUR 4.50 has already been spent on the meal itself. Determine the amount remaining from one sale.

Hint 3: Divide the monthly fixed costs by that remaining amount. The quotient is the number of meals required to cover them exactly.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the fixed costs are EUR 3,600 per month, the price is EUR 12 and the variable cost is EUR 4.50 per meal. Step 2: the contribution margin is 12 - 4.50 = EUR 7.50, the portion of each sale remaining once the meal has been paid for. Step 3: 3,600 / 7.50 = 480 meals per month, equivalent to 16 meals per day over a 30-day month. Division by the price would instead give 3,600 / 12 = 300 meals, and a truck selling 300 meals would remain EUR 1,350 short of its fixed costs.
