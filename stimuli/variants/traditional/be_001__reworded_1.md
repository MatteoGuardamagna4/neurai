# Explanation

Every business eventually faces the same question: how many units do we have to sell before we stop losing money? That point is called break-even, and finding it takes three numbers. The first is fixed costs, the bills that do not move with sales volume, like rent, salaries and insurance. The second is the selling price, the money one unit brings in. The third is the variable cost, the extra cost of making and delivering one more unit, like materials, packaging and labour paid per unit.

Take the price and subtract the variable cost, and you get the contribution margin. Think of it as what is left from each sale once the unit has paid for itself, and that leftover goes towards the fixed costs. As long as the fixed costs are not fully covered the business runs at a loss. After they are covered, each extra unit sold adds one contribution margin of profit.

So the break-even quantity is simply the fixed costs divided by the contribution margin:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. Picture a cafe with EUR 24,000 a month of rent, salaries and insurance. A coffee sells for EUR 5, and the beans, milk and cup for it cost EUR 2. Each cup therefore contributes 5 - 2 = EUR 3, and the cafe breaks even at 24,000 / 3 = 8,000 cups a month. At 7,000 cups it loses EUR 3,000; at 9,000 cups it makes EUR 3,000.

People most often go wrong by dividing the fixed costs by the price instead of by the contribution margin. For the cafe that gives 24,000 / 5 = 4,800 cups, which is far too few: it treats every euro of revenue as if it could pay the rent, when EUR 2 of each cup was already used up making it. Remember the order: subtract, then divide. Get the margin first, and only then divide the fixed costs by it. Those same two steps answer every break-even question, whether it asks for a quantity, a revenue, a price or the units needed for a target profit.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 180,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 75 and has a variable cost of EUR 45. How many units must it sell per year to break even? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: Begin by listing the three figures in the problem: the fixed costs, the price per unit and the variable cost per unit. Work out what job each one does before you calculate anything.

Hint 2: Do not divide by the price. First find the contribution margin, the price minus the variable cost per unit. That margin is how much each sale adds towards the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Now divide the fixed costs by that contribution margin. What you get is the number of units required to break even.

# Worked solution

Step 1: list the inputs. The fixed costs are EUR 180,000, the price is EUR 75 and the variable cost is EUR 45 per unit. Step 2: find the contribution margin: 75 - 45 = EUR 30 per unit. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by the margin: 180,000 / 30 = 6,000 units. To break even the company has to sell 6,000 units a year. Dividing by the price would give 180,000 / 75 = 2,400 units, which is much too low because it leaves out the EUR 45 it costs to make every unit.
