# Explanation

The margin of safety is about risk, not profit: it tells you how far sales can drop from the level a company is planning for before it stops covering its costs. A company that budgets sales well above break-even can survive a bad quarter; one that budgets only just above it cannot. Lenders and boards treat the figure as a measure of resilience, because with a thin margin of safety a lost customer, a price cut or a slow month turns a planned profit into a loss. You need two figures: budgeted sales, and the break-even sales the company has already worked out.

The gap between them is the cushion, measured in euros. That gap says much more as a percentage, since EUR 300,000 of headroom means one thing for a small firm and quite another for a large one. The percentage is taken against budgeted sales, the level the company is planning for, so the result reads as the share of the plan that could be lost before trouble begins.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A publisher plans sales of EUR 250,000 for the year and works out that it breaks even at EUR 200,000 of sales. The cushion is 250,000 - 200,000 = EUR 50,000. As a share of budgeted sales that is 50,000 / 250,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%.

The usual slip is dividing the cushion by break-even sales instead. For the publisher that gives 50,000 / 200,000 = 25%, which makes things look better and, worse, answers another question: it shows how far above break-even the plan sits compared with break-even, not what share of the plan is at risk. Before you divide, ask what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is almost always the sales the company plans to make. The rule: cushion on top, plan below. Divide the same cushion by the selling price and you get the margin of safety in units instead of euros.

# Problem

A manufacturer budgets sales of EUR 800,000 for next year and breaks even at EUR 500,000 of sales. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: The margin of safety measures how far sales can fall before the company starts losing money. Begin by working out that distance in euros.

Hint 2: The question wants a percentage of budgeted sales, so budgeted sales is the base of the fraction, not the break-even figure.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion you found by the budgeted sales figure, then read the result as a percentage.

# Worked solution

Step 1: find the cushion in euros: 800,000 - 500,000 = EUR 300,000 of sales can be lost before the company hits break-even. Step 2: divide by the base the question names, budgeted sales: 300,000 / 800,000 = 0.375, a margin of safety of 37.5%. Dividing by break-even sales would give 300,000 / 500,000 = 60%, which makes things look better: it compares the cushion with the sales the company must make, not with the sales it plans to make.
