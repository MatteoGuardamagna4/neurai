# Explanation

Markup and margin describe the same difference between the cost of a product and its selling price, but they measure that difference against different bases, and confusing them is among the most costly errors in retail pricing. A markup expresses the difference as a percentage of the cost. A gross margin expresses the same difference as a percentage of the selling price. Since the price is always the larger base, the margin percentage is always the smaller figure for any given product.

The practical implication is that a target margin cannot be achieved by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer requires two fifths of the selling price to be gross profit, the cost must constitute the remaining three fifths of the price, so the price equals the cost divided by 0.6 rather than the cost multiplied by 1.4. Expressing the relationship as an equation, cost equals price multiplied by one minus the margin, makes the rearrangement evident.

The rule is a division rather than an addition:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A shop purchases a lamp for EUR 30 and seeks a gross margin of 25% of the selling price. If the margin is one quarter of the price, the cost is the remaining three quarters: price = 30 / 0.75 = EUR 40. Verification: the margin is 40 - 30 = EUR 10, one quarter of 40. Adding 25% to the cost would have yielded EUR 37.50, a margin of only 20%.

The characteristic error is to multiply the cost by one plus the target margin. A lamp priced in this manner sells for EUR 37.50 in the belief that a quarter of the price is gross profit, whereas the profit of EUR 7.50 is only one fifth of it. The discrepancy increases with the target: a margin of half the price requires the cost to be doubled, while adding half to the cost yields a margin of only one third. The two measures are related by margin = markup / (1 + markup). The essential question is always: a percentage of what? A margin is a share of the price, requiring division by one minus it; a markup is a share of the cost, requiring multiplication.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: A gross margin is always measured against the selling price, whereas a markup is measured against the cost. The question specifies a margin, so determine what share of the price the cost must represent.

Hint 2: If the margin accounts for 40% of the price, the cost represents the remaining 60%. Express this as an equation: cost equals price multiplied by 0.6.

Hint 3: Rearrange the equation: divide the cost by one minus the target margin to obtain the price.

# Worked solution

Step 1: interpret the percentage correctly. The margin is measured on the selling price, so the cost represents the remainder: 1 - 0.4 = 0.6 of the price. Step 2: state the relationship: 51 = price x 0.6. Step 3: divide: 51 / 0.6 = EUR 85.00. Verification: the gross profit is 85 - 51 = EUR 34, and 34 / 85 is 40% of the price, as required. Adding 40% to the cost would instead give EUR 71.40, where the gross profit of EUR 20.40 is only 28.6% of the price, since the percentage was applied to the smaller base.
