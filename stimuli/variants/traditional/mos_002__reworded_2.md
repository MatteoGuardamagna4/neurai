# Explanation

Two businesses may plan an identical profit and nevertheless face entirely different risks, and the margin of safety is the measure that distinguishes them. It indicates how far sales may fall below the planned level before the business ceases to cover its costs. A hotel planning well above its break-even point can withstand a poor season; one planning only slightly above it cannot, even if both anticipate a profitable year.

Two figures are required, and their order is significant. The cushion is budgeted sales less break-even sales, expressed in euros. Converting that cushion into a percentage renders it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base of the percentage is budgeted sales, since the question being addressed is what share of the plan could be lost.

Formally:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and determines that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Relative to budgeted revenue this is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The characteristic error is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. For the ski school this gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering figure that answers a question no one posed: it indicates how far the plan lies above break-even relative to break-even, rather than what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, state explicitly what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is almost always the level the business is planning for. Cushion as numerator, plan as denominator. Dividing the same cushion by the average selling price instead yields the margin of safety in units.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: Begin with the distance in euros between the revenue the hotel plans to earn and the revenue it must earn to cover its costs. That distance is the cushion.

Hint 2: A percentage is always a percentage of something. Here the question specifies budgeted revenue, so that forms the denominator of the fraction.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion by budgeted revenue and interpret the result as a percentage of the plan.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the cushion is 1,000,000 - 800,000 = EUR 200,000 of revenue that could be lost before the hotel ceases to cover its costs. Step 2: divide by the base specified in the question, budgeted revenue: 200,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%. One booking season in five could be lost before difficulties arise. Division by break-even revenue would instead report 25%, which appears safer and addresses a different question: it compares the cushion with the revenue the hotel must earn rather than with the revenue it plans to earn.
