# Explanation

Where a business requires its break-even point to be expressed in euros of revenue rather than in units, the contribution margin ratio is the appropriate instrument. The ratio addresses a single question: of each euro paid by a customer, what amount remains once the cost of serving that customer has been deducted? It is defined as the contribution margin divided by the price, and it is invariably a proportion of the price rather than of the cost.

The distinction is significant because each sale comprises two complementary proportions. One is expended in delivering the sale; the other remains for the fixed costs and subsequently for profit. The two always sum to one, so stating both explicitly before dividing is a reliable safeguard against selecting the wrong one. A ratio of 0.4 signifies that 40 cents of each euro remains and 60 cents is consumed.

Once the ratio has been determined, the revenue required to cover the fixed costs follows directly:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A translation agency incurs fixed costs of EUR 60,000 per year. A typical assignment is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. Contribution margin = 500 - 200 = EUR 300, and the ratio is therefore 300 / 500 = 0.6. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.6 = EUR 100,000 of billings per year.

The characteristic error is to divide by the consumed proportion rather than the remaining one. At the translation agency, freelance fees absorb 200 / 500 = 0.4 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.4 yields EUR 150,000, overstating the agency's requirement by one half. The correct ratio produces a substantially lower figure, so the error is never negligible. The sequence is: margin, then ratio, then division. The same ratio also converts any profit target into the additional revenue required to fund it.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: The question requires an amount of revenue, not a number of contracts, so the divisor must be a proportion of each euro rather than an amount in euros.

Hint 2: Calculate the margin on one contract first, then express it as a fraction of the billed price. That fraction is the proportion of every euro of revenue remaining to meet the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the annual fixed costs by that fraction. The result is the level of billings at which the company covers all its costs and no more.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin on one contract is 800 - 500 = EUR 300. Step 2: as a proportion of the price this is 300 / 800 = 0.375, so 37.5 cents of every euro billed remains after labour and materials have been paid. Step 3: 144,000 / 0.375 = EUR 384,000 of billings per year. Division by the variable cost ratio of 0.625 would instead give EUR 230,400, a measure of how much revenue the company spends rather than how much it retains.
