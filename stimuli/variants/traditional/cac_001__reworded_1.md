# Explanation

A company pays to win a customer before that customer has paid it anything, so a growing company is always funding a gap. The payback period on customer acquisition cost tells you how long that gap lasts: how many months a customer needs to pay back what it cost to win them. When payback is short, the sales and marketing money returns fast and can be reinvested; when it is long, growth has to be financed from somewhere else while you wait.

You need two figures. The first is the acquisition cost per customer: all the sales and marketing spend of a period divided by the customers it brought in, including not just the advertising bills but also the salaries, tools and commissions behind them. The second is the monthly repayment, which is the gross margin a customer produces, since the slice of the subscription that pays for hosting, support and payment processing is never free to repay anything.

Divide the first by the second:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. In one quarter a company spends EUR 48,000 on sales and marketing and wins 200 new customers. Each one pays EUR 80 a month, at a gross margin of 50%. Acquisition cost per customer = 48,000 / 200 = EUR 240. Monthly gross margin per customer = 80 x 0.5 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The usual slip is dividing the acquisition cost by the price the customer pays. Here that gives 240 / 80 = 3.0 months, which makes the channel look like it pays back twice as fast as it does, and the error grows for businesses with thin gross margins, the very ones that can least afford it. Keep the sister measure in mind too: payback tells you how fast the money returns, lifetime value tells you how much returns overall. Cost per customer goes on top, monthly gross margin goes underneath. Both figures must refer to the same customers and the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 81,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 300 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 100 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 60%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: There are two steps here. First work out what winning one customer costs, then work out what one customer pays back each month.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost per customer is the total sales and marketing spend divided by the number of customers that spend won.

Hint 3: What the customer repays each month is the gross margin they bring in, not the price they pay: multiply monthly revenue by the margin, then divide the acquisition cost by the result.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 81,000 / 300 = EUR 270. Step 2: monthly gross margin per customer = 100 x 0.6 = EUR 60, the amount that is really there to repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 270 / 60 = 4.5 months. Dividing by the EUR 100 of revenue would give 2.7 months, treating the 40 cents of every euro that goes on serving the customer as though it came back to the company.
