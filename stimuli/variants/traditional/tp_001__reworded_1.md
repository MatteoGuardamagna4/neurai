# Explanation

Target-profit questions ask how much a business has to sell to reach a stated profit, and the arithmetic is simply the break-even calculation with one extra term. Every unit sold contributes its margin, the price minus the variable cost, first towards the fixed costs and then towards profit, so the quantity needed is the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Tax changes what that required profit is. A company does not keep the profit it makes; it keeps what is left once the tax authority has taken its cut. If the tax rate is a quarter, then out of every euro of profit before tax the owners keep 75 cents, so a target given after tax is smaller than the profit the business really has to earn. To convert one into the other, divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate.

So the volume needed is:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop's fixed costs are EUR 120,000 a year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner wants EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit needed = 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = EUR 60,000. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 60,000) / 12 = 15,000 units.

The usual slip is adding the after-tax target straight onto the fixed costs. For the workshop that gives (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units, and at that volume the pre-tax profit is exactly EUR 45,000, of which the tax authority takes a quarter, leaving the owner EUR 33,750 and well short of the goal. Gross the profit up first, and from then on treat it as if it were one more fixed cost. Two steps, in this order: gross up the target, then divide by the contribution margin. Skipping the first step always understates how much the company has to sell.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: Sales volume depends on the profit the company must earn before tax, because that is the profit tax is charged on. The figure you were given is after tax, so it is not yet the number you need.

Hint 2: Convert the target: if tax takes one fifth of the pre-tax profit, the after-tax figure is only four fifths of it. Divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate to get back the pre-tax profit.

Hint 3: Add that pre-tax profit to the fixed costs and divide the total by the contribution margin per unit, the price minus the variable cost.

# Worked solution

Step 1: gross up the target. Tax takes 20%, so after-tax profit is 80% of pre-tax profit: 128,000 / 0.8 = EUR 160,000 has to be earned before tax. Step 2: find the contribution margin: 90 - 50 = EUR 40 per unit. Step 3: contribution has to cover the fixed costs and the pre-tax profit: (300,000 + 160,000) / 40 = 11,500 units. Adding the after-tax target directly would give (300,000 + 128,000) / 40 = 10,700 units, which leaves the company short once the tax bill comes in.
