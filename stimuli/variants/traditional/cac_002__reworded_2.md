# Explanation

Growth is financed before it is earned: the expenditure required to acquire a customer is incurred months before that customer has repaid it. The payback period on customer acquisition cost is the number of months a customer requires to return the amount spent on acquiring them. A short payback allows the marketing budget to be recovered and redeployed quickly; a long payback requires growth to be funded from other sources in the interim.

The numerator comprises the entire cost of acquisition, not merely advertising invoices: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions are all included, divided by the customers that expenditure actually generated. The denominator is the gross margin generated by a customer each month, since the portion of the subscription that funds hosting, delivery or support never becomes available for repayment.

The payback is the quotient of the two:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing in a quarter and acquires 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 per month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly margin = 100 x 0.4 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The characteristic error is division by the price paid by the customer. For the fitness app this reports 240 / 100 = 2.4 months rather than 6.0, suggesting that the channel is two and a half times faster than it is, and the distortion is greatest for businesses with low margins, which are precisely those least able to afford a misjudgement. The complementary measure should remain in view: payback indicates how quickly expenditure is recovered, lifetime value how much is recovered in total. The cost per customer forms the numerator and the monthly gross margin the denominator, both describing the same customers over the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: The problem has two components: the cost of acquiring one new customer, and the amount one customer returns each month. Determine each before dividing.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost equals the quarter's total sales and marketing expenditure divided by the customers that expenditure generated.

Hint 3: The monthly repayment is the gross margin, not the price: half of every EUR 150 is spent on serving the customer and never becomes available.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 144,000 / 400 = EUR 360. Step 2: the monthly gross margin per customer is 150 x 0.5 = EUR 75, the amount genuinely available to repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 360 / 75 = 4.8 months. Division by the EUR 150 of revenue would instead report 2.4 months, treating the half of every euro spent on serving the customer as though it were returned to the company.
