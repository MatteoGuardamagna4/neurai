# Explanation

If a business wants its break-even point in euros of revenue rather than in units, the contribution margin ratio is what gets it there. The ratio answers one plain question: out of every euro a customer pays, how much is left once the cost of serving that customer has come out? It equals the contribution margin divided by the price, and it is always a share of the price, not of the cost.

That difference matters because every sale splits into two shares that complement each other. One share is used up delivering the sale; the other is left over for the fixed costs and, after them, for profit. They always sum to one, so saying both out loud before you divide is a dependable way to avoid grabbing the wrong one. A ratio of 0.4 means 40 cents of each euro is left and 60 cents is used up.

With the ratio in hand, the revenue that covers the fixed costs follows straight away:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A translation agency's fixed costs are EUR 60,000 a year. A typical job is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. Contribution margin = 500 - 200 = EUR 300, so the ratio is 300 / 500 = 0.6. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.6 = EUR 100,000 of billings a year.

The slip is dividing by the used-up share instead of the share that is left. At the translation agency, freelance fees take 200 / 500 = 0.4 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.4 gives EUR 150,000, overstating the agency's needs by half. The right ratio gives a much lower, correct figure, so this error is never a small one. Margin first, then the ratio, then the division. The same ratio also turns any profit target into the extra revenue needed to pay for it.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: The question wants an amount of revenue, not a number of contracts, so your divisor has to be a share of each euro, not an amount in euros.

Hint 2: First find the margin on one contract, then write it as a fraction of the billed price. That fraction is the share of every euro of revenue left over for the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the yearly fixed costs by that fraction. The answer is the level of billings at which the company covers everything and nothing more.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin on one contract is 800 - 500 = EUR 300. Step 2: as a share of the price that is 300 / 800 = 0.375, so 37.5 cents of every euro billed remains once labour and materials are paid. Step 3: 144,000 / 0.375 = EUR 384,000 of billings a year. Dividing by the variable cost ratio of 0.625 would give EUR 230,400, which measures how much revenue the company spends, not how much it keeps.
