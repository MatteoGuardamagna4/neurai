# Explanation

When a business sells a cheap item next to an expensive one, its break-even point depends as much on the mix between them as on either margin. With two products the fixed costs are shared but the margins are different, so there is no one number to divide by. The answer is to build the margin of an average sale, and an average sale is defined by the proportions in which the products really sell, not by the products themselves.

Weighting takes care of that. If two loaves go out for every cake, then in any group of three items two earn the loaf margin and one earns the cake margin, so the average item contributes two thirds of the first plus one third of the second. Divide the fixed costs by that weighted figure and you get a total item count, which splits back into loaves and cakes in the same two-to-one proportion.

For a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A kiosk earns a margin of EUR 1 on a drink and EUR 4 on a sandwich, and sells three drinks for every sandwich. Its fixed costs are EUR 14,000 a month. Weighted average margin = (3 x 1 + 1 x 4) / 4 = 7 / 4 = EUR 1.75 per item. Break-even = 14,000 / 1.75 = 8,000 items a month.

The slip is using a plain average. For the kiosk, averaging EUR 1 and EUR 4 gives EUR 2.50 instead of the correct EUR 1.75, and break-even comes out at 5,600 items rather than 8,000: the kiosk would believe it was safe roughly 2,400 items too early. A plain average always assumes an equal mix, which is exactly what these questions are designed to test. Margins first, then weights, then divide. The mix is an input to the calculation, not a detail to tidy away.

# Problem

A bakery sells two products. A loaf sells for EUR 8 with a variable cost of EUR 5; a cake sells for EUR 26 with a variable cost of EUR 17. The bakery sells two loaves for every cake, and its fixed costs are EUR 90,000 a year. How many items in total must it sell to break even? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: There is no one margin here, so build one that matches what the bakery really sells: two loaves for every cake.

Hint 2: The margins are EUR 3 on a loaf and EUR 9 on a cake. In each group of three items sold, two are loaves and one is a cake, so weight them that way.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the weighted average margin to get the total number of items.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 8 - 5 = EUR 3 on a loaf and 26 - 17 = EUR 9 on a cake. Step 2: weight by the mix. In every three items, two are loaves and one is a cake, so the average item contributes (2 x 3 + 1 x 9) / 3 = 15 / 3 = EUR 5. Step 3: divide: 90,000 / 5 = 18,000 items a year, or 12,000 loaves and 6,000 cakes. A plain average of EUR 3 and EUR 9 would give EUR 6 and 15,000 items, understating what the bakery must sell because it assumes as many cakes as loaves.
