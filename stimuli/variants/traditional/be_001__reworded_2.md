# Explanation

Break-even analysis determines the sales volume at which a business ceases to make a loss. The calculation requires three inputs. Fixed costs are those expenses that do not vary with the volume of sales, for example rent, salaries and insurance. The selling price is the revenue generated by a single unit. The variable cost is the additional cost of producing and delivering one further unit, for example materials, packaging and labour paid per unit.

The contribution margin is defined as the selling price less the variable cost. It represents the amount that each sale leaves available, once the unit itself has been paid for, to meet the fixed costs. For as long as the fixed costs remain only partly covered, the business operates at a loss; beyond that point, each additional unit adds one contribution margin to profit.

It follows that the break-even quantity equals the fixed costs divided by the contribution margin:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A cafe incurs EUR 24,000 per month in rent, salaries and insurance. Coffee sells at EUR 5 per cup, and the beans, milk and cup together cost EUR 2. The contribution margin is 5 - 2 = EUR 3 per cup, and the cafe accordingly breaks even at 24,000 / 3 = 8,000 cups per month. Sales of 7,000 cups produce a loss of EUR 3,000, whereas sales of 9,000 cups produce a profit of EUR 3,000.

The error encountered most frequently is to divide the fixed costs by the price rather than by the contribution margin. Applied to the cafe, this yields 24,000 / 5 = 4,800 cups, a considerable underestimate, since it assumes that all revenue is available to cover the rent although EUR 2 of each cup has already been consumed in production. The correct sequence is to subtract first and divide second: determine the margin, then divide the fixed costs by it. These two steps resolve every break-even question, whether the unknown is a quantity, a revenue, a price or the volume required for a target profit.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 180,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 75 and has a variable cost of EUR 45. How many units must it sell per year to break even? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: Record the three quantities given in the problem: the fixed costs, the price per unit and the variable cost per unit. Establish the role of each before performing any calculation.

Hint 2: The price is not the correct divisor. Calculate the contribution margin first, that is, the price less the variable cost per unit. This margin is the contribution of each sale towards the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the contribution margin obtained. The quotient is the number of units required to break even.

# Worked solution

Step 1: identify the inputs. The fixed costs amount to EUR 180,000, the price to EUR 75 and the variable cost to EUR 45 per unit. Step 2: calculate the contribution margin: 75 - 45 = EUR 30 per unit. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by the margin: 180,000 / 30 = 6,000 units. The company must therefore sell 6,000 units per year to break even. Division by the price would instead yield 180,000 / 75 = 2,400 units, a substantial underestimate, because it disregards the EUR 45 required to produce each unit.
