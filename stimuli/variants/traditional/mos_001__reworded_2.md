# Explanation

The margin of safety is a measure of risk rather than of profit: it indicates how far sales may fall below the planned level before a company ceases to cover its costs. A company that budgets sales well above its break-even point can withstand an adverse quarter; one that budgets only marginally above it cannot. Lenders and boards regard the figure as an indicator of resilience, since a narrow margin of safety means that the loss of a customer, a price reduction or a weak month converts a planned profit into a loss. Two figures are required: the budgeted sales and the break-even sales already calculated by the company.

The difference between them is the cushion, expressed in euros. The difference is considerably more informative as a percentage, since EUR 300,000 of headroom signifies one thing for a small firm and another for a large one. The percentage is calculated against budgeted sales, the level the company is planning for, so the result represents the share of the plan that could be lost before difficulties arise.

Formally:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A publisher budgets sales of EUR 250,000 for the year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 200,000 of sales. The cushion is 250,000 - 200,000 = EUR 50,000. As a proportion of budgeted sales this is 50,000 / 250,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%.

The characteristic error is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. For the publisher this gives 50,000 / 200,000 = 25%, which flatters the position and, more seriously, addresses a different question: it indicates how far above break-even the plan lies relative to break-even, rather than what share of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, establish what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is almost invariably the sales the company plans to achieve. The rule is: cushion as numerator, plan as denominator. Dividing the same cushion by the selling price yields the margin of safety in units rather than in euros.

# Problem

A manufacturer budgets sales of EUR 800,000 for next year and breaks even at EUR 500,000 of sales. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: The margin of safety measures how far sales can fall before the company begins to incur a loss. Begin by calculating that distance in euros.

Hint 2: The question requires a percentage of budgeted sales, so budgeted sales form the base of the fraction, not the break-even figure.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion calculated by the budgeted sales figure, then express the result as a percentage.

# Worked solution

Step 1: calculate the cushion in euros: 800,000 - 500,000 = EUR 300,000 of sales may be lost before the company reaches break-even. Step 2: divide by the base specified in the question, budgeted sales: 300,000 / 800,000 = 0.375, a margin of safety of 37.5%. Division by break-even sales would instead give 300,000 / 500,000 = 60%, which flatters the position, since the cushion is compared with the sales the company must achieve rather than with those it plans to achieve.
