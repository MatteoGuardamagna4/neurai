# Explanation

The acquisition of a customer entails expenditure before that customer has made any payment, so a growing company continually finances a gap. The payback period on customer acquisition cost measures the duration of that gap: the number of months required for a customer to repay the amount spent to acquire them. A short payback indicates that sales and marketing expenditure is recovered rapidly and can be redeployed; a long payback indicates that growth must meanwhile be funded from other sources.

Two figures are required. The acquisition cost per customer is the total sales and marketing expenditure of a period divided by the number of customers it generated, including not only advertising invoices but also the associated salaries, tools and commissions. The monthly repayment is the gross margin generated by a customer, since the portion of the subscription that funds hosting, support and payment processing is never available for repayment.

The payback is the quotient of the two:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A company spends EUR 48,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 200 new customers. Each pays EUR 80 per month, and the gross margin is 50%. Acquisition cost per customer = 48,000 / 200 = EUR 240. Monthly gross margin per customer = 80 x 0.5 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The characteristic error is to divide the acquisition cost by the price paid by the customer. In this example that yields 240 / 80 = 3.0 months, overstating the speed of repayment by a factor of two, and the distortion increases for businesses with low gross margins, which are precisely those least able to absorb it. The complementary measure should also be considered: payback indicates how quickly the expenditure is recovered, lifetime value how much is recovered in total. The cost per customer forms the numerator and the monthly gross margin the denominator, both relating to the same customers and the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 81,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 300 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 100 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 60%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: The calculation proceeds in two steps. Determine first the cost of acquiring one customer, then the amount one customer returns per month.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost per customer equals the total sales and marketing expenditure divided by the number of customers it generated.

Hint 3: The monthly repayment is the gross margin generated by the customer, not the price paid: multiply the monthly revenue by the margin, then divide the acquisition cost by that figure.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 81,000 / 300 = EUR 270. Step 2: monthly gross margin per customer = 100 x 0.6 = EUR 60, the amount genuinely available to repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 270 / 60 = 4.5 months. Division by the EUR 100 of revenue would instead yield 2.7 months, treating the 40 cents of every euro spent on serving the customer as though it were returned to the company.
