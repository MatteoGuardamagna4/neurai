# Explanation

Where a business sells an inexpensive item alongside an expensive one, its break-even point depends as much on the mix between them as on either margin. With two products the fixed costs are shared but the margins differ, so no single divisor is available. The remedy is to construct the margin of an average sale, and an average sale is defined by the proportions in which the products are actually sold rather than by the products themselves.

Weighting accomplishes this. If two loaves are sold for every cake, then in any group of three items two carry the loaf margin and one the cake margin, so the average item contributes two thirds of the former plus one third of the latter. Dividing the fixed costs by this weighted figure yields a total item count, which may be allocated back to loaves and cakes in the same two-to-one proportion.

For a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A kiosk earns a margin of EUR 1 on each drink and EUR 4 on each sandwich, and sells three drinks for every sandwich. Its fixed costs are EUR 14,000 per month. Weighted average margin = (3 x 1 + 1 x 4) / 4 = 7 / 4 = EUR 1.75 per item. Break-even = 14,000 / 1.75 = 8,000 items per month.

The characteristic error is the unweighted average. For the kiosk, averaging EUR 1 and EUR 4 gives EUR 2.50 rather than the correct EUR 1.75, and the break-even figure becomes 5,600 items instead of 8,000: the kiosk would consider itself secure approximately 2,400 items before it actually was. The unweighted average invariably assumes an equal mix, which is precisely the assumption such questions are designed to test. The sequence is: margins, then weights, then division. The mix is an input to the calculation, not a detail to be disregarded.

# Problem

A bakery sells two products. A loaf sells for EUR 8 with a variable cost of EUR 5; a cake sells for EUR 26 with a variable cost of EUR 17. The bakery sells two loaves for every cake, and its fixed costs are EUR 90,000 a year. How many items in total must it sell to break even? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: No single margin applies here, so construct one that reflects what the bakery actually sells: two loaves for every cake.

Hint 2: The margins are EUR 3 on a loaf and EUR 9 on a cake. In every group of three items sold, two are loaves and one is a cake, so weight them accordingly.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the weighted average margin to obtain the total number of items.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 8 - 5 = EUR 3 on a loaf and 26 - 17 = EUR 9 on a cake. Step 2: weight by the mix. In every three items, two are loaves and one is a cake, so the average item contributes (2 x 3 + 1 x 9) / 3 = 15 / 3 = EUR 5. Step 3: divide: 90,000 / 5 = 18,000 items per year, comprising 12,000 loaves and 6,000 cakes. An unweighted average of EUR 3 and EUR 9 would give EUR 6 and 15,000 items, understating the bakery's requirement since it assumes as many cakes as loaves.
