# Explanation

Hardly any business sells just one thing, and as soon as there are two products the break-even formula needs a margin that does not exist yet. Every product has its own contribution margin, while the fixed costs are shared by all of them, so there is no single number to divide by. The fix is to build one: an average margin for a typical sale, which depends not only on the two margins but on how many of each the business really sells.

That is why the average has to be weighted. If three cheap items go out for every expensive one, the cheap item's margin describes three quarters of what crosses the counter and should count three times as much. Weighting by the sales mix gives the margin of an average unit; divide the fixed costs by it and you get the total number of units, which you can then split back into the separate products in the same proportion.

In symbols, for a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A cafe earns a margin of EUR 2 on a coffee and EUR 5 on a cake, and sells four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 a month. Weighted average margin = (4 x 2 + 1 x 5) / 5 = 13 / 5 = EUR 2.60 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 2.60 = 10,000 items a month, split into 8,000 coffees and 2,000 cakes.

The slip is averaging the two margins without weights. For the cafe that gives (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 rather than EUR 2.60, understating the items needed by a quarter, because it silently assumes the cafe sells as many cakes as coffees when it actually sells four times more coffee. A plain average is right only in the single case where the products sell in equal numbers. Weight the margins by the mix, divide the fixed costs by the result, then split the total back in the same proportion.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: With two products there is no one contribution margin, so your first task is to build one that reflects the mix the shop really sells.

Hint 2: Find the margin on each product, then weight them: out of every four units sold, three are A and one is B, so A's margin should count three times as much.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by that weighted average margin to get the total units, then split them into A and B in the same three-to-one proportion.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 30 - 10 = EUR 20 on A and 50 - 40 = EUR 10 on B. Step 2: weight them by the mix. In each batch of four units, three are A and one is B, so the average unit contributes (3 x 20 + 1 x 10) / 4 = 70 / 4 = EUR 17.50. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that figure: 105,000 / 17.50 = 6,000 units in total, which is 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B. A plain average of the two margins would give EUR 15 and 7,000 units, overstating the need because it pretends the shop sells as many of the thin-margin product as of the fat one.
