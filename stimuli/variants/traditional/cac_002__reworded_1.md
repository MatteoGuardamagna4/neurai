# Explanation

Growth gets paid for before it gets earned: the money spent winning a customer leaves the company months before that customer has paid it back. The payback period on customer acquisition cost counts the months a customer needs to return what it cost to win them. A short payback lets the marketing budget come back fast and be spent again; a long one means someone else has to fund the growth while everybody waits.

On top goes the full cost of acquisition, not only the advertising bills: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions all count, divided by the customers that spending really brought in. Underneath goes the gross margin a customer produces each month, because the slice of the subscription that pays for hosting, delivery or support is never free to repay anything.

Divide one by the other:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. In one quarter a fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing and gains 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 a month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly margin = 100 x 0.4 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The slip is dividing by the price the customer pays. For the fitness app that shows 240 / 100 = 2.4 months instead of 6.0, so the channel looks two and a half times faster than it really is, and the error is largest for thin-margin businesses, exactly the ones that can least afford to get it wrong. Keep the sister measure in sight: payback tells you how quickly the money comes back, lifetime value tells you how much comes back altogether. Cost per customer above the line, monthly gross margin below it, and both figures must describe the same customers over the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: There are two halves here: what it costs to win one new customer, and what one customer pays back each month. Find each one before you divide.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost is the quarter's total sales and marketing spend divided by the customers that spend won.

Hint 3: What pays that cost back each month is the gross margin, not the price: half of every EUR 150 goes on serving the customer and is never available.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 144,000 / 400 = EUR 360. Step 2: the monthly gross margin per customer is 150 x 0.5 = EUR 75, the amount that can really repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 360 / 75 = 4.8 months. Dividing by the EUR 150 of revenue would show 2.4 months, counting the half of every euro spent on serving the customer as if it came back to the company.
