# Explanation

Markup and margin both describe the gap between what a product costs and what it sells for, but they measure that gap against different bases, and mixing them up is one of the costliest mistakes in retail pricing. A markup is the gap as a percentage of the cost. A gross margin is the same gap as a percentage of the selling price. Since the price is always the bigger base, the margin percentage is always the smaller of the two for the same product.

What follows in practice is that you cannot hit a target margin by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer wants two fifths of the selling price to be gross profit, the cost must be the remaining three fifths of the price, so the price is the cost divided by 0.6, not the cost times 1.4. Writing it as an equation, cost equals price times one minus the margin, makes the rearranging obvious.

The rule is a division, not an addition:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A shop pays EUR 30 for a lamp and wants a gross margin of 25% of the selling price. If the margin is a quarter of the price, the cost is the other three quarters: price = 30 / 0.75 = EUR 40. Check: the margin is 40 - 30 = EUR 10, a quarter of 40. Adding 25% to the cost would have given EUR 37.50, which is a margin of just 20%.

The slip is multiplying the cost by one plus the target margin. Price the lamp that way and you charge EUR 37.50, thinking a quarter of the price is gross profit, when the EUR 7.50 of profit is only a fifth of it. The gap widens as the target rises: a margin of half the price needs the cost doubled, while adding half to the cost gives a margin of only a third. The two measures are linked by margin = markup / (1 + markup). Always ask: a percentage of what? A margin is a share of the price, so divide by one minus it; a markup is a share of the cost, so multiply.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: A gross margin is always measured against the selling price, a markup against the cost. The question names a margin, so work out what share of the price the cost must be.

Hint 2: If the margin takes 40% of the price, the cost is the other 60% of it. Write that as an equation: cost equals price times 0.6.

Hint 3: Rearrange the equation: divide the cost by one minus the target margin to get the price back.

# Worked solution

Step 1: read the percentage the right way. The margin is measured on the selling price, so the cost is what remains: 1 - 0.4 = 0.6 of the price. Step 2: write the relationship: 51 = price x 0.6. Step 3: divide: 51 / 0.6 = EUR 85.00. Check: gross profit is 85 - 51 = EUR 34, and 34 / 85 is 40% of the price, as asked. Adding 40% to the cost would give EUR 71.40, where the EUR 20.40 of gross profit is only 28.6% of the price, because the percentage went onto the smaller base.
