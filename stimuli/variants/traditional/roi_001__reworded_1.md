# Explanation

Return on investment is the most quoted number in business and the one most often worked out wrongly, nearly always in the same direction. It answers a single question: for every euro put in, how much came back on top of that euro? The words "on top of" are the whole difficulty, because the money a project returns usually includes the original stake, and the stake was never profit.

So the calculation has two steps that must stay separate. First pull the gain apart from the stake: if EUR 400,000 goes in and EUR 500,000 comes out, the gain is EUR 100,000 and the rest is simply the company's own money coming back. Then divide that gain by what was put at risk, because the bottom of any return is the amount committed, not the amount received.

In symbols:

ROI = (Total returned - Amount invested) / Amount invested

Worked example. A shop spends EUR 50,000 on a refit, and the refit brings in EUR 65,000 of extra gross profit over its life. The gain is 65,000 - 50,000 = EUR 15,000. ROI = 15,000 / 50,000 = 0.3, a return of 30% on the money invested.

The slip is dividing the total returned by the amount invested and calling that a return. That is the money multiple: it counts the stake as profit and turns a decent project into a spectacular one on paper. A quieter version divides the gain by the total received instead of by the investment, which understates the return. A handy check: a project that only breaks even must show a return of zero, not one. Gain on top, money committed underneath. A project that just gives back what it used up has earned a return of nothing at all.

# Problem

A company invests EUR 400,000 in a project and receives EUR 500,000 back in total over the project's life. What is the return on investment? Try it on your own. If you get it wrong, you will get up to three hints, one at a time, and a new attempt after each.

# Hints

Hint 1: The EUR 500,000 includes the EUR 400,000 the company put in. Separate the money returned from the money gained before you divide anything.

Hint 2: Return on investment is a return on the amount at risk, so the bottom of the fraction is what was invested, never what came back.

Hint 3: Divide the gain by the investment and read the answer as a percentage of the money committed.

# Worked solution

Step 1: split the stake from the gain. Of the EUR 500,000 received, EUR 400,000 is the company's own money coming home, so the gain is 500,000 - 400,000 = EUR 100,000. Step 2: divide the gain by what was invested: 100,000 / 400,000 = 0.25, a return of 25%. Reporting 500,000 / 400,000 = 125% would make the same project look five times better than it is, because it counts the returned stake as profit. Dividing by the EUR 500,000 received would give 20%, which measures the gain against the proceeds rather than against the risk taken.
