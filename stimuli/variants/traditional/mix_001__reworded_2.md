# Explanation

Very few businesses sell a single product, and once there are two, the break-even formula requires a margin that does not yet exist. Each product has its own contribution margin, whereas the fixed costs are shared among all of them, so no single figure is available as a divisor. The solution is to construct one: an average margin for a representative sale, which depends not only on the two margins but also on the quantities of each actually sold.

This is what makes the average a weighted one. If three inexpensive items are sold for every expensive one, the margin of the inexpensive item describes three quarters of the goods sold and should carry three times the weight. Weighting by the sales mix yields the margin of an average unit, and dividing the fixed costs by it yields the total number of units, which may then be allocated back to the individual products in the same proportion.

Formally, for a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A cafe earns a margin of EUR 2 on each coffee and EUR 5 on each cake, and sells four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 per month. Weighted average margin = (4 x 2 + 1 x 5) / 5 = 13 / 5 = EUR 2.60 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 2.60 = 10,000 items per month, comprising 8,000 coffees and 2,000 cakes.

The characteristic error is to average the two margins without weights. For the cafe this gives (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 instead of EUR 2.60, understating the required volume by a quarter, since it implicitly assumes that the cafe sells as many cakes as coffees, whereas it in fact sells four times as much coffee. The unweighted average is correct only where the products sell in equal numbers. The margins should be weighted by the mix, the fixed costs divided by the result, and the total allocated back in the same proportion.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: With two products there is no single contribution margin, so the first task is to construct one that represents the mix the shop actually sells.

Hint 2: Determine the margin on each product, then weight them: of every four units sold, three are A and one is B, so the margin of A should carry three times the weight.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by that weighted average margin to obtain the total number of units, then allocate them to A and B in the same three-to-one proportion.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 30 - 10 = EUR 20 on A and 50 - 40 = EUR 10 on B. Step 2: weight them by the mix. In every batch of four units, three are A and one is B, so the average unit contributes (3 x 20 + 1 x 10) / 4 = 70 / 4 = EUR 17.50. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that figure: 105,000 / 17.50 = 6,000 units in total, comprising 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B. An unweighted average of the two margins would give EUR 15 and 7,000 units, overstating the requirement since it assumes the shop sells as many of the low-margin product as of the high-margin one.
