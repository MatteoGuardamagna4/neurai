# Explanation

A subscription business does not earn its revenue at the first sale, so assessing a customer on the basis of that first payment understates their worth considerably. Lifetime value is the worth of a single customer across the entire relationship, and it is the figure that determines how much may reasonably be spent to acquire them. It converts a marketing budget from an expense into an investment whose return can be verified.

It comprises two elements, each easily misjudged. The first is the amount the company retains each period, which is a margin rather than a price: a subscription of EUR 80 that costs EUR 32 to fulfil leaves EUR 48, and only that EUR 48 is available for acquisition, overheads and profit. The second is the duration of the relationship, which derives from churn: where a constant proportion departs each month, the average life equals one divided by that proportion, so a monthly churn of 5% implies twenty months and 10% implies ten.

In combination:

Lifetime value = Period revenue x Gross margin / Churn rate for that period

Worked example. A magazine charges EUR 15 per month. Printing, postage and editorial absorb 20% of that amount, leaving an 80% gross margin, and 4% of subscribers cancel each month. Monthly margin = 15 x 0.8 = EUR 12. The average subscriber remains 1 / 0.04 = 25 months. Lifetime value = 12 x 25 = EUR 300.

The characteristic error is to base the figure on revenue. For the magazine this gives 15 / 0.04 = EUR 375 rather than EUR 300, and the discrepancy grows as margins narrow: a business retaining only a third of its revenue would overstate each customer threefold. A related error assumes a fixed life of one year, discarding precisely the information conveyed by churn and concealing the benefit of improved retention. The margin per period forms the numerator and the churn per period the denominator, and the periods must correspond: a monthly margin requires a monthly churn rate.

# Problem

A meal-kit company charges EUR 80 per customer per month. Its gross margin is 60% of that revenue, and 8% of its customers cancel each month. What is the lifetime value of a customer, before discounting? Solve it independently. Should your answer be incorrect, up to three hints will follow, one at a time, each followed by a further attempt.

# Hints

Hint 1: Lifetime value comprises two elements: the amount the company retains from a customer each month, and the number of months that customer remains. Determine them separately.

Hint 2: The company does not retain the entire EUR 80. Apply the gross margin first, so that the cost of the ingredients and delivery has already been removed.

Hint 3: A monthly cancellation rate of 8% implies an average life of one divided by 0.08. Multiplying by that life or dividing by the churn rate yields the same result.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the monthly gross margin is 80 x 0.6 = EUR 48, the amount remaining after the boxes have been packed and delivered. Step 2: with 8% cancelling each month, a customer remains 1 / 0.08 = 12.5 months on average. Step 3: multiply: 48 x 12.5 = EUR 600, equivalent to dividing the monthly margin by the churn rate. Using the full EUR 80 of revenue would give EUR 1,000 and would allow the company to spend on acquisition money already committed to ingredients and couriers.
