# Explanation

Let's go through markup and margin together. They both describe the gap between what a product costs and what it sells for, but they measure that gap against different bases, and mixing them up is one of the costliest mistakes in retail pricing. A markup is the gap as a percentage of the cost. A gross margin is the same gap as a percentage of the selling price. Since the price is always the bigger base, the margin percentage is always the smaller of the two for the same product.

What follows in practice is that you cannot hit a target margin by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer wants two fifths of the selling price to be gross profit, the cost must be the remaining three fifths of the price, so the price is the cost divided by 0.6, not the cost times 1.4. Writing it as an equation, cost equals price times one minus the margin, makes the rearranging obvious.

The rule is a division, not an addition:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A shop pays EUR 30 for a lamp and wants a gross margin of 25% of the selling price. If the margin is a quarter of the price, the cost is the other three quarters: price = 30 / 0.75 = EUR 40. Check: the margin is 40 - 30 = EUR 10, a quarter of 40. Adding 25% to the cost would have given EUR 37.50, which is a margin of just 20%.

The slip is multiplying the cost by one plus the target margin. Price the lamp that way and you charge EUR 37.50, thinking a quarter of the price is gross profit, when the EUR 7.50 of profit is only a fifth of it. The gap widens as the target rises: a margin of half the price needs the cost doubled, while adding half to the cost gives a margin of only a third. The two measures are linked by margin = markup / (1 + markup). Keep that in mind as I walk you through the calculation below, because choosing the base is where people slip most.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? There is no need to work it out yourself. If you answer wrongly, I will show you the full calculation and the answer straight away, one step at a time.

# Worked solution

Step 1: read the percentage the right way. The margin is measured on the selling price, so the cost is what remains: 1 - 0.4 = 0.6 of the price. Step 2: write the relationship: 51 = price x 0.6. Step 3: divide: 51 / 0.6 = EUR 85.00. Check: gross profit is 85 - 51 = EUR 34, and 34 / 85 is 40% of the price, as asked. Adding 40% to the cost would give EUR 71.40, where the EUR 20.40 of gross profit is only 28.6% of the price, because the percentage went onto the smaller base. That last check is worth doing every time: divide the gross profit by the price and make sure you get the margin you were asked for.

Use this method for the rest of the unit: take the target margin away from one, divide the cost by the result, then check by dividing the gross profit by the price. If a question gives you a markup rather than a margin, convert it with margin = markup / (1 + markup) first. You don't need to try another approach first; just follow these steps as shown.
