# Explanation

Let's revisit the margin of safety, this time for another business. Two businesses can plan exactly the same profit and still face very different risks. It measures how far sales can fall from the planned level before the business stops covering its costs. A hotel planning well above its break-even point can ride out a bad season; one planning just above it cannot, even when both expect a profitable year.

You need two figures, and the order matters. The cushion is budgeted sales minus break-even sales, in euros. Turning the cushion into a percentage lets you compare businesses of different sizes, and the base of that percentage is budgeted sales, because the question you are answering is what share of the plan could be lost.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school plans revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and finds that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Against budgeted revenue that is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The slip is dividing the cushion by break-even sales. For the ski school that gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering figure that answers a question no one asked: it shows how far the plan sits above break-even relative to break-even, not what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before you divide, say out loud what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is nearly always the level the business is planning for. Keep that base in mind as I walk you through the calculation below, because the denominator is where this goes wrong.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? There is no need to work it out yourself. If you answer wrongly, I will show you the full calculation and the answer straight away, one step at a time.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the cushion is 1,000,000 - 800,000 = EUR 200,000 of revenue that could be lost before the hotel stops covering its costs. Step 2: divide by the base the question names, budgeted revenue: 200,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%. One booking season in five could vanish before trouble begins. Dividing by break-even revenue would show 25%, which sounds safer and answers another question: it compares the cushion with the revenue the hotel must make, not the revenue it plans to make. Read the answer as a statement about the plan: a fifth of budgeted revenue could vanish before this hotel stops covering its costs.

Use these two steps for the rest of the unit: take break-even away from budgeted sales to get the cushion, then divide the cushion by budgeted sales. If the question asks for units or room-nights, divide the same cushion by the price instead. Follow the steps exactly as shown.
