# Explanation

Let us return to price elasticity, applied to a different business. A price increase that succeeds for one business may prove ruinous for another, depending on the strength of the customer response. Elasticity measures that response as the ratio of two percentage changes: the movement in quantity relative to the movement in price. The use of percentages is not a matter of presentation but the only means by which the figure acquires meaning, since it removes the units and leaves a pure comparison of responsiveness.

Each percentage is calculated against its own starting value. Members lost are expressed as a share of the members previously held, and the price increase as a share of the previous price. The result is interpreted relative to one: below one, demand is inelastic and total revenue rises with the price; above one, it is elastic and revenue falls; at exactly one, the two effects offset each other.

Formally:

Elasticity = (% change in quantity) / (% change in price)

Worked example. A cinema increases its ticket price from EUR 10 to EUR 11 and weekly admissions decline from 2,000 to 1,800. Admissions fall by 200 / 2,000 = 10%. The price rises by 1 / 10 = 10%. Elasticity = 10% / 10% = 1.00, the unit-elastic case in which revenue is unchanged.

The characteristic error is to divide absolute members by absolute euros. For the cinema this yields 200 admissions per euro, which would become 2 admissions per cent for identical behaviour, so it cannot describe the customers at all. A second variant measures the changes against the final values rather than the initial ones, so that a price increase and the identical reduction yield different elasticities for the same pair of points. Bear the comparison with one in mind as I present the calculation below, since absolute amounts are the customary error.

# Problem

A gym raises its monthly membership from EUR 20 to EUR 24 and the number of members falls from 1,000 to 700. What is the price elasticity of demand, expressed as a positive number? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: membership declines by 300 out of 1,000, which is 30%. Step 2: the price increases by 4 out of 20, which is 20%. Step 3: divide: 30% / 20% = 1.50. Demand is elastic: membership responded proportionately more than the price changed, so revenue falls from EUR 20,000 to EUR 16,800 and the increase was an error. Dividing the absolute figures would report 75 members per euro, which would become 0.75 were the price quoted in cents, and so cannot be a property of demand itself. Note the implication for the decision: above one, the gym sells proportionately fewer memberships than the price rose, so the increase reduced its revenue.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: express each change as a percentage of its starting value, divide the quantity percentage by the price percentage, then compare the result with one to determine the direction of the change in revenue. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
