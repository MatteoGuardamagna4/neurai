# Explanation

Let us examine target profit after tax together. Such questions ask what volume a business must sell to achieve a specified profit, and the arithmetic is the break-even calculation with a single additional term. Each unit sold contributes its margin, the price less the variable cost, first to the fixed costs and subsequently to profit, so the required quantity equals the fixed costs plus the desired profit, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Taxation alters what that required profit is. A company does not retain the profit it earns; it retains the amount remaining after the tax authority has taken its share. If the tax rate is one quarter, the owners retain 75 cents of every euro of pre-tax profit, so a target expressed after tax is smaller than the profit the business must actually generate. Converting the one into the other requires dividing the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate.

The required volume is therefore:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop incurs fixed costs of EUR 120,000 per year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner requires EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit required = 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = EUR 60,000. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 60,000) / 12 = 15,000 units.

The characteristic error is to add the after-tax target directly to the fixed costs. For the workshop this gives (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units, and at that volume the pre-tax profit is precisely EUR 45,000, of which the tax authority takes one quarter, leaving the owner EUR 33,750 and well short of the target. The profit must first be grossed up, and thereafter treated as though it were an additional fixed cost. Bear this first step in mind as I present the calculation below, since grossing up the target is the step most commonly omitted.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: gross up the target. Tax takes 20%, so the after-tax profit is 80% of the pre-tax profit: 128,000 / 0.8 = EUR 160,000 must be earned before tax. Step 2: calculate the contribution margin: 90 - 50 = EUR 40 per unit. Step 3: the contribution must cover the fixed costs and the pre-tax profit: (300,000 + 160,000) / 40 = 11,500 units. Adding the after-tax target directly would give (300,000 + 128,000) / 40 = 10,700 units, which leaves the company short once the tax liability falls due. Observe that the pre-tax profit behaves precisely as an additional fixed cost: once grossed up, it simply joins the EUR 300,000 in the numerator.

Apply these three steps for the remainder of the unit: divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate, add the result to the fixed costs, then divide the total by the contribution margin per unit. Where the tax rate changes, repeat the first step before proceeding. No alternative route is required; apply the steps exactly as presented.
