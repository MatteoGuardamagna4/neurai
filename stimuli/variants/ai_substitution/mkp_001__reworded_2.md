# Explanation

Let us examine markup and margin together. They describe the same difference between the cost of a product and its selling price, but they measure that difference against different bases, and confusing them is among the most costly errors in retail pricing. A markup expresses the difference as a percentage of the cost. A gross margin expresses the same difference as a percentage of the selling price. Since the price is always the larger base, the margin percentage is always the smaller figure for any given product.

The practical implication is that a target margin cannot be achieved by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer requires two fifths of the selling price to be gross profit, the cost must constitute the remaining three fifths of the price, so the price equals the cost divided by 0.6 rather than the cost multiplied by 1.4. Expressing the relationship as an equation, cost equals price multiplied by one minus the margin, makes the rearrangement evident.

The rule is a division rather than an addition:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A shop purchases a lamp for EUR 30 and seeks a gross margin of 25% of the selling price. If the margin is one quarter of the price, the cost is the remaining three quarters: price = 30 / 0.75 = EUR 40. Verification: the margin is 40 - 30 = EUR 10, one quarter of 40. Adding 25% to the cost would have yielded EUR 37.50, a margin of only 20%.

The characteristic error is to multiply the cost by one plus the target margin. A lamp priced in this manner sells for EUR 37.50 in the belief that a quarter of the price is gross profit, whereas the profit of EUR 7.50 is only one fifth of it. The discrepancy increases with the target: a margin of half the price requires the cost to be doubled, while adding half to the cost yields a margin of only one third. The two measures are related by margin = markup / (1 + markup). Bear this in mind as I present the calculation below, since the choice of base is the step most frequently mistaken.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: interpret the percentage correctly. The margin is measured on the selling price, so the cost represents the remainder: 1 - 0.4 = 0.6 of the price. Step 2: state the relationship: 51 = price x 0.6. Step 3: divide: 51 / 0.6 = EUR 85.00. Verification: the gross profit is 85 - 51 = EUR 34, and 34 / 85 is 40% of the price, as required. Adding 40% to the cost would instead give EUR 71.40, where the gross profit of EUR 20.40 is only 28.6% of the price, since the percentage was applied to the smaller base. The concluding verification merits repetition every time: divide the gross profit by the price and confirm that the result equals the required margin.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: subtract the target margin from one, divide the cost by the result, then verify by dividing the gross profit by the price. Where a question provides a markup rather than a margin, convert it using margin = markup / (1 + markup) before proceeding. No alternative approach is required; apply the steps exactly as presented.
