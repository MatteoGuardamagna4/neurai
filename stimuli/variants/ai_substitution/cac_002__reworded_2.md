# Explanation

Let us return to CAC payback, applied to a different company. Growth is financed before it is earned, and this measure indicates how long the gap persists. The payback period on customer acquisition cost is the number of months a customer requires to return the amount spent on acquiring them. A short payback allows the marketing budget to be recovered and redeployed quickly; a long payback requires growth to be funded from other sources in the interim.

The numerator comprises the entire cost of acquisition, not merely advertising invoices: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions are all included, divided by the customers that expenditure actually generated. The denominator is the gross margin generated by a customer each month, since the portion of the subscription that funds hosting, delivery or support never becomes available for repayment.

The payback is the quotient of the two:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing in a quarter and acquires 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 per month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly margin = 100 x 0.4 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The characteristic error is division by the price paid by the customer. For the fitness app this reports 240 / 100 = 2.4 months rather than 6.0, suggesting that the channel is two and a half times faster than it is, and the distortion is greatest for businesses with low margins, which are precisely those least able to afford a misjudgement. The complementary measure should remain in view: payback indicates how quickly expenditure is recovered, lifetime value how much is recovered in total. Bear this in mind as I present the calculation below, since using margin rather than revenue is the step most commonly omitted.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 144,000 / 400 = EUR 360. Step 2: the monthly gross margin per customer is 150 x 0.5 = EUR 75, the amount genuinely available to repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 360 / 75 = 4.8 months. Division by the EUR 150 of revenue would instead report 2.4 months, treating the half of every euro spent on serving the customer as though it were returned to the company. Observe that the margin matters as much as the expenditure: a company with an identical acquisition cost but half the margin waits twice as long for the same return.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: divide the period's sales and marketing expenditure by the customers it generated, multiply the monthly revenue by the gross margin, then divide the first result by the second. Where paid and organic customers are distinguished, divide the paid expenditure only by the customers it actually acquired. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
