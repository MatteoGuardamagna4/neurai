# Explanation

Let us return to the margin of safety, applied to a different business. Two businesses may plan an identical profit and nevertheless face entirely different risks. The measure indicates how far sales may fall below the planned level before the business ceases to cover its costs. A hotel planning well above its break-even point can withstand a poor season; one planning only slightly above it cannot, even if both anticipate a profitable year.

Two figures are required, and their order is significant. The cushion is budgeted sales less break-even sales, expressed in euros. Converting that cushion into a percentage renders it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base of the percentage is budgeted sales, since the question being addressed is what share of the plan could be lost.

Formally:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and determines that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Relative to budgeted revenue this is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The characteristic error is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. For the ski school this gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering figure that answers a question no one posed: it indicates how far the plan lies above break-even relative to break-even, rather than what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, state explicitly what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is almost always the level the business is planning for. Bear this base in mind as I present the calculation below, since the denominator is where the error arises.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the cushion is 1,000,000 - 800,000 = EUR 200,000 of revenue that could be lost before the hotel ceases to cover its costs. Step 2: divide by the base specified in the question, budgeted revenue: 200,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%. One booking season in five could be lost before difficulties arise. Division by break-even revenue would instead report 25%, which appears safer and addresses a different question: it compares the cushion with the revenue the hotel must earn rather than with the revenue it plans to earn. Interpret the result as a statement about the plan: one fifth of budgeted revenue could be lost before this hotel ceases to cover its costs.

Apply these two steps for the remainder of the unit: subtract break-even from budgeted sales to obtain the cushion, then divide the cushion by budgeted sales. Where the question requires units or room-nights, divide the same cushion by the price instead. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
