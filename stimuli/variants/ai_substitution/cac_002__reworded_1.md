# Explanation

Let's revisit CAC payback, this time for another company. Growth gets paid for before it gets earned, and this tells you how long the gap lasts. The payback period on customer acquisition cost counts the months a customer needs to return what it cost to win them. A short payback lets the marketing budget come back fast and be spent again; a long one means someone else has to fund the growth while everybody waits.

On top goes the full cost of acquisition, not only the advertising bills: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions all count, divided by the customers that spending really brought in. Underneath goes the gross margin a customer produces each month, because the slice of the subscription that pays for hosting, delivery or support is never free to repay anything.

Divide one by the other:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. In one quarter a fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing and gains 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 a month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly margin = 100 x 0.4 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The slip is dividing by the price the customer pays. For the fitness app that shows 240 / 100 = 2.4 months instead of 6.0, so the channel looks two and a half times faster than it really is, and the error is largest for thin-margin businesses, exactly the ones that can least afford to get it wrong. Keep the sister measure in sight: payback tells you how quickly the money comes back, lifetime value tells you how much comes back altogether. Keep that in mind as I walk you through the calculation below, because using margin instead of revenue is the step people skip most.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? There is no need to work it out yourself. If you answer wrongly, I will show you the full calculation and the answer straight away, one step at a time.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 144,000 / 400 = EUR 360. Step 2: the monthly gross margin per customer is 150 x 0.5 = EUR 75, the amount that can really repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 360 / 75 = 4.8 months. Dividing by the EUR 150 of revenue would show 2.4 months, counting the half of every euro spent on serving the customer as if it came back to the company. See how the margin counts as much as the spending: a company with the same acquisition cost but half the margin waits twice as long for the same money.

Use this method for the rest of the unit: divide the period's sales and marketing spend by the customers it won, multiply the monthly revenue by the gross margin, then divide the first by the second. If paid and organic customers are kept apart, divide the paid spend only by the customers it really acquired. Follow the steps exactly as shown.
