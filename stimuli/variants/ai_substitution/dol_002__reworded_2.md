# Explanation

Let us return to operating leverage, applied to a different industry. Software firms move from losses to large profits on relatively modest changes in sales, for arithmetical reasons. Almost all of their costs are fixed and incurred before any copy is sold, whereas each additional licence is inexpensive to deliver. In combination, these features render profit highly sensitive to volume: the fixed costs must be met in all circumstances, so each sale beyond the break-even point accrues almost entirely to profit, and each lost sale removes almost the entire margin.

The degree of operating leverage expresses this sensitivity as a multiplier. It is total contribution divided by operating profit, and since contribution is the larger quantity, it always exceeds one for a profitable firm. A value of 4 indicates that a 5% decline in sales would reduce operating profit by 20%, which explains why a highly leveraged business requires a larger margin of safety than one that is not.

Formally:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A consultancy bills 600 days at EUR 900 per day. Each billed day incurs EUR 300 in contractor fees, and the firm's fixed costs amount to EUR 240,000. Contribution = 600 x 600 = EUR 360,000. Operating profit = 360,000 - 240,000 = EUR 120,000. Leverage = 360,000 / 120,000 = 3.00: a 10% change in billings alters profit by 30%.

The characteristic error is to invert the ratio. Operating profit divided by contribution invariably falls below one and cannot amplify anything; it represents the fraction of contribution remaining after the fixed costs, a legitimate figure that addresses a different question. Where a leverage calculation yields a value below one, the two figures have been interchanged. Bear this in mind as I present the calculation below, since inversion of the ratio is the customary error.

# Problem

A software firm sells 4,000 licences a year at EUR 250 each. Each licence costs EUR 50 in support and hosting, and the firm's fixed costs are EUR 550,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin per licence is 250 - 50 = EUR 200, so contribution is 4,000 x 200 = EUR 800,000. Step 2: operating profit is 800,000 - 550,000 = EUR 250,000. Step 3: leverage is 800,000 / 250,000 = 3.20. A 10% increase in licence sales would raise operating profit by 32%; a 10% decrease would reduce it correspondingly, which is precisely why software firms are so exposed to a weak quarter. Inverting the ratio would give 0.3125, the share of contribution remaining after fixed costs, which addresses a different question. Observe the warning the figure conveys: with leverage above three, the loss of one sixth of licence sales would eliminate half of operating profit.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: multiply the volume by the unit margin to obtain contribution, deduct the fixed costs to obtain operating profit, then divide contribution by profit. Apply any percentage change in sales by multiplying it by that figure. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
