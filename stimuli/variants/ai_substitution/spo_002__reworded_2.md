# Explanation

Let us return to a one-off engagement decision, applied to a different business. Such an engagement should be evaluated on the basis of what it alters, and the costing system is not designed to address that question. A cost is relevant to the decision only if acceptance of the work causes it to differ. Costs that will be incurred irrespective of the decision are irrelevant, however carefully the accounts distribute them across engagements, and a share of an unavoidable cost remains unavoidable.

Allocated overhead is the classic pitfall, and it operates in both directions. Office rent and management salaries are distributed across engagements so that each bears a portion, which is the appropriate way to report the full cost of an engagement, yet the rent does not increase when one further engagement is accepted. Conversely, a licence, a permit or a tool acquired solely because the engagement was accepted is entirely relevant, even though it is a fixed amount rather than a cost per hour.

For work that can be accommodated within existing capacity:

Incremental profit = Price - Costs the job causes

Worked example. A photographer with an unoccupied weekend is offered a wedding for EUR 1,800. Travel, assistants and album printing for the engagement cost EUR 700. The accounts allocate EUR 400 of studio rent and equipment depreciation to a booking of this size, and the couple require a drone permit costing EUR 150, acquired solely for this wedding. The EUR 400 of studio rent is incurred regardless of the weekend's activity, so it is not relevant. Incremental profit = 1,800 - 700 - 150 = EUR 950. Charging the allocated rent would have shown EUR 550 and made an excellent booking appear unremarkable.

The characteristic error is to compare the offered price with the full cost reported by the accounts. The photographer would set EUR 1,800 against a full cost of EUR 1,250 and conclude that the wedding scarcely justified the weekend; had the allocated share been larger, the booking would have been declined and the unoccupied weekend would have earned nothing. The engagement should be assessed on the cash it alters, while the allocation fulfils its separate reporting function. Bear this test in mind as I present the calculation below, since the classification of costs is where the error arises.

# Problem

A software firm with idle developer time is offered a one-off customisation for EUR 40,000. The developer hours and cloud capacity the job consumes cost EUR 22,000. The firm's costing system allocates EUR 12,000 of office and management overhead to a job of this size, and that overhead is incurred whether or not the job is taken. The job also needs a third-party licence costing EUR 5,000, bought only for this customer. What is the incremental profit from accepting? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: classify the three costs. The EUR 22,000 of developer time and cloud capacity is occasioned by the engagement, so it is included. The EUR 12,000 of allocated overhead is incurred irrespective of the firm's decision, so it is not. The EUR 5,000 licence arises solely because of the engagement, so it is included. Step 2: deduct only the costs that change: 40,000 - 22,000 - 5,000 = EUR 13,000 of incremental profit, and the engagement should be accepted. Charging the allocated overhead as well would leave EUR 1,000 and make a soundly profitable engagement appear negligible, inducing the firm to leave its developers idle. Observe that the licence and the allocated overhead are both fixed amounts and are nevertheless treated differently: what matters is not whether a cost is fixed but whether the decision creates it.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: classify every cost as altered or unaltered by the decision, deduct from the price only those that are altered, and exclude allocated overhead. Where the business has no spare capacity, also deduct the contribution forgone on the work displaced. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
