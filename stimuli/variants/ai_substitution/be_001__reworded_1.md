# Explanation

Let's go through break-even analysis together. Every business eventually faces the same question: how many units do we have to sell before we stop losing money? Finding that point takes three numbers. The first is fixed costs, the bills that do not move with sales volume, like rent, salaries and insurance. The second is the selling price, the money one unit brings in. The third is the variable cost, the extra cost of making and delivering one more unit, like materials, packaging and labour paid per unit.

Take the price and subtract the variable cost, and you get the contribution margin. Think of it as what is left from each sale once the unit has paid for itself, and that leftover goes towards the fixed costs. As long as the fixed costs are not fully covered the business runs at a loss. After they are covered, each extra unit sold adds one contribution margin of profit.

So the break-even quantity is simply the fixed costs divided by the contribution margin:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. Picture a cafe with EUR 24,000 a month of rent, salaries and insurance. A coffee sells for EUR 5, and the beans, milk and cup for it cost EUR 2. Each cup therefore contributes 5 - 2 = EUR 3, and the cafe breaks even at 24,000 / 3 = 8,000 cups a month. At 7,000 cups it loses EUR 3,000; at 9,000 cups it makes EUR 3,000.

People most often go wrong by dividing the fixed costs by the price instead of by the contribution margin. For the cafe that gives 24,000 / 5 = 4,800 cups, which is far too few: it treats every euro of revenue as if it could pay the rent, when EUR 2 of each cup was already used up making it. Remember the order: subtract, then divide. Get the margin first, and only then divide the fixed costs by it. Keep that trap in mind as I walk you through the calculation below, since it is the step people skip most.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 180,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 75 and has a variable cost of EUR 45. How many units must it sell per year to break even? There is no need to work it out yourself. If you answer wrongly, I will show you the full calculation and the answer straight away, one step at a time.

# Worked solution

Step 1: list the inputs. The fixed costs are EUR 180,000, the price is EUR 75 and the variable cost is EUR 45 per unit. Step 2: find the contribution margin: 75 - 45 = EUR 30 per unit. From each EUR 75 that comes in, EUR 45 has already gone on making the unit, so only EUR 30 is left to pay off the fixed costs. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by the margin: 180,000 / 30 = 6,000 units. To break even the company has to sell 6,000 units a year. Dividing by the price would give 180,000 / 75 = 2,400 units, which is much too low because it leaves out the EUR 45 it costs to make every unit.

Keep this two-step method handy for the rest of the unit: find the margin, then divide. Note the fixed costs, the price and the variable cost, take the variable cost away from the price, and divide the fixed costs by what is left. If the figures change, work out the margin again before dividing. You don't need to try another approach first; just follow these steps as shown.
