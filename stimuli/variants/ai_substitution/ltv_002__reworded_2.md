# Explanation

Let us return to customer lifetime value, applied to a different business. A subscription company does not earn its revenue at the first sale, so that first payment considerably understates a customer's worth. Lifetime value is the worth of a single customer across the entire relationship, and it is the figure that determines how much may reasonably be spent to acquire them. It converts a marketing budget from an expense into an investment whose return can be verified.

It comprises two elements, each easily misjudged. The first is the amount the company retains each period, which is a margin rather than a price: a subscription of EUR 80 that costs EUR 32 to fulfil leaves EUR 48, and only that EUR 48 is available for acquisition, overheads and profit. The second is the duration of the relationship, which derives from churn: where a constant proportion departs each month, the average life equals one divided by that proportion, so a monthly churn of 5% implies twenty months and 10% implies ten.

In combination:

Lifetime value = Period revenue x Gross margin / Churn rate for that period

Worked example. A magazine charges EUR 15 per month. Printing, postage and editorial absorb 20% of that amount, leaving an 80% gross margin, and 4% of subscribers cancel each month. Monthly margin = 15 x 0.8 = EUR 12. The average subscriber remains 1 / 0.04 = 25 months. Lifetime value = 12 x 25 = EUR 300.

The characteristic error is to base the figure on revenue. For the magazine this gives 15 / 0.04 = EUR 375 rather than EUR 300, and the discrepancy grows as margins narrow: a business retaining only a third of its revenue would overstate each customer threefold. A related error assumes a fixed life of one year, discarding precisely the information conveyed by churn and concealing the benefit of improved retention. Bear this in mind as I present the calculation below, since the application of the margin is the step most commonly omitted.

# Problem

A meal-kit company charges EUR 80 per customer per month. Its gross margin is 60% of that revenue, and 8% of its customers cancel each month. What is the lifetime value of a customer, before discounting? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the monthly gross margin is 80 x 0.6 = EUR 48, the amount remaining after the boxes have been packed and delivered. Step 2: with 8% cancelling each month, a customer remains 1 / 0.08 = 12.5 months on average. Step 3: multiply: 48 x 12.5 = EUR 600, equivalent to dividing the monthly margin by the churn rate. Using the full EUR 80 of revenue would give EUR 1,000 and would allow the company to spend on acquisition money already committed to ingredients and couriers. Observe that lifetime value responds far more to churn than to price: halving the cancellation rate doubles the result, whereas a ten per cent price increase raises it by only a tenth.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: multiply the period revenue by the gross margin to obtain the amount retained, then divide by the churn rate for the same period. Where the question specifies an average life rather than a churn rate, multiply by it instead of dividing. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
