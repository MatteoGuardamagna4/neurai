# Explanation

Let us return to the contribution margin ratio, applied to a different business. It is the instrument that converts a break-even calculation from units into euros of revenue. The ratio addresses a single question: of each euro paid by a customer, what amount remains once the cost of serving that customer has been deducted? It is defined as the contribution margin divided by the price, and it is invariably a proportion of the price rather than of the cost.

The distinction is significant because each sale comprises two complementary proportions. One is expended in delivering the sale; the other remains for the fixed costs and subsequently for profit. The two always sum to one, so stating both explicitly before dividing is a reliable safeguard against selecting the wrong one. A ratio of 0.4 signifies that 40 cents of each euro remains and 60 cents is consumed.

Once the ratio has been determined, the revenue required to cover the fixed costs follows directly:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A translation agency incurs fixed costs of EUR 60,000 per year. A typical assignment is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. Contribution margin = 500 - 200 = EUR 300, and the ratio is therefore 300 / 500 = 0.6. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.6 = EUR 100,000 of billings per year.

The characteristic error is to divide by the consumed proportion rather than the remaining one. At the translation agency, freelance fees absorb 200 / 500 = 0.4 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.4 yields EUR 150,000, overstating the agency's requirement by one half. The correct ratio produces a substantially lower figure, so the error is never negligible. Bear these two proportions in mind as I present the calculation below, since selecting the wrong one is the customary error.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin on one contract is 800 - 500 = EUR 300. Step 2: as a proportion of the price this is 300 / 800 = 0.375, so 37.5 cents of every euro billed remains after labour and materials have been paid. Step 3: 144,000 / 0.375 = EUR 384,000 of billings per year. Division by the variable cost ratio of 0.625 would instead give EUR 230,400, a measure of how much revenue the company spends rather than how much it retains. Observe why the result must be a revenue rather than a count: division by a ratio yields euros, whereas division by a margin in euros would yield a number of contracts.

Apply these three steps for the remainder of the unit: calculate the margin on one sale, divide it by the price to obtain the ratio, then divide the fixed costs by that ratio. Where a profit target is specified, add it to the fixed costs first. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
