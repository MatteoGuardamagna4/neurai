# Explanation

Let's revisit markup and margin, this time for another shop. One and the same euro of profit can be called a 25% markup or a 20% margin. Both describe the gap between what an item costs and what it sells for. A markup measures that gap against the cost; a gross margin measures it against the selling price. Because the price is the larger base, the margin percentage is always the smaller number for the very same item.

So in practice you cannot reach a target margin by adding that percentage to the cost. If three tenths of the price is meant to be gross profit, the other seven tenths must be the cost, so the price is the cost divided by 0.7. Writing it as cost equals price times one minus the margin makes the rearranging obvious and takes away the urge to multiply.

The rule is a division:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A bookshop pays EUR 45 for a hardback and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. If the margin takes 40% of the price, the cost is the other 60%: price = 45 / 0.6 = EUR 75. Check: the profit is 75 - 45 = EUR 30, which is 40% of 75. Adding 40% to the cost would have given EUR 63, where the EUR 18 of profit is only 28.6% of the price.

The slip is multiplying the cost by one plus the target margin. The bookshop would then charge EUR 63 and think it earned 40% of the price, when the EUR 18 of profit is 28.6% of it. The gap widens as the target rises, which is why the error does most damage on the items a shop leans on most. The two measures are linked by margin = markup / (1 + markup), a conversion worth learning by heart. Keep that in mind as I walk you through the calculation below, because the base is where this goes wrong.

# Problem

A boutique buys a jacket for EUR 84 and wants a gross margin of 30% of the selling price. What price should it set? There is no need to work it out yourself. If you answer wrongly, I will show you the full calculation and the answer straight away, one step at a time.

# Worked solution

Step 1: read the percentage against the right base. The margin is a share of the selling price, so the cost must be 1 - 0.3 = 0.7 of it. Step 2: write the relationship: 84 = price x 0.7. Step 3: divide: 84 / 0.7 = EUR 120.00. Check: gross profit is 120 - 84 = EUR 36, and 36 / 120 is 30% of the price, just as required. Adding 30% to the cost would give EUR 109.20, where the EUR 25.20 of profit is only 23.1% of the price, because the percentage went onto the smaller of the two bases. That final check is the habit worth keeping: divide the gross profit by the price you have just set and make sure it matches the margin you were asked for.

Use this method for the rest of the unit: take the target margin away from one, divide the cost by the result, then check by dividing the profit by the price. If a question gives a markup instead, convert it with margin = markup / (1 + markup) before anything else. Follow the steps exactly as shown.
