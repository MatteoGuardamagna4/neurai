# Explanation

Let us examine CAC payback together. The acquisition of a customer entails expenditure before that customer has made any payment, so a growing company continually finances a gap, and this measure indicates its duration: the number of months required for a customer to repay the amount spent to acquire them. A short payback indicates that sales and marketing expenditure is recovered rapidly and can be redeployed; a long payback indicates that growth must meanwhile be funded from other sources.

Two figures are required. The acquisition cost per customer is the total sales and marketing expenditure of a period divided by the number of customers it generated, including not only advertising invoices but also the associated salaries, tools and commissions. The monthly repayment is the gross margin generated by a customer, since the portion of the subscription that funds hosting, support and payment processing is never available for repayment.

The payback is the quotient of the two:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A company spends EUR 48,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 200 new customers. Each pays EUR 80 per month, and the gross margin is 50%. Acquisition cost per customer = 48,000 / 200 = EUR 240. Monthly gross margin per customer = 80 x 0.5 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The characteristic error is to divide the acquisition cost by the price paid by the customer. In this example that yields 240 / 80 = 3.0 months, overstating the speed of repayment by a factor of two, and the distortion increases for businesses with low gross margins, which are precisely those least able to absorb it. The complementary measure should also be considered: payback indicates how quickly the expenditure is recovered, lifetime value how much is recovered in total. Bear this in mind as I present the calculation below, since using margin rather than revenue is the step most commonly omitted.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 81,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 300 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 100 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 60%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 81,000 / 300 = EUR 270. Step 2: monthly gross margin per customer = 100 x 0.6 = EUR 60, the amount genuinely available to repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 270 / 60 = 4.5 months. Division by the EUR 100 of revenue would instead yield 2.7 months, treating the 40 cents of every euro spent on serving the customer as though it were returned to the company. Observe that the payback depends as much on the margin as on the expenditure: a company with an identical acquisition cost but a lower margin waits considerably longer for its money.

Apply this method for the remainder of the unit: divide the period's sales and marketing expenditure by the customers it generated, multiply the monthly revenue by the gross margin, then divide the first result by the second. Where a question distinguishes paid from organic customers, divide the paid expenditure only by the customers it actually acquired. No alternative approach is required; apply the steps exactly as presented.
