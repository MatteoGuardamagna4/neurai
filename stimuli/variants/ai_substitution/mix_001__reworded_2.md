# Explanation

Let us examine break-even where more than one product is sold. Once a business sells two products, the formula requires a margin that does not yet exist. Each product has its own contribution margin, whereas the fixed costs are shared among all of them, so no single figure is available as a divisor. The solution is to construct one: an average margin for a representative sale, which depends not only on the two margins but also on the quantities of each actually sold.

This is what makes the average a weighted one. If three inexpensive items are sold for every expensive one, the margin of the inexpensive item describes three quarters of the goods sold and should carry three times the weight. Weighting by the sales mix yields the margin of an average unit, and dividing the fixed costs by it yields the total number of units, which may then be allocated back to the individual products in the same proportion.

Formally, for a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A cafe earns a margin of EUR 2 on each coffee and EUR 5 on each cake, and sells four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 per month. Weighted average margin = (4 x 2 + 1 x 5) / 5 = 13 / 5 = EUR 2.60 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 2.60 = 10,000 items per month, comprising 8,000 coffees and 2,000 cakes.

The characteristic error is to average the two margins without weights. For the cafe this gives (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 instead of EUR 2.60, understating the required volume by a quarter, since it implicitly assumes that the cafe sells as many cakes as coffees, whereas it in fact sells four times as much coffee. The unweighted average is correct only where the products sell in equal numbers. Bear the weights in mind as I present the calculation below, since averaging without them is the customary error.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? It is not necessary to solve this yourself. Should your answer be incorrect, I shall present the complete calculation and the answer immediately, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 30 - 10 = EUR 20 on A and 50 - 40 = EUR 10 on B. Step 2: weight them by the mix. In every batch of four units, three are A and one is B, so the average unit contributes (3 x 20 + 1 x 10) / 4 = 70 / 4 = EUR 17.50. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that figure: 105,000 / 17.50 = 6,000 units in total, comprising 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B. An unweighted average of the two margins would give EUR 15 and 7,000 units, overstating the requirement since it assumes the shop sells as many of the low-margin product as of the high-margin one. Note that the result is a total across both products, which is why the final step allocates it back into 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B in the same three-to-one proportion.

Apply these three steps for the remainder of the unit: calculate each product's margin, weight them by the sales mix to obtain the margin of an average unit, then divide the fixed costs by that weighted margin. Allocate the total back to the products in the same proportions. Apply the steps exactly as presented.
