# Explanation

Let us examine the weighted average cost of capital together. Capital is not costless and seldom derives from a single source, so this is the single rate that summarises the annual cost to the company of all its financing. It is the rate applied to discount projects of average risk, so an error in it distorts every investment decision the company makes. It comprises three elements: the cost of each source of capital, the proportion of the total provided by each source, and the tax treatment of interest.

The weights derive from the amounts of equity and debt the company actually employs: a firm financed predominantly by shareholders is dominated by the cost of equity, the return those shareholders require for the risk they bear. Interest differs from a dividend in one significant respect. It is deducted before profit is taxed, so each euro of interest reduces the tax liability, and the company bears only one minus the tax rate of the rate charged by its lender.

Combining the three elements:

WACC = (E / (E + D)) x Cost of equity + (D / (E + D)) x Cost of debt x (1 - Tax rate)

Worked example. A firm is financed with EUR 400 million of equity and EUR 100 million of debt. Shareholders require 15%, the bank charges 8%, and the corporate tax rate is 25%. Weights: equity represents 400 / 500 = 0.8 of the capital, debt 0.2. After-tax cost of debt = 8% x (1 - 0.25) = 6%. WACC = 0.8 x 15% + 0.2 x 6% = 12% + 1.2% = 13.2%.

The characteristic error is to average the two costs and proceed no further. In the example the simple average of 15% and 8% is 11.5%, considerably below the weighted figure of 13.2%, which would make projects appear cheaper to finance than they are; for a heavily indebted firm the same error operates in the opposite direction. Each source must be weighted by the extent of its use, and the tax shield must never be applied to equity, since dividends are not deductible. Bear these three steps in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your treatment of the tax rate.

# Problem

A firm is financed with EUR 300 million of equity and EUR 200 million of debt. Its shareholders require a return of 12%, its debt carries an interest rate of 5% before tax, and the corporate tax rate is 25%. What is the firm's weighted average cost of capital? # Diagnostic questions

# Hints

Hint 1: The two sources of capital are not employed in equal amounts, so begin by determining the proportion of total capital each provides.

Hint 2: Interest is deducted before tax is calculated, so debt costs the company less than the rate charged by the lender. Multiply the interest rate by one minus the tax rate before using it.

Hint 3: Multiply each source's proportion of capital by its cost, using the after-tax cost for debt, and sum the two products.
