# Explanation

Let's come back to operating leverage, this time in another industry. Software firms lurch from losses to big profits on fairly small changes in sales, and the cause is arithmetic. Nearly all their cost is fixed and spent before the first copy is sold, while each extra licence is cheap to deliver. Put those together and profit becomes extremely sensitive to volume: the fixed costs have to be paid regardless, so every sale past break-even falls almost wholly into profit, and every lost sale takes nearly its whole margin away.

The degree of operating leverage measures that sensitivity as a multiplier. It is total contribution divided by operating profit, and since contribution is the bigger of the two, it is always above one for a profitable firm. A value of 4 says that sales down 5% would cut operating profit by 20%, which is why a highly leveraged business needs a wider margin of safety than one that is not.

In symbols:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A consultancy bills 600 days a year at EUR 900 a day. Every billed day costs EUR 300 in contractor fees, and the firm's fixed costs are EUR 240,000. Contribution = 600 x 600 = EUR 360,000. Operating profit = 360,000 - 240,000 = EUR 120,000. Leverage = 360,000 / 120,000 = 3.00: billings moving 10% move profit 30%.

The slip is turning the ratio over. Operating profit divided by contribution always comes out under one and cannot amplify anything; it gives the fraction of contribution that survives the fixed costs, a real number that answers another question. If a leverage calculation gives you less than one, you have swapped the two figures. Hold on to that while you tackle the problem below, because if your result is off, I will start by asking which figure you divided by which.

# Problem

A software firm sells 4,000 licences a year at EUR 250 each. Each licence costs EUR 50 in support and hosting, and the firm's fixed costs are EUR 550,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? Have a go on your own first. I won't give you the answer: if you get it wrong, I'll ask you something about how you worked it out and offer a hint, and then you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I comment on your result, here are three questions for you.

Before the EUR 550,000 of fixed costs comes off, what do the 4,000 licences contribute altogether?

Is your answer bigger than one, and what would it say about the firm if it were not?

Imagine someone reports 0.3125. What has that person really worked out?

# Hints

Hint 1: You need two figures first: what the licences contribute altogether, and what is left once the fixed costs are paid.

Hint 2: Contribution is the number of licences times the margin on one. Operating profit is that amount minus the EUR 550,000 of fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the contribution by the operating profit. A software firm with heavy fixed costs and a small variable cost will always come out high.

The last step of the calculation is for you to do.
