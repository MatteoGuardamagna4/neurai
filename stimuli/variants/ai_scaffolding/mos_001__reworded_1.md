# Explanation

Let's figure out the margin of safety together. It is about risk, not profit: it tells you how far sales can drop from the level a company is planning for before it stops covering its costs. A company that budgets sales well above break-even can survive a bad quarter; one that budgets only just above it cannot. Lenders and boards treat the figure as a measure of resilience, because with a thin margin of safety a lost customer, a price cut or a slow month turns a planned profit into a loss. You need two figures: budgeted sales, and the break-even sales the company has already worked out.

The gap between them is the cushion, measured in euros. That gap says much more as a percentage, since EUR 300,000 of headroom means one thing for a small firm and quite another for a large one. The percentage is taken against budgeted sales, the level the company is planning for, so the result reads as the share of the plan that could be lost before trouble begins.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A publisher plans sales of EUR 250,000 for the year and works out that it breaks even at EUR 200,000 of sales. The cushion is 250,000 - 200,000 = EUR 50,000. As a share of budgeted sales that is 50,000 / 250,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%.

The usual slip is dividing the cushion by break-even sales instead. For the publisher that gives 50,000 / 200,000 = 25%, which makes things look better and, worse, answers another question: it shows how far above break-even the plan sits compared with break-even, not what share of the plan is at risk. Before you divide, ask what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is almost always the sales the company plans to make. Hold on to that base while you tackle the problem below, because if your result is off, I will start by asking what you divided by.

# Problem

A manufacturer budgets sales of EUR 800,000 for next year and breaks even at EUR 500,000 of sales. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales? Have a go on your own first. I won't give you the answer: if you get it wrong, I'll ask you something about how you worked it out and offer a hint, and then you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I comment on your result, here are three questions for you.

How big is the cushion in euros, and which two figures did you subtract to find it?

When you made that cushion into a percentage, what did you divide by: the sales the company plans to make, or the sales it has to make?

Imagine someone reports 300,000 / 500,000 as the margin of safety. What question have they really answered?

# Hints

Hint 1: The margin of safety measures how far sales can fall before the company starts losing money. Begin by working out that distance in euros.

Hint 2: The question wants a percentage of budgeted sales, so budgeted sales is the base of the fraction, not the break-even figure.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion you found by the budgeted sales figure, then read the result as a percentage.

The last step of the calculation is for you to do.
