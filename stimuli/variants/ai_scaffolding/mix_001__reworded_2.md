# Explanation

Let us examine break-even where more than one product is sold. Once a business sells two products, the formula requires a margin that does not yet exist. Each product has its own contribution margin, whereas the fixed costs are shared among all of them, so no single figure is available as a divisor. The solution is to construct one: an average margin for a representative sale, which depends not only on the two margins but also on the quantities of each actually sold.

This is what makes the average a weighted one. If three inexpensive items are sold for every expensive one, the margin of the inexpensive item describes three quarters of the goods sold and should carry three times the weight. Weighting by the sales mix yields the margin of an average unit, and dividing the fixed costs by it yields the total number of units, which may then be allocated back to the individual products in the same proportion.

Formally, for a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A cafe earns a margin of EUR 2 on each coffee and EUR 5 on each cake, and sells four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 per month. Weighted average margin = (4 x 2 + 1 x 5) / 5 = 13 / 5 = EUR 2.60 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 2.60 = 10,000 items per month, comprising 8,000 coffees and 2,000 cakes.

The characteristic error is to average the two margins without weights. For the cafe this gives (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 instead of EUR 2.60, understating the required volume by a quarter, since it implicitly assumes that the cafe sells as many cakes as coffees, whereas it in fact sells four times as much coffee. The unweighted average is correct only where the products sell in equal numbers. Bear the weights in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern how you combined the two margins.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

What is the contribution margin on one unit of A, and on one unit of B?

Of every four units the shop sells, how many are A, and should the margin of A therefore count once or three times?

Consider averaging EUR 20 and EUR 10 to obtain EUR 15. What does that assume about the quantities of each product sold?

# Hints

Hint 1: With two products there is no single contribution margin, so the first task is to construct one that represents the mix the shop actually sells.

Hint 2: Determine the margin on each product, then weight them: of every four units sold, three are A and one is B, so the margin of A should carry three times the weight.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by that weighted average margin to obtain the total number of units, then allocate them to A and B in the same three-to-one proportion.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
