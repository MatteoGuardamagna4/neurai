# Explanation

Let us return to the weighted average cost of capital, applied to a different firm. A company that borrows at 8% and whose shareholders require 12% faces neither figure as its cost of capital. The weighted average cost of capital is the single rate representing the annual cost to the company of all its financing. It is the rate applied to discount projects of ordinary risk, so an error in it distorts every investment decision the company makes, in the same direction, for as long as the error persists.

It comprises three elements. Each source has its own cost: shareholders require a return for the risk they bear, and lenders quote an interest rate. Each source provides a proportion of the total capital, and those proportions are the weights. Finally, interest, unlike a dividend, is deducted before profit is taxed, so each euro of interest reduces the tax liability and the company bears only one minus the tax rate of the quoted rate.

Combining the three:

WACC = (E / (E + D)) x Cost of equity + (D / (E + D)) x Cost of debt x (1 - Tax rate)

Worked example. A firm is financed equally by equity and debt. Shareholders require 16%, the bank charges 10%, and the tax rate is 20%. After-tax cost of debt = 10% x (1 - 0.20) = 8%. WACC = 0.5 x 16% + 0.5 x 8% = 8% + 4% = 12%.

The characteristic error is to average the two costs and proceed no further. The firm in the example happens to be financed equally, so the weights are identical and the average appears innocuous, yet the tax shield remains significant: disregarding it would report 13% instead of 12%. For a firm financed predominantly in one way the weights matter considerably more than the shield, and a simple average may err by several points in either direction. The shield must never be applied to equity, since dividends are not deductible. Bear these three steps in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your treatment of the tax rate.

# Problem

A firm is financed with EUR 150 million of equity and EUR 50 million of debt. Its shareholders require a return of 12%, its debt carries an interest rate of 8% before tax, and the corporate tax rate is 25%. What is the firm's weighted average cost of capital? # Diagnostic questions

What proportion of this firm's capital is provided by shareholders, and what proportion by lenders?

Hint 2: Interest is deducted before tax is levied, so each euro of interest reduces the tax liability. The company bears only one minus the tax rate of the interest rate quoted by the lender.
