# Explanation

Let us return to CAC payback, applied to a different company. Growth is financed before it is earned, and this measure indicates how long the gap persists. The payback period on customer acquisition cost is the number of months a customer requires to return the amount spent on acquiring them. A short payback allows the marketing budget to be recovered and redeployed quickly; a long payback requires growth to be funded from other sources in the interim.

The numerator comprises the entire cost of acquisition, not merely advertising invoices: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions are all included, divided by the customers that expenditure actually generated. The denominator is the gross margin generated by a customer each month, since the portion of the subscription that funds hosting, delivery or support never becomes available for repayment.

The payback is the quotient of the two:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing in a quarter and acquires 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 per month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly margin = 100 x 0.4 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The characteristic error is division by the price paid by the customer. For the fitness app this reports 240 / 100 = 2.4 months rather than 6.0, suggesting that the channel is two and a half times faster than it is, and the distortion is greatest for businesses with low margins, which are precisely those least able to afford a misjudgement. The complementary measure should remain in view: payback indicates how quickly expenditure is recovered, lifetime value how much is recovered in total. Bear this in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your denominator.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

What did it cost this company to acquire one customer, and which two figures did you divide to establish this?

Of the EUR 150 a customer pays each month, what portion is available to repay that acquisition cost?

Consider dividing the acquisition cost by 150 rather than by the monthly margin. What does such a calculation assume about the cost of serving a customer?

# Hints

Hint 1: The problem has two components: the cost of acquiring one new customer, and the amount one customer returns each month. Determine each before dividing.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost equals the quarter's total sales and marketing expenditure divided by the customers that expenditure generated.

Hint 3: The monthly repayment is the gross margin, not the price: half of every EUR 150 is spent on serving the customer and never becomes available.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
