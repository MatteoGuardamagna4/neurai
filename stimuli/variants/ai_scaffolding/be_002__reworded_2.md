# Explanation

Let us return to break-even volume, applied to a different business. Every business that sells a product has a threshold below which it incurs a loss and above which it earns a profit. Identifying it requires three figures that must be kept strictly distinct: the costs that remain constant irrespective of activity, the revenue from a single sale, and the cost added by one additional sale. Rent, a licence and insurance fall into the first category; ingredients and packaging into the third.

The difference between the price and the variable cost is the contribution margin, and it is the only portion of a sale available to meet other expenses. A meal sold at EUR 12 that consumes EUR 4.50 of ingredients does not contribute EUR 12 towards the rent; it contributes EUR 7.50, since the remaining EUR 4.50 has already been expended on the meal.

Dividing the fixed costs by this margin yields the volume at which they are precisely covered:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A bike repair shop incurs EUR 2,400 per month for its workshop, tools and insurance. A standard service is priced at EUR 50 and requires EUR 20 of parts and consumables. Contribution margin = 50 - 20 = EUR 30 per service. Break-even = 2,400 / 30 = 80 services per month.

The most costly error in practice is division by the price. For the repair shop this would give 2,400 / 50 = 48 services, and a shop performing 48 services per month would fall far short of covering its workshop, since the EUR 20 of parts consumed on each job would have been disregarded. The sequence is invariant: subtract first, then divide. Bear it in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your divisor.

# Problem

A food truck pays EUR 3,600 a month for its pitch, licence and insurance. A meal is sold at EUR 12 and costs EUR 4.50 in ingredients and packaging. How many meals must it sell each month to break even? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

Which of the three quantities in the problem does the truck pay irrespective of whether it sells one meal or a thousand?

Once the meal itself has been paid for, what portion of the EUR 12 remains available for the pitch fee?

Consider a calculation of 3,600 divided by 12. What has become of the EUR 4.50 of ingredients in it?

# Hints

Hint 1: Three quantities are relevant, and each serves a distinct function: the amount the truck pays irrespective of sales, the revenue from one meal, and the cost of producing one meal. Identify them before calculating.

Hint 2: Only a portion of the EUR 12 is available for the pitch and the licence, since EUR 4.50 has already been spent on the meal itself. Determine the amount remaining from one sale.

Hint 3: Divide the monthly fixed costs by that remaining amount. The quotient is the number of meals required to cover them exactly.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
