# Explanation

Let's come back to the margin of safety, this time for another business. Two businesses can plan exactly the same profit and still face very different risks. It measures how far sales can fall from the planned level before the business stops covering its costs. A hotel planning well above its break-even point can ride out a bad season; one planning just above it cannot, even when both expect a profitable year.

You need two figures, and the order matters. The cushion is budgeted sales minus break-even sales, in euros. Turning the cushion into a percentage lets you compare businesses of different sizes, and the base of that percentage is budgeted sales, because the question you are answering is what share of the plan could be lost.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school plans revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and finds that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Against budgeted revenue that is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The slip is dividing the cushion by break-even sales. For the ski school that gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering figure that answers a question no one asked: it shows how far the plan sits above break-even relative to break-even, not what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before you divide, say out loud what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is nearly always the level the business is planning for. Hold on to that base while you tackle the problem below, because if your result is off, I will start by asking what you put under the line.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? Have a go on your own first. I won't give you the answer: if you get it wrong, I'll ask you something about how you worked it out and offer a hint, and then you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I comment on your result, here are three questions for you.

In euros, how much revenue can this hotel lose before it stops covering its costs?

When you made that into a percentage, did you divide by the revenue the hotel plans to take in or the revenue it must take in?

Imagine someone reports 200,000 / 800,000. What question have they answered, and is it the one you were asked?

# Hints

Hint 1: Begin with the distance in euros between what the hotel plans to take in and what it must take in to cover its costs. That distance is the cushion.

Hint 2: A percentage is always a percentage of something. Here the question names budgeted revenue, so that goes underneath the fraction.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion by budgeted revenue and read the result as a percentage of the plan.

The last step of the calculation is for you to do.
