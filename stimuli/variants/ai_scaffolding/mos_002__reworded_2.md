# Explanation

Let us return to the margin of safety, applied to a different business. Two businesses may plan an identical profit and nevertheless face entirely different risks. The measure indicates how far sales may fall below the planned level before the business ceases to cover its costs. A hotel planning well above its break-even point can withstand a poor season; one planning only slightly above it cannot, even if both anticipate a profitable year.

Two figures are required, and their order is significant. The cushion is budgeted sales less break-even sales, expressed in euros. Converting that cushion into a percentage renders it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base of the percentage is budgeted sales, since the question being addressed is what share of the plan could be lost.

Formally:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and determines that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Relative to budgeted revenue this is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The characteristic error is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. For the ski school this gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering figure that answers a question no one posed: it indicates how far the plan lies above break-even relative to break-even, rather than what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, state explicitly what the percentage is a percentage of; the base is almost always the level the business is planning for. Bear this base in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your denominator.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

How much revenue, in euros, can this hotel lose before it ceases to cover its costs?

In converting that into a percentage, did you divide by the revenue the hotel plans to earn or by the revenue it must earn?

Consider a result reported as 200,000 / 800,000. Which question does it answer, and is that the question posed?

# Hints

Hint 1: Begin with the distance in euros between the revenue the hotel plans to earn and the revenue it must earn to cover its costs. That distance is the cushion.

Hint 2: A percentage is always a percentage of something. Here the question specifies budgeted revenue, so that forms the denominator of the fraction.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion by budgeted revenue and interpret the result as a percentage of the plan.
