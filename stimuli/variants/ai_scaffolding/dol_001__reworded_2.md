# Explanation

Let us examine operating leverage together. Two companies with identical sales and profit may respond quite differently to an adverse quarter. The determining factor is the proportion of the cost base that is fixed. A firm whose costs vary with sales loses relatively little when sales decline, since its costs decline as well. A firm with substantial fixed costs continues to bear them regardless, so an identical decline in sales reduces profit far more severely.

The degree of operating leverage quantifies this sensitivity. It relates the total contribution of sales to the amount remaining after the fixed costs, and it is interpreted as a multiplier: a value of 3 signifies that each 1% change in sales alters operating profit by 3%, upwards in a favourable year and downwards in an unfavourable one. For a profitable firm it always exceeds one, since contribution always exceeds the profit remaining after fixed costs.

The ratio is defined as:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A workshop sells 5,000 units at EUR 40 with a variable cost of EUR 24, and incurs fixed costs of EUR 40,000. Contribution = 5,000 x 16 = EUR 80,000. Operating profit = 80,000 - 40,000 = EUR 40,000. Degree of operating leverage = 80,000 / 40,000 = 2.00, so a 10% increase in sales raises profit by 20%.

The characteristic error is to invert the ratio. Dividing profit by contribution produces a value below one, which cannot function as a multiplier and in fact represents the share of contribution that remains after the fixed costs. The verification is straightforward: leverage measures amplification, so a result below one indicates that the division has been reversed. Bear this verification in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your numerator.

# Problem

A manufacturer sells 10,000 units a year at EUR 50 each, with a variable cost of EUR 30 per unit and fixed costs of EUR 120,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? # Diagnostic questions

What is the total contribution of all 10,000 units before the fixed costs are deducted?

Which of your two figures is the larger, and which must form the numerator if the result is to be a multiplier?

# Hints

Hint 1: Operating leverage relates two quantities already available to the business: the total contribution of its sales, and the amount remaining after the fixed costs. Determine both before forming any ratio.

Hint 2: Total contribution equals the units sold multiplied by the margin on each. Operating profit equals that contribution less the fixed costs, and is therefore always the smaller of the two.

Hint 3: The degree of operating leverage is contribution divided by operating profit, the larger divided by the smaller, so the result always exceeds one for a profitable firm.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
