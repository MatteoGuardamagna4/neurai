# Explanation

Let us examine return on investment together. It is the most frequently cited figure in business and the most frequently miscalculated, almost invariably in the same direction. It addresses a single question: for each euro committed, how much was returned in addition to that euro? The words "in addition to" constitute the entire difficulty, since the funds a project returns generally include the original stake, and the stake was never profit.

The calculation therefore comprises two steps that must not be combined. First, distinguish the gain from the stake: if EUR 400,000 is invested and EUR 500,000 is received, the gain is EUR 100,000 and the remainder is merely the return of the company's own funds. Second, divide that gain by the amount placed at risk, since the denominator of any return is the amount committed rather than the amount received.

Formally:

ROI = (Total returned - Amount invested) / Amount invested

Worked example. A shop spends EUR 50,000 on a refit, which generates EUR 65,000 of additional gross profit over its life. The gain is 65,000 - 50,000 = EUR 15,000. ROI = 15,000 / 50,000 = 0.3, a return of 30% on the funds invested.

The characteristic error is to divide the total returned by the amount invested and describe the result as a return. That figure is the money multiple: it treats the stake as profit and renders a respectable project spectacular on paper. A less conspicuous variant divides the gain by the total received rather than by the investment, which understates the return. A useful verification is that a project which merely breaks even must show a return of zero, not of one. Bear this verification in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern your treatment of the original stake.

# Problem

A company invests EUR 400,000 in a project and receives EUR 500,000 back in total over the project's life. What is the return on investment? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

Of the EUR 500,000 received by the company, what portion is a genuine gain and what portion is the return of its own funds?

In your division, what formed the denominator: the funds placed at risk, or the funds received?

Consider a project that returns exactly its cost. What should its return on investment be, and what would the multiple report instead?

# Hints

Hint 1: The EUR 500,000 includes the EUR 400,000 invested by the company. Distinguish the funds returned from the gain before performing any division.

Hint 2: Return on investment is a return on the amount at risk, so the denominator is the amount invested, never the amount received.

Hint 3: Divide the gain by the investment and interpret the result as a percentage of the funds committed.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
