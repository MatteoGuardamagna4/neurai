# Explanation

Let us examine break-even revenue together. It may be calculated in euros of sales rather than in units, and for a business selling a wide range of products this is the only practicable approach. The relevant instrument is the contribution margin ratio: the proportion of each euro of sales that remains, once the variable cost of that sale has been met, to cover the fixed costs.

The ratio is obtained by dividing the contribution margin per unit, that is, the selling price less the variable cost, by the selling price. A ratio of 0.7 signifies that 70 cents of every euro of revenue is available for the fixed costs and, beyond them, for profit, whereas 30 cents covers the goods or the service itself. Since the ratio is a proportion of the price and never of the cost, it invariably lies between zero and one.

Dividing the fixed costs by this ratio yields the level of sales at which the business breaks even:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A bakery incurs fixed costs of EUR 96,000 per year. A cake is sold for EUR 40 and costs EUR 15 in ingredients, packaging and delivery. Contribution margin = 40 - 15 = EUR 25, and the contribution margin ratio is therefore 25 / 40 = 0.625. Break-even revenue = 96,000 / 0.625 = EUR 153,600 per year.

The most frequent error is division by the wrong ratio. In the bakery example, ingredients, packaging and delivery absorb 15 / 40 = 0.375 of every euro of sales; dividing the fixed costs by this ratio gives 96,000 / 0.375 = EUR 256,000, a considerable overstatement of the sales required. As the two ratios always sum to one, each should be identified before dividing: the variable cost ratio is what a sale consumes, the contribution margin ratio what it leaves. Bear this distinction in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern the ratio you used.

# Problem

A subscription business has fixed costs of EUR 252,000 per year. Each subscription sells for EUR 60 and carries variable servicing costs of EUR 24. What annual sales revenue does it need to break even? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

Which two figures did you use to construct your ratio, and which did you divide by the other?

Does your ratio measure the share of each euro that the business retains, or the share it spends on servicing the subscription?

Consider dividing 252,000 by 0.4. What does that 0.4 represent, and can the fixed costs be met from it?

# Hints

Hint 1: The question requires a revenue, not a number of units, so the divisor must be a ratio rather than an amount in euros. Identify the two euro amounts from which that ratio is constructed.

Hint 2: The contribution margin ratio equals the contribution margin per unit divided by the selling price. Calculate the margin per unit first, then express it as a proportion of the price.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the contribution margin ratio obtained. The quotient is the sales revenue at which the business breaks even.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
