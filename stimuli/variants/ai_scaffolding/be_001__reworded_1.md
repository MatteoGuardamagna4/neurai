# Explanation

Let's figure out break-even analysis together. Every business eventually faces the same question: how many units do we have to sell before we stop losing money? Finding that point takes three numbers. The first is fixed costs, the bills that do not move with sales volume, like rent, salaries and insurance. The second is the selling price, the money one unit brings in. The third is the variable cost, the extra cost of making and delivering one more unit, like materials, packaging and labour paid per unit.

Take the price and subtract the variable cost, and you get the contribution margin. Think of it as what is left from each sale once the unit has paid for itself, and that leftover goes towards the fixed costs. As long as the fixed costs are not fully covered the business runs at a loss. After they are covered, each extra unit sold adds one contribution margin of profit.

So the break-even quantity is simply the fixed costs divided by the contribution margin:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. Picture a cafe with EUR 24,000 a month of rent, salaries and insurance. A coffee sells for EUR 5, and the beans, milk and cup for it cost EUR 2. Each cup therefore contributes 5 - 2 = EUR 3, and the cafe breaks even at 24,000 / 3 = 8,000 cups a month. At 7,000 cups it loses EUR 3,000; at 9,000 cups it makes EUR 3,000.

People most often go wrong by dividing the fixed costs by the price instead of by the contribution margin. For the cafe that gives 24,000 / 5 = 4,800 cups, which is far too few: it treats every euro of revenue as if it could pay the rent, when EUR 2 of each cup was already used up making it. Remember the order: subtract, then divide. Get the margin first, and only then divide the fixed costs by it. Hold on to that mistake while you tackle the problem below, because if your result is off, my first question will be about your divisor.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 180,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 75 and has a variable cost of EUR 45. How many units must it sell per year to break even? Have a go on your own first. I won't give you the answer: if you get it wrong, I'll ask you something about how you worked it out and offer a hint, and then you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I comment on your result, here are three questions for you.

Which three figures did you pull out of the problem, and what does each one do in the calculation?

What did you divide by: the whole price of EUR 75, or a smaller amount?

Imagine someone works out 180,000 / 75. Does that allow for the EUR 45 it takes to make each unit?

# Hints

Hint 1: Begin by listing the three figures in the problem: the fixed costs, the price per unit and the variable cost per unit. Work out what job each one does before you calculate anything.

Hint 2: Do not divide by the price. First find the contribution margin, the price minus the variable cost per unit. That margin is how much each sale adds towards the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Now divide the fixed costs by that contribution margin. What you get is the number of units required to break even.

The last step of the calculation is for you to do.
