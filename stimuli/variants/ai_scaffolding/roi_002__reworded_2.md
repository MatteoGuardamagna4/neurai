# Explanation

Let us return to return on investment, applied to a different purchase. A machine that cost EUR 100,000 and generated EUR 160,000 has not yielded a return of 160%. A return measures the amount earned beyond the funds committed, per euro committed. The cash generated by a project ordinarily includes the recovery of the original outlay, and the return of one's own funds does not constitute profit, so that portion must be removed before any division is performed.

Once the gain has been isolated, the denominator is the amount placed at risk. This is the second point at which the calculation may err: dividing the gain by the cash generated rather than by the cash invested produces a smaller and equally incorrect result, since it measures the profit against the proceeds rather than against the exposure.

Formally:

ROI = (Total returned - Amount invested) / Amount invested

Worked example. A haulier spends EUR 25,000 on a trailer, which earns EUR 30,000 of additional cash before it is scrapped. The gain is 30,000 - 25,000 = EUR 5,000. ROI = 5,000 / 25,000 = 0.2, a return of 20% on the funds spent.

The characteristic error is the money multiple: total returned divided by amount invested. For the haulier this reports 120% on a trailer that in fact returned 20%, and the error invariably flatters, which is why it persists in investor presentations. The decisive test is the break-even case: a project that returns exactly its cost has earned nothing, so the correct method yields zero whereas the multiple yields one. Bear the break-even test in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern the treatment of the original EUR 100,000.

# Problem

A company buys equipment for EUR 100,000 and it generates EUR 160,000 of cash over its working life. What is the return on investment? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

What portion of the EUR 160,000 generated by the equipment is profit, and what portion is the recovery of the purchase price?

By what did you divide the gain, and why is that the appropriate base for a return?

Consider a reported figure of 160%. What would the same method report for equipment that generated exactly EUR 100,000?

# Hints

Hint 1: The EUR 160,000 represents everything the equipment produced, part of which is merely the EUR 100,000 being recovered. Determine the gain first.

Hint 2: The denominator of a return is always the amount placed at risk, not the amount received at the end.

Hint 3: Divide the gain by the EUR 100,000 invested and express the result as a percentage.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
