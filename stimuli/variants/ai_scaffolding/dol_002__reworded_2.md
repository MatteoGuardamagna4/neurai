# Explanation

Let us return to operating leverage, applied to a different industry. Software firms move from losses to large profits on relatively modest changes in sales, for arithmetical reasons. Almost all of their costs are fixed and incurred before any copy is sold, whereas each additional licence is inexpensive to deliver. In combination, these features render profit highly sensitive to volume: the fixed costs must be met in all circumstances, so each sale beyond the break-even point accrues almost entirely to profit, and each lost sale removes almost the entire margin.

The degree of operating leverage expresses this sensitivity as a multiplier. It is total contribution divided by operating profit, and since contribution is the larger quantity, it always exceeds one for a profitable firm. A value of 4 indicates that a 5% decline in sales would reduce operating profit by 20%, which explains why a highly leveraged business requires a larger margin of safety than one that is not.

Formally:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A consultancy bills 600 days at EUR 900 per day. Each billed day incurs EUR 300 in contractor fees, and the firm's fixed costs amount to EUR 240,000. Contribution = 600 x 600 = EUR 360,000. Operating profit = 360,000 - 240,000 = EUR 120,000. Leverage = 360,000 / 120,000 = 3.00: a 10% change in billings alters profit by 30%.

The characteristic error is to invert the ratio. Operating profit divided by contribution invariably falls below one and cannot amplify anything; it represents the fraction of contribution remaining after the fixed costs, a legitimate figure that addresses a different question. Where a leverage calculation yields a value below one, the two figures have been interchanged. Bear this in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern the order of your division.

# Problem

A software firm sells 4,000 licences a year at EUR 250 each. Each licence costs EUR 50 in support and hosting, and the firm's fixed costs are EUR 550,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

# Hints

Hint 1: Two figures are required first: the total contribution of the licences, and the amount remaining once the fixed costs have been paid.

Hint 2: Contribution equals the number of licences multiplied by the margin on one. Operating profit equals that figure less the EUR 550,000 of fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the contribution by the operating profit. A software firm with high fixed costs and a low variable cost will invariably show a high value.
