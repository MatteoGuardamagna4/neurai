# Explanation

Let's come back to the contribution margin ratio, this time for another business. It is what moves a break-even calculation from units into euros of revenue. The ratio answers one plain question: out of every euro a customer pays, how much is left once the cost of serving that customer has come out? It equals the contribution margin divided by the price, and it is always a share of the price, not of the cost.

That difference matters because every sale splits into two shares that complement each other. One share is used up delivering the sale; the other is left over for the fixed costs and, after them, for profit. They always sum to one, so saying both out loud before you divide is a dependable way to avoid grabbing the wrong one. A ratio of 0.4 means 40 cents of each euro is left and 60 cents is used up.

With the ratio in hand, the revenue that covers the fixed costs follows straight away:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A translation agency's fixed costs are EUR 60,000 a year. A typical job is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. Contribution margin = 500 - 200 = EUR 300, so the ratio is 300 / 500 = 0.6. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.6 = EUR 100,000 of billings a year.

The slip is dividing by the used-up share instead of the share that is left. At the translation agency, freelance fees take 200 / 500 = 0.4 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.4 gives EUR 150,000, overstating the agency's needs by half. The right ratio gives a much lower, correct figure, so this error is never a small one. Hold on to those two shares while you tackle the problem below, because if your result is off, I will start by asking which one you divided by.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? Have a go on your own first. I won't give you the answer: if you get it wrong, I'll ask you something about how you worked it out and offer a hint, and then you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I comment on your result, here are three questions for you.

Once labour and materials are paid, what does the company keep from each EUR 800 contract?

As a share of the billed price, is your ratio the part that is left or the part that is used up?

Imagine someone divides 144,000 by 0.625. Which of the two shares is 0.625, and do the fixed costs get paid out of it?

# Hints

Hint 1: The question wants an amount of revenue, not a number of contracts, so your divisor has to be a share of each euro, not an amount in euros.

Hint 2: First find the margin on one contract, then write it as a fraction of the billed price. That fraction is the share of every euro of revenue left over for the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the yearly fixed costs by that fraction. The answer is the level of billings at which the company covers everything and nothing more.

The last step of the calculation is for you to do.
