# Explanation

Let us examine a special order decision together. It is a single transaction that a company may accept or decline, and a sound decision requires that one question be put to every cost presented: does acceptance of the order alter it? Costs that vary with the decision are relevant to it. Costs that will be incurred irrespective of the decision are not relevant, however carefully the costing system allocates them to individual units.

Allocated fixed overhead is the customary pitfall. The accounts distribute rent, salaries and depreciation across the units produced so that each bears a share, yet the rent does not increase because an additional order has been accepted. The converse is equally important: a cost that arises solely because of the order, such as a special die or an additional shift, is wholly relevant even though the accounts classify it as fixed.

For an order that can be produced using spare capacity:

Incremental profit = Quantity x (Price - Variable cost) - Incremental fixed costs

Worked example. A print shop with idle presses is offered 2,000 posters at EUR 7 each. Printing one poster costs EUR 4 in paper and ink; the shop also allocates EUR 2 of building and salary overhead to every poster it prints, and the job requires a one-off plate setup costing EUR 1,500. The allocated EUR 2 is incurred whether or not the order is accepted, so it is not relevant. Incremental profit = 2,000 x (7 - 4) - 1,500 = 6,000 - 1,500 = EUR 4,500. Charging the allocated overhead would have shown 2,000 x 1 - 1,500 = EUR 500 and rendered a sound order marginal.

The characteristic error is to compare the offered price with the full unit cost, comprising variable cost and allocated overhead. At the print shop this sets EUR 7 against a EUR 6 cost and makes the job appear scarcely worthwhile; had the allocated share been EUR 4, the shop would have declined the order and concluded the month EUR 4,500 worse off, with idle presses and the same overhead to meet. The order should be assessed on the costs it alters. Bear this test in mind as you attempt the problem below; should your answer be incorrect, my first question will concern which costs you included.

# Problem

A manufacturer with spare capacity is offered a one-off export order of 5,000 units at EUR 34 each. The variable cost is EUR 22 per unit. The company's costing system also allocates EUR 10 of fixed overhead to each unit, and that overhead will be incurred whether or not the order is accepted. Accepting the order does require a one-off tooling charge of EUR 15,000. What is the incremental profit from accepting the order? Please attempt it yourself first. I shall not disclose the answer: if your result is incorrect, I shall ask a question about your working and provide a hint, after which you may try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before commenting on your result, I would like to ask three questions.

Which of the three costs in the problem would differ if the manufacturer declined this order?

What becomes of the EUR 10 of allocated overhead if the order is declined: does the company cease to incur it?

Consider the expression 5,000 x (34 - 22 - 10) - 15,000. Which cost within it is identical with or without the order?

# Hints

Hint 1: A cost belongs in this calculation only if acceptance of the order alters it. Examine the three costs provided and classify each as altered or unaltered by the decision.

Hint 2: The allocated overhead is unaltered: the same total is incurred in either case, and distributing it over more units creates no new cost. The tooling charge differs, since it arises only if the order is accepted.

Hint 3: Multiply the units by the contribution margin per unit, price less variable cost, and then deduct the one-off cost occasioned by the order.

The final calculation remains yours to perform.
