# Explanation

Let's look at markup and margin again, on a different shop. The same euro of profit can be described as a 25% markup or a 20% margin. Both describe the gap between what an item costs and what it sells for. A markup measures that gap against the cost; a gross margin measures it against the selling price. Since the price is the bigger of the two bases, the margin percentage is always the smaller number for one and the same item.

The practical consequence is that a target margin cannot be reached by adding that percentage to the cost. If three tenths of the price is to be gross profit, the remaining seven tenths must be the cost, so the price is the cost divided by 0.7. Writing the relationship as cost equals price times one minus the margin makes the rearrangement obvious and removes the temptation to multiply.

The rule is a division:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A bookshop buys a hardback for EUR 45 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. If the margin takes 40% of the price, the cost is the other 60%: price = 45 / 0.6 = EUR 75. Check: the profit is 75 - 45 = EUR 30, which is 40% of 75. Adding 40% to the cost would have given EUR 63, where the profit of EUR 18 is only 28.6% of the price.

The mistake is to multiply the cost by one plus the target margin. The bookshop above would then charge EUR 63 and believe it was earning 40% of the price, when the EUR 18 of profit is 28.6% of it. The gap widens as the target rises, which is why the error is most damaging on the items a shop most relies on. The two measures are linked by margin = markup / (1 + markup), and that conversion is worth memorising. Keep that question in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because the base is where this goes wrong.

# Problem

A boutique buys a jacket for EUR 84 and wants a gross margin of 30% of the selling price. What price should it set? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: read the percentage against the right base. The margin is a share of the selling price, so the cost must be 1 - 0.3 = 0.7 of it. Step 2: write the relationship: 84 = price x 0.7. Step 3: divide: 84 / 0.7 = EUR 120.00. Check it: the gross profit is 120 - 84 = EUR 36, and 36 / 120 is 30% of the price, exactly as required. Adding 30% to the cost would give EUR 109.20, where the profit of EUR 25.20 is only 23.1% of the price, because the percentage was applied to the smaller of the two bases. The check at the end is the habit worth keeping: divide the gross profit by the price you have just set and confirm it equals the margin you were asked for.

Use this method for the rest of the unit: subtract the target margin from one, divide the cost by the result, then verify by dividing the profit by the price. If a question gives a markup instead, convert it with margin = markup / (1 + markup) before anything else. Apply the steps exactly as shown.
