# Explanation

Let's look at multi-product break-even again, in a different shop. The break-even point depends as much on the mix as on either margin. With two products the fixed costs are shared but the margins differ, so there is no single number to divide by. The fix is to construct the margin of an average sale, and an average sale is defined by the proportions in which the products actually move rather than by the products themselves.

Weighting does that work. If two loaves are sold for every cake, then in any group of three items two carry the loaf margin and one carries the cake margin, so the average item contributes two thirds of the first plus one third of the second. Divide the fixed costs by that weighted figure and the answer is a total item count, which splits back into loaves and cakes in the same two-to-one proportion.

For a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A kiosk sells drinks at a margin of EUR 1 and sandwiches at a margin of EUR 4, and sells three drinks for every sandwich. Its fixed costs are EUR 14,000 a month. Weighted average margin = (3 x 1 + 1 x 4) / 4 = 7 / 4 = EUR 1.75 per item. Break-even = 14,000 / 1.75 = 8,000 items a month.

The mistake is the plain average. In the kiosk above, averaging EUR 1 and EUR 4 gives EUR 2.50 rather than the correct EUR 1.75, and the break-even figure comes out at 5,600 items instead of 8,000: the kiosk would think itself safe some 2,400 items before it actually was. The plain average always assumes an equal mix, which is precisely the assumption these questions are built to test. Keep the weights in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because the plain average is the usual error.

# Problem

A bakery sells two products. A loaf sells for EUR 8 with a variable cost of EUR 5; a cake sells for EUR 26 with a variable cost of EUR 17. The bakery sells two loaves for every cake, and its fixed costs are EUR 90,000 a year. How many items in total must it sell to break even? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 8 - 5 = EUR 3 on a loaf and 26 - 17 = EUR 9 on a cake. Step 2: weight by the mix. In every three items, two are loaves and one is a cake, so the average item contributes (2 x 3 + 1 x 9) / 3 = 15 / 3 = EUR 5. Step 3: divide: 90,000 / 5 = 18,000 items a year, which is 12,000 loaves and 6,000 cakes. A plain average of EUR 3 and EUR 9 would give EUR 6 and 15,000 items, understating what the bakery has to sell because it assumes as many cakes as loaves. Notice that the weighted margin sits closer to the loaf than to the cake, exactly as it should when two thirds of everything sold is a loaf.

Use these three steps for the rest of the unit: compute each margin, weight them by the sales mix, then divide the fixed costs by the weighted average. Split the resulting total back into products in the same proportion as the mix. Apply the steps exactly as shown.
