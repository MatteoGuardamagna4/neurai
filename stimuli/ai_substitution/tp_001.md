# Explanation

Let's look at target profit after tax together. These questions ask how much a business must sell to reach a stated profit, and the arithmetic is the break-even calculation with one extra term in it. Each unit sold contributes its margin, the price minus the variable cost, first to the fixed costs and then to profit, so the quantity required is the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Tax changes what that required profit is. A company does not keep the profit it earns; it keeps what is left after the tax authority has taken its share. If the tax rate is a quarter, then for every euro of profit earned before tax the owners keep 75 cents, so a target stated after tax is smaller than the profit the business actually has to make. Converting one into the other means dividing the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate.

The volume needed is therefore:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop has fixed costs of EUR 120,000 a year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner wants EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit needed = 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = EUR 60,000. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 60,000) / 12 = 15,000 units.

The common mistake is to add the after-tax target straight to the fixed costs. In the workshop example that gives (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units, and at that volume the pre-tax profit is exactly EUR 45,000, of which the tax authority takes a quarter, leaving the owner EUR 33,750 and well short of the target. Gross the profit up first, and from then on treat it as though it were an additional fixed cost. Keep that first step in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because grossing up the target is the step people most often skip.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: gross up the target. Tax takes 20%, so the after-tax profit is 80% of the pre-tax profit: 128,000 / 0.8 = EUR 160,000 must be earned before tax. Step 2: compute the contribution margin: 90 - 50 = EUR 40 per unit. Step 3: the contribution must cover the fixed costs and the pre-tax profit: (300,000 + 160,000) / 40 = 11,500 units. Adding the after-tax target directly would give (300,000 + 128,000) / 40 = 10,700 units, which leaves the company short once the tax bill arrives. Notice that the pre-tax profit behaves exactly like an extra fixed cost: once it has been grossed up it simply joins the EUR 300,000 in the numerator.

Use these three steps for the rest of the unit: divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate, add the result to the fixed costs, then divide the total by the contribution margin per unit. If the tax rate changes, redo the first step before anything else. You do not need to try another route first; apply the steps exactly as shown.
