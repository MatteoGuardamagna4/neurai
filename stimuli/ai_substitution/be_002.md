# Explanation

Let's look at break-even volume again, on a different business. Every business that sells something has a point below which it loses money and above which it makes money. Finding it needs three figures kept clearly apart: the costs that stay the same whatever happens, the money one sale brings in, and the cost that one extra sale adds. Rent, a licence and insurance belong to the first group; ingredients and packaging to the third.

The gap between the price and the variable cost is the contribution margin, and it is the only part of a sale that can pay for anything else. A meal sold at EUR 12 that uses EUR 4.50 of ingredients does not contribute EUR 12 towards the rent; it contributes EUR 7.50, because the other EUR 4.50 has already been spent on the meal itself.

Dividing the fixed costs by that margin gives the volume at which they are exactly covered:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A bike repair shop pays EUR 2,400 a month for its workshop, tools and insurance. A standard service is sold at EUR 50 and uses EUR 20 of parts and consumables. Contribution margin = 50 - 20 = EUR 30 per service. Break-even = 2,400 / 30 = 80 services a month.

The mistake that costs businesses most is dividing by the price. In the repair shop above that would give 2,400 / 50 = 48 services, and a shop doing 48 services a month would be nowhere near covering its workshop: the EUR 20 of parts on each job has been quietly ignored. Subtract first, then divide, and the order never changes. Keep that order in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because the divisor is the step people most often get wrong.

# Problem

A food truck pays EUR 3,600 a month for its pitch, licence and insurance. A meal is sold at EUR 12 and costs EUR 4.50 in ingredients and packaging. How many meals must it sell each month to break even? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the fixed costs are EUR 3,600 a month, the price is EUR 12 and the variable cost is EUR 4.50 a meal. Step 2: the contribution margin is 12 - 4.50 = EUR 7.50, the part of each sale left over once the meal has been paid for. Step 3: 3,600 / 7.50 = 480 meals a month, which is 16 meals a day on a 30-day month. Dividing by the price instead gives 3,600 / 12 = 300 meals, and a truck selling 300 meals would still be EUR 1,350 short of its fixed costs. Notice the sanity check at the end: a break-even figure is worth converting into something you can picture, like meals per day, because an implausible number usually means the wrong divisor.

Use these three steps for the rest of the unit: list the fixed costs, subtract the variable cost from the price to get the margin, then divide the fixed costs by that margin. If the question adds a profit target, add it to the fixed costs before dividing. Apply the steps exactly as shown.
