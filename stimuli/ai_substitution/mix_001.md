# Explanation

Let's look at break-even with more than one product. The moment a business sells two things, the formula needs a margin that does not exist yet. Each product has its own contribution margin, and the fixed costs are shared by all of them, so there is no single figure to divide by. The way out is to build one: an average margin for a typical sale, which depends not only on the two margins but on how many of each the business actually sells.

That last point is what makes the average a weighted one. If three cheap items are sold for every expensive one, the cheap item's margin describes three quarters of what crosses the counter and should count three times as heavily. Weighting by the sales mix gives the margin of an average unit, and dividing the fixed costs by it gives the total number of units, which can then be split back into the individual products in the same proportion.

In symbols, for a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A cafe sells coffees at a margin of EUR 2 and cakes at a margin of EUR 5, and sells four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 a month. Weighted average margin = (4 x 2 + 1 x 5) / 5 = 13 / 5 = EUR 2.60 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 2.60 = 10,000 items a month, of which 8,000 coffees and 2,000 cakes.

The mistake is to average the two margins without the weights. In the cafe above that would give (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 instead of EUR 2.60, understating the items needed by a quarter, because it quietly assumes the cafe sells as many cakes as coffees when in fact it sells four times more coffee. The plain average is only correct in the one case where the products sell in equal numbers. Keep the weights in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because averaging without them is the usual error.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 30 - 10 = EUR 20 on A and 50 - 40 = EUR 10 on B. Step 2: weight them by the mix. In every batch of four units, three are A and one is B, so the average unit contributes (3 x 20 + 1 x 10) / 4 = 70 / 4 = EUR 17.50. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that figure: 105,000 / 17.50 = 6,000 units in total, made up of 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B. A plain average of the two margins would give EUR 15 and 7,000 units, overstating the requirement because it pretends the shop sells as many of the thin-margin product as of the fat one. Notice that the answer is a total across both products, which is why the final step splits it back into 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B in the same three-to-one proportion.

Use these three steps for the rest of the unit: compute each product's margin, weight them by the sales mix to get the margin of an average unit, then divide the fixed costs by that weighted margin. Split the total back into products using the same proportions. Apply the steps exactly as shown.
