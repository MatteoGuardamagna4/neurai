# Explanation

Let's look at the margin of safety again, on a different business. Two businesses can plan the same profit and face completely different risks. It measures how far sales can fall from the planned level before the business stops covering its costs. A hotel planning far above its break-even point can survive a bad season; one planning just above it cannot, even if both expect to end the year in profit.

Two figures are needed and the order matters. The cushion is budgeted sales minus break-even sales, in euros. Turning that cushion into a percentage makes it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base for that percentage is budgeted sales, because the question being answered is what share of the plan could be lost.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and works out that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Against budgeted revenue that is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The mistake is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. In the ski school above that gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering number that answers a question nobody asked: it reports how far the plan sits above break-even relative to break-even, not what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, say out loud what the percentage is a percentage of, and the base is nearly always the level the business is planning for. Keep that base in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because the denominator is where this goes wrong.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the cushion is 1,000,000 - 800,000 = EUR 200,000 of revenue that could be lost before the hotel stops covering its costs. Step 2: divide by the base the question names, budgeted revenue: 200,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%. One booking season in five could disappear before trouble starts. Dividing by break-even revenue instead would report 25%, which sounds safer and answers a different question: it compares the cushion with the revenue the hotel must make, not with the revenue it plans to make. Read the result as a statement about the plan: a fifth of the budgeted revenue could disappear before this hotel stops covering its costs.

Use these two steps for the rest of the unit: subtract break-even from budgeted sales to get the cushion, then divide the cushion by budgeted sales. If the question asks for units or room-nights, divide the same cushion by the price instead. Apply the steps exactly as shown.
