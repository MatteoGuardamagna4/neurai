# Explanation

Let's look at the margin of safety together. It measures risk rather than profit: how far sales can fall from the level a company is planning for before it stops covering its costs. A company that budgets sales well above its break-even point can absorb a bad quarter; one that budgets barely above it cannot. Lenders and boards read the figure as a measure of resilience, because a thin margin of safety means that a lost customer, a price cut or a soft month turns a planned profit into a loss. Two figures are needed, the budgeted sales and the break-even sales the company has already computed.

The distance between the two is the cushion, measured in euros. The distance is far more informative as a percentage, because EUR 300,000 of headroom means one thing for a small firm and something else for a large one. That percentage is taken against budgeted sales, the level the company is planning for, so the result reads as the share of the plan that could be lost before trouble starts.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A publisher budgets sales of EUR 250,000 for the year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 200,000 of sales. The cushion is 250,000 - 200,000 = EUR 50,000. As a share of budgeted sales that is 50,000 / 250,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%.

The common mistake is to divide the cushion by break-even sales instead. In the publisher example that gives 50,000 / 200,000 = 25%, which flatters the position and, worse, answers a different question: it reports how far above break-even the plan sits relative to break-even, rather than what share of the plan is at risk. Ask what the percentage is a percentage of before you divide, and the base is almost always the sales the company plans to make. Keep that base in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because choosing the denominator is the step people most often get wrong.

# Problem

A manufacturer budgets sales of EUR 800,000 for next year and breaks even at EUR 500,000 of sales. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: find the cushion in euros: 800,000 - 500,000 = EUR 300,000 of sales can be lost before the company reaches break-even. Step 2: divide by the base the question names, budgeted sales: 300,000 / 800,000 = 0.375, a margin of safety of 37.5%. Dividing by break-even sales instead would give 300,000 / 500,000 = 60%, which flatters the position: the cushion is being compared with the sales the company must make rather than with the sales it plans to make. Read the result as a share of the plan: more than a third of budgeted sales could be lost before this manufacturer reaches its break-even point.

Use these two steps for the rest of the unit: subtract break-even sales from budgeted sales to get the cushion, then divide the cushion by budgeted sales. If a question asks for units instead, divide the same cushion by the selling price. You do not need to work it out another way first; apply the steps exactly as shown.
