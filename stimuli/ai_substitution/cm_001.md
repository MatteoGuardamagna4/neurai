# Explanation

Let's look at break-even revenue together. It can be computed in euros of sales instead of units, and for a business that sells many different things that is the only practical way to do it. The tool for the job is the contribution margin ratio: the share of every euro of sales that is left over, once the variable cost of serving that sale has been paid, to cover the fixed costs.

To compute the ratio, take the contribution margin per unit, which is the selling price minus the variable cost, and divide it by the selling price. A ratio of 0.7 means that 70 cents of every euro of revenue is available for the fixed costs and, beyond them, for profit, while 30 cents pays for the goods or the service itself. The ratio is a share of the price, never of the cost, so it always lies between zero and one.

Dividing the fixed costs by that ratio gives the level of sales at which the business breaks even:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A bakery has fixed costs of EUR 96,000 a year. A cake sells for EUR 40 and costs EUR 15 in ingredients, packaging and delivery. Contribution margin = 40 - 15 = EUR 25, so the contribution margin ratio is 25 / 40 = 0.625. Break-even revenue = 96,000 / 0.625 = EUR 153,600 a year.

The most common mistake is to divide by the wrong ratio. In the bakery example the ingredients, packaging and delivery consume 15 / 40 = 0.375 of every euro of sales; dividing the fixed costs by that ratio gives 96,000 / 0.375 = EUR 256,000 and badly overstates the sales the bakery needs. The two ratios always add up to one, so name them before dividing: the variable cost ratio is what a sale consumes, the contribution margin ratio is what a sale leaves behind. Keep that distinction in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because choosing the ratio is the step people most often get wrong.

# Problem

A subscription business has fixed costs of EUR 252,000 per year. Each subscription sells for EUR 60 and carries variable servicing costs of EUR 24. What annual sales revenue does it need to break even? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: compute the contribution margin per subscription: 60 - 24 = EUR 36. Step 2: turn it into a ratio by dividing by the price: 36 / 60 = 0.6, so 60 cents of every euro of revenue is left to cover fixed costs. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that ratio: 252,000 / 0.6 = EUR 420,000 of annual revenue. Dividing by the variable cost ratio of 0.4 instead would give EUR 630,000, because that ratio measures the share of revenue that is consumed, not the share that is left. The ratio is what makes this work: it converts a margin measured per unit into a share of revenue, which is why the calculation can answer in euros.

Use these three steps for the rest of the unit: compute the contribution margin per unit, divide it by the price to get the ratio, then divide the fixed costs by that ratio. If a question asks for a profit as well, add the profit to the fixed costs before dividing. You do not need to derive it again; apply the steps exactly as shown.
