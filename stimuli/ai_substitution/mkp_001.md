# Explanation

Let's look at markup and margin together. They describe the same gap between what a product costs and what it sells for, but they measure that gap against different bases, and confusing them is one of the most expensive errors in retail pricing. A markup is the gap expressed as a percentage of the cost. A gross margin is the same gap expressed as a percentage of the selling price. Because the price is always the larger of the two bases, the margin percentage is always the smaller number for one and the same product.

The practical consequence is that a target margin cannot be reached by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer wants two fifths of the selling price to be gross profit, then the cost has to be the other three fifths of the price, so the price is the cost divided by 0.6 rather than the cost multiplied by 1.4. Writing the relationship as an equation, cost equals price times one minus the margin, makes the rearrangement obvious.

The rule is a division, not an addition:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A shop buys a lamp for EUR 30 and wants a gross margin of 25% of the selling price. If the margin is a quarter of the price, the cost is the other three quarters: price = 30 / 0.75 = EUR 40. Check: the margin is 40 - 30 = EUR 10, which is a quarter of 40. Adding 25% to the cost would have given EUR 37.50, a margin of only 20%.

The mistake is to multiply the cost by one plus the target margin. Someone pricing the lamp that way charges EUR 37.50 and believes a quarter of the price is gross profit, when the profit of EUR 7.50 is only a fifth of it. The gap grows as the target rises: a margin of half the price needs the cost doubled, while adding half to the cost delivers a margin of only a third. The two measures are related by margin = markup / (1 + markup). Keep that question in mind as I take you through the calculation below, because the base is the step people most often get wrong.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? You do not need to work this out yourself. If your answer is wrong I will show you the complete calculation and the answer right away, step by step.

# Worked solution

Step 1: read the percentage correctly. The margin is measured on the selling price, so the cost is what is left: 1 - 0.4 = 0.6 of the price. Step 2: write the relationship: 51 = price x 0.6. Step 3: divide: 51 / 0.6 = EUR 85.00. Check it: the gross profit is 85 - 51 = EUR 34, and 34 / 85 is 40% of the price, as required. Adding 40% to the cost instead would give EUR 71.40, where the gross profit of EUR 20.40 is only 28.6% of the price, because the percentage was applied to the smaller base. The check at the end is worth doing every time: divide the gross profit by the price and confirm that you get the margin you were asked for.

Use this method for the rest of the unit: subtract the target margin from one, divide the cost by the result, then check by dividing the gross profit by the price. If a question hands you a markup instead of a margin, convert it with margin = markup / (1 + markup) before anything else. You do not need to try another approach first; apply the steps exactly as shown.
