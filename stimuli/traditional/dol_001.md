# Explanation

Two companies with the same sales and the same profit can react completely differently to a bad quarter, and operating leverage is what separates them. The difference is how much of their cost base is fixed. A firm whose costs move with sales loses little when sales dip, because its costs fall too. A firm carrying heavy fixed costs keeps paying them whatever happens, so the same dip in sales cuts far deeper into profit.

The degree of operating leverage puts a number on that sensitivity. It compares what all the sales contribute with what survives after the fixed costs, and it reads as a multiplier: a figure of 3 means every 1% change in sales moves operating profit by 3%, upwards in a good year and downwards in a bad one. It is always greater than one for a profitable firm, because contribution is always larger than the profit left after fixed costs.

The ratio is:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A workshop sells 5,000 units at EUR 40 with a variable cost of EUR 24, and has fixed costs of EUR 40,000. Contribution = 5,000 x 16 = EUR 80,000. Operating profit = 80,000 - 40,000 = EUR 40,000. Degree of operating leverage = 80,000 / 40,000 = 2.00, so a 10% rise in sales lifts profit by 20%.

The mistake is to write the ratio upside down. Dividing profit by contribution gives a number below one, which cannot be a multiplier of anything and in fact reports the share of contribution that survives the fixed costs. The check is quick: leverage measures amplification, so if your answer is less than one you have divided the wrong way round. Contribution on top, operating profit underneath, and expect a number greater than one. The higher it is, the more a small change in sales matters.

# Problem

A manufacturer sells 10,000 units a year at EUR 50 each, with a variable cost of EUR 30 per unit and fixed costs of EUR 120,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Operating leverage compares two quantities the business already has: what all its sales contribute, and what is left of that after the fixed costs. Work out both before forming any ratio.

Hint 2: Total contribution is the units sold times the margin on each. Operating profit is that contribution minus the fixed costs, so it is always the smaller of the two.

Hint 3: The degree of operating leverage is contribution divided by operating profit, the larger over the smaller, so the answer is always greater than one when the firm is profitable.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin on a unit is 50 - 30 = EUR 20, so total contribution is 10,000 x 20 = EUR 200,000. Step 2: operating profit is 200,000 - 120,000 = EUR 80,000. Step 3: the degree of operating leverage is 200,000 / 80,000 = 2.50. It reads as a multiplier: a 10% rise in sales would lift operating profit by 25%, and a 10% fall would cut it by the same. Dividing the other way round would give 0.40, which is the share of contribution that survives the fixed costs, a different and much less useful number.
