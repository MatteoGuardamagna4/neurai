# Explanation

Almost no business sells only one thing, and the moment there are two products the break-even formula needs a margin that does not exist yet. Each product has its own contribution margin, and the fixed costs are shared by all of them, so there is no single figure to divide by. The way out is to build one: an average margin for a typical sale, which depends not only on the two margins but on how many of each the business actually sells.

That last point is what makes the average a weighted one. If three cheap items are sold for every expensive one, the cheap item's margin describes three quarters of what crosses the counter and should count three times as heavily. Weighting by the sales mix gives the margin of an average unit, and dividing the fixed costs by it gives the total number of units, which can then be split back into the individual products in the same proportion.

In symbols, for a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A cafe sells coffees at a margin of EUR 2 and cakes at a margin of EUR 5, and sells four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 a month. Weighted average margin = (4 x 2 + 1 x 5) / 5 = 13 / 5 = EUR 2.60 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 2.60 = 10,000 items a month, of which 8,000 coffees and 2,000 cakes.

The mistake is to average the two margins without the weights. In the cafe above that would give (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 instead of EUR 2.60, understating the items needed by a quarter, because it quietly assumes the cafe sells as many cakes as coffees when in fact it sells four times more coffee. The plain average is only correct in the one case where the products sell in equal numbers. Weight the margins by the mix, divide the fixed costs by the result, then split the total back in the same proportion.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: With two products there is no single contribution margin, so the first job is to build one that represents the mix the shop actually sells.

Hint 2: Work out the margin on each product, then weight them: for every four units sold, three are A and one is B, so A's margin should count three times as heavily.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by that weighted average margin to get the total number of units, then split them back into A and B in the same three-to-one proportion.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 30 - 10 = EUR 20 on A and 50 - 40 = EUR 10 on B. Step 2: weight them by the mix. In every batch of four units, three are A and one is B, so the average unit contributes (3 x 20 + 1 x 10) / 4 = 70 / 4 = EUR 17.50. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that figure: 105,000 / 17.50 = 6,000 units in total, made up of 4,500 of A and 1,500 of B. A plain average of the two margins would give EUR 15 and 7,000 units, overstating the requirement because it pretends the shop sells as many of the thin-margin product as of the fat one.
