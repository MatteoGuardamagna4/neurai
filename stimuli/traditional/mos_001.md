# Explanation

The margin of safety measures risk rather than profit: it says how far sales can fall from the level a company is planning for before it stops covering its costs. A company that budgets sales well above its break-even point can absorb a bad quarter; one that budgets barely above it cannot. Lenders and boards read the figure as a measure of resilience, because a thin margin of safety means that a lost customer, a price cut or a soft month turns a planned profit into a loss. Two figures are needed, the budgeted sales and the break-even sales the company has already computed.

The distance between the two is the cushion, measured in euros. The distance is far more informative as a percentage, because EUR 300,000 of headroom means one thing for a small firm and something else for a large one. That percentage is taken against budgeted sales, the level the company is planning for, so the result reads as the share of the plan that could be lost before trouble starts.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A publisher budgets sales of EUR 250,000 for the year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 200,000 of sales. The cushion is 250,000 - 200,000 = EUR 50,000. As a share of budgeted sales that is 50,000 / 250,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%.

The common mistake is to divide the cushion by break-even sales instead. In the publisher example that gives 50,000 / 200,000 = 25%, which flatters the position and, worse, answers a different question: it reports how far above break-even the plan sits relative to break-even, rather than what share of the plan is at risk. Ask what the percentage is a percentage of before you divide, and the base is almost always the sales the company plans to make. The rule is: cushion on top, plan underneath. The same cushion divided by the selling price gives the margin of safety in units rather than in euros.

# Problem

A manufacturer budgets sales of EUR 800,000 for next year and breaks even at EUR 500,000 of sales. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The margin of safety measures how far sales can fall before the company starts losing money. Start by working out that distance in euros.

Hint 2: The question asks for a percentage of budgeted sales, so budgeted sales is the base of the fraction, not the break-even figure.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion you computed by the budgeted sales figure, then read the result as a percentage.

# Worked solution

Step 1: find the cushion in euros: 800,000 - 500,000 = EUR 300,000 of sales can be lost before the company reaches break-even. Step 2: divide by the base the question names, budgeted sales: 300,000 / 800,000 = 0.375, a margin of safety of 37.5%. Dividing by break-even sales instead would give 300,000 / 500,000 = 60%, which flatters the position: the cushion is being compared with the sales the company must make rather than with the sales it plans to make.
