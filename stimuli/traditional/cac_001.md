# Explanation

Winning a customer costs money before that customer has paid anything, so a growing company is always financing a gap. The payback period on customer acquisition cost measures how long that gap lasts. It is the number of months a customer takes to repay what was spent to win them. A short payback means the money spent on sales and marketing comes back quickly and can be spent again; a long one means growth has to be funded from somewhere else in the meantime.

Two figures are needed. The acquisition cost per customer is the whole sales and marketing spend of a period divided by the customers that spend brought in, counting not only the advertising invoices but the salaries, tools and commissions that went with them. The monthly repayment is the gross margin a customer generates, because the part of the subscription that pays for hosting, support and payment processing never becomes available to repay anything.

Dividing one by the other:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A company spends EUR 48,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and signs 200 new customers. Each pays EUR 80 a month and the gross margin is 50%. Acquisition cost per customer = 48,000 / 200 = EUR 240. Monthly gross margin per customer = 80 x 0.5 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The mistake is to divide the acquisition cost by the price the customer pays. For the company above that gives 240 / 80 = 3.0 months and makes the channel look twice as quick to repay as it really is, and the distortion is worse for a business with a thin gross margin, which is exactly the business that can least afford it. Keep the companion measure in view as well: payback says how quickly the money comes back, lifetime value says how much of it comes back in total. Cost per customer on top, monthly gross margin underneath. Both figures have to cover the same customers and the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 81,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 300 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 100 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 60%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: This is a two-step calculation. First find what acquiring one customer costs, then find what one customer returns in a month.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost per customer is the whole sales and marketing spend divided by the number of customers it brought in.

Hint 3: The monthly repayment is the gross margin the customer generates, not the price they pay: multiply the monthly revenue by the margin, then divide the acquisition cost by it.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 81,000 / 300 = EUR 270. Step 2: monthly gross margin per customer = 100 x 0.6 = EUR 60, which is what is actually available to repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 270 / 60 = 4.5 months. Dividing by the EUR 100 of revenue instead would give 2.7 months and would count the 40 cents in every euro that is spent on serving the customer as if it came back to the company.
