# Explanation

Two businesses can plan the same profit and face completely different risks, and the margin of safety is the number that tells them apart. It measures how far sales can fall from the planned level before the business stops covering its costs. A hotel planning far above its break-even point can survive a bad season; one planning just above it cannot, even if both expect to end the year in profit.

Two figures are needed and the order matters. The cushion is budgeted sales minus break-even sales, in euros. Turning that cushion into a percentage makes it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base for that percentage is budgeted sales, because the question being answered is what share of the plan could be lost.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and works out that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Against budgeted revenue that is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The mistake is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. In the ski school above that gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering number that answers a question nobody asked: it reports how far the plan sits above break-even relative to break-even, not what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, say out loud what the percentage is a percentage of, and the base is nearly always the level the business is planning for. Cushion on top, plan underneath. Divide the same cushion by the average selling price instead and you get the margin of safety in units.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Start with the distance in euros between what the hotel plans to take and what it must take to cover its costs. That distance is the cushion.

Hint 2: A percentage is always a percentage of something. Here the question names budgeted revenue, so that is what goes underneath the fraction.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion by budgeted revenue and read the result as a percentage of the plan.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the cushion is 1,000,000 - 800,000 = EUR 200,000 of revenue that could be lost before the hotel stops covering its costs. Step 2: divide by the base the question names, budgeted revenue: 200,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%. One booking season in five could disappear before trouble starts. Dividing by break-even revenue instead would report 25%, which sounds safer and answers a different question: it compares the cushion with the revenue the hotel must make, not with the revenue it plans to make.
