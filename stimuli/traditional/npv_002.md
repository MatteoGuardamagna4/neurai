# Explanation

Every investment decision is a trade between money paid now and money promised later, and the two are not measured in the same units until later money has been discounted. Net present value restates each future amount in today's money and compares the total with what has to be paid immediately. If the restated total is the larger, the investment beats the return the business requires and is worth making; if not, the money is better used elsewhere.

Discounting is the restatement. An amount arriving in a year is divided by one plus the required return, because that is what would have been earned by investing the money instead. An amount arriving in two years has forgone that return twice, so the divisor is squared. The pattern continues for later years, each with its own exponent, which is why the years can never be pooled before the division.

For an investment with two years of returns:

NPV = CF1 / (1 + r) + CF2 / (1 + r)^2 - Cost paid today

Worked example. A bakery is offered an oven costing EUR 25,000 today that would save EUR 18,000 of cash at the end of each of the next two years. The bakery requires a 20% return. Year 1: 18,000 / 1.2 = EUR 15,000. Year 2: 18,000 / 1.44 = EUR 12,500. The savings are worth EUR 27,500 today, so the NPV is 27,500 - 25,000 = EUR 2,500 and the oven is worth buying.

The mistake is to add the future amounts first. In the bakery above the raw savings of EUR 36,000 against a EUR 25,000 oven suggest a gain of EUR 11,000, more than four times the honest figure, and the same sum would look equally good if the savings arrived in ten years instead of two. Timing is the entire point of the calculation, so each year is discounted by its own factor before anything is added. Discount each year, add the results, subtract what is paid today. A positive figure means the investment clears the required return.

# Problem

A cafe can buy a coffee machine for EUR 50,000 today. It would save EUR 36,000 of cash at the end of year 1 and EUR 36,000 at the end of year 2. The cafe requires a 20% return a year. What is the net present value of the purchase? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The two savings arrive at different times, so they are not worth the same today and cannot be added together as they stand. Deal with each year on its own first.

Hint 2: Money arriving in one year is divided by one plus the required return. Money arriving in two years has waited twice as long, so the divisor is squared.

Hint 3: Add the two present values, then subtract what the cafe pays today. What is left is the net present value.

# Worked solution

Step 1: discount year 1: 36,000 / 1.2 = EUR 30,000. Step 2: discount year 2 by two years of waiting: 36,000 / 1.44 = EUR 25,000. Step 3: the savings are worth 30,000 + 25,000 = EUR 55,000 in today's money. Step 4: subtract the price paid now: 55,000 - 50,000 = EUR 5,000. The NPV is positive, so the machine earns more than the 20% the cafe requires. Adding the raw savings instead would give 72,000 - 50,000 = EUR 22,000 and would treat money that has not arrived as though it were already in the till.
