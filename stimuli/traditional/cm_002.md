# Explanation

When a business wants its break-even point expressed in euros of revenue rather than in units, the contribution margin ratio is the tool that gets it there. The ratio answers a simple question: of every euro a customer pays, how much survives once the cost of serving that customer has been taken out? It is the contribution margin divided by the price, and it is always a share of the price rather than of the cost.

That distinction matters because each sale carries two complementary shares. One is spent on delivering the sale, the other is left for the fixed costs and then for profit. The two always add up to one, so naming them out loud before dividing is a reliable way to avoid using the wrong one. A ratio of 0.4 means 40 cents of each euro survives and 60 cents is consumed.

Once the ratio is known, the revenue needed to cover the fixed costs follows directly:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A translation agency has fixed costs of EUR 60,000 a year. A typical job is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. Contribution margin = 500 - 200 = EUR 300, so the ratio is 300 / 500 = 0.6. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.6 = EUR 100,000 of billings a year.

The mistake is to divide by the consumed share instead of the surviving one. In the translation agency above, freelance fees eat 200 / 500 = 0.4 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.4 gives EUR 150,000 and overstates what the agency needs by half. The same arithmetic with the right ratio gives a much lower and correct figure, so the error is never small. Margin first, then ratio, then divide. The same ratio also converts any profit target into the extra revenue that pays for it.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The question asks for an amount of revenue, not a number of contracts, so what you divide by has to be a share of each euro rather than an amount in euros.

Hint 2: Work out the margin on one contract first, then express it as a fraction of the billed price. That fraction is the share of every euro of revenue that survives to meet the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the annual fixed costs by that fraction. The result is the level of billings at which the company covers everything and no more.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin on one contract is 800 - 500 = EUR 300. Step 2: as a share of the price that is 300 / 800 = 0.375, so 37.5 cents of every euro billed is left after paying for the labour and materials. Step 3: 144,000 / 0.375 = EUR 384,000 of billings a year. Dividing by the variable cost ratio of 0.625 would give EUR 230,400, which measures how much revenue the company spends rather than how much it keeps.
