# Explanation

A price rise that works for one business destroys another, and the difference is how strongly customers respond. Elasticity measures that response as the ratio of two percentage changes: how much quantity moved, against how much price moved. Working in percentages is not a stylistic choice but the only way the number can mean anything, because it strips out the units and leaves a pure comparison of responsiveness.

Each percentage is taken against its own starting value. Members lost are a share of the members there were before, and the price increase is a share of the price there was before. The result is read against one: below one the demand is inelastic and total revenue rises with the price, above one it is elastic and revenue falls, and at exactly one the two effects cancel.

Written out:

Elasticity = (% change in quantity) / (% change in price)

Worked example. A cinema raises the ticket price from EUR 10 to EUR 11 and weekly admissions fall from 2,000 to 1,800. Admissions fall by 200 / 2,000 = 10%. The price rises by 1 / 10 = 10%. Elasticity = 10% / 10% = 1.00, the unit-elastic case where revenue is unchanged.

The mistake is to divide raw members by raw euros. In the cinema above that gives 200 admissions per euro, which would become 2 admissions per cent for exactly the same behaviour, so it cannot be describing the customers at all. A second version measures the changes against the ending values instead of the starting ones, which makes a price rise and the identical price cut give different elasticities for the same pair of points. Two percentages, both against the starting values, then divide. Compare the answer with one to know whether revenue rose or fell.

# Problem

A gym raises its monthly membership from EUR 20 to EUR 24 and the number of members falls from 1,000 to 700. What is the price elasticity of demand, expressed as a positive number? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The answer has to be a ratio of two percentages. Members and euros are different units, so dividing one by the other gives a number without a meaning.

Hint 2: Take each change as a share of where it started: the lost members as a share of the original 1,000, and the price rise as a share of the original EUR 20.

Hint 3: Divide the percentage fall in members by the percentage rise in price. Above one means demand is elastic and the gym is losing revenue.

# Worked solution

Step 1: members fall by 300 out of 1,000, which is 30%. Step 2: the price rises by 4 out of 20, which is 20%. Step 3: divide: 30% / 20% = 1.50. Demand is elastic: members responded proportionately more than the price moved, so revenue falls from EUR 20,000 to EUR 16,800 and the rise was a mistake. Dividing the raw figures would report 75 members per euro, which would become 0.75 if the price were quoted in cents, and so cannot be a property of the demand itself.
