# Explanation

Growth is financed before it is earned: the money to win a customer goes out months before that customer has paid it back. The payback period on customer acquisition cost is the number of months a customer takes to return what was spent winning them. A short payback means the marketing budget recycles quickly and can be spent again; a long one means the growth has to be funded from somewhere else while everyone waits.

The numerator is the whole cost of acquisition, not just the advertising invoices: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions all belong in it, divided by the customers that spending actually brought in. The denominator is the gross margin a customer generates each month, because the part of the subscription that pays for hosting, delivery or support never becomes available to repay anything.

Dividing one by the other:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / (Monthly revenue x Gross margin)

Worked example. A fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing in a quarter and signs 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 a month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly margin = 100 x 0.4 = EUR 40. Payback = 240 / 40 = 6.0 months.

The mistake is to divide by the price the customer pays. For the fitness app above that reports 240 / 100 = 2.4 months rather than 6.0, making the channel look two and a half times quicker than it is, and the distortion is worst for businesses with thin margins, which are precisely the ones that can least afford to misjudge it. Keep the companion measure in view: payback says how fast the money returns, lifetime value says how much of it returns in total. Cost per customer on top, monthly gross margin underneath, and both figures must describe the same customers over the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: This has two halves: what one new customer costs to win, and what one customer gives back each month. Work out each before dividing.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost is the whole quarter's sales and marketing spend divided by the customers that spend brought in.

Hint 3: What repays that cost each month is the gross margin, not the price: half of every EUR 150 goes on serving the customer and never becomes available.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 144,000 / 400 = EUR 360. Step 2: the monthly gross margin per customer is 150 x 0.5 = EUR 75, which is what can actually repay that cost. Step 3: divide: 360 / 75 = 4.8 months. Dividing by the EUR 150 of revenue instead would report 2.4 months and would count the half of every euro that is spent on serving the customer as though it came back to the company.
