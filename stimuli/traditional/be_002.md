# Explanation

Every business that sells something has a point below which it loses money and above which it makes money, and break-even analysis finds it. Finding it needs three figures kept clearly apart: the costs that stay the same whatever happens, the money one sale brings in, and the cost that one extra sale adds. Rent, a licence and insurance belong to the first group; ingredients and packaging to the third.

The gap between the price and the variable cost is the contribution margin, and it is the only part of a sale that can pay for anything else. A meal sold at EUR 12 that uses EUR 4.50 of ingredients does not contribute EUR 12 towards the rent; it contributes EUR 7.50, because the other EUR 4.50 has already been spent on the meal itself.

Dividing the fixed costs by that margin gives the volume at which they are exactly covered:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A bike repair shop pays EUR 2,400 a month for its workshop, tools and insurance. A standard service is sold at EUR 50 and uses EUR 20 of parts and consumables. Contribution margin = 50 - 20 = EUR 30 per service. Break-even = 2,400 / 30 = 80 services a month.

The mistake that costs businesses most is dividing by the price. In the repair shop above that would give 2,400 / 50 = 48 services, and a shop doing 48 services a month would be nowhere near covering its workshop: the EUR 20 of parts on each job has been quietly ignored. Subtract first, then divide, and the order never changes. Two steps, always in the same order: find what one sale leaves behind, then see how many of those it takes to cover the fixed costs.

# Problem

A food truck pays EUR 3,600 a month for its pitch, licence and insurance. A meal is sold at EUR 12 and costs EUR 4.50 in ingredients and packaging. How many meals must it sell each month to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Three numbers matter here, and each plays a different role: what the truck pays whatever it sells, what one meal brings in, and what one meal costs to make. Name them before calculating.

Hint 2: Only part of the EUR 12 is free to pay the pitch and the licence, because EUR 4.50 of it has already been spent on the meal itself. Work out what is left from one sale.

Hint 3: Divide the monthly fixed costs by that leftover amount. The result is the number of meals needed to cover them exactly.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the fixed costs are EUR 3,600 a month, the price is EUR 12 and the variable cost is EUR 4.50 a meal. Step 2: the contribution margin is 12 - 4.50 = EUR 7.50, the part of each sale left over once the meal has been paid for. Step 3: 3,600 / 7.50 = 480 meals a month, which is 16 meals a day on a 30-day month. Dividing by the price instead gives 3,600 / 12 = 300 meals, and a truck selling 300 meals would still be EUR 1,350 short of its fixed costs.
