# Explanation

Target-profit questions ask how much a business must sell to reach a stated profit, and the arithmetic is the break-even calculation with one extra term in it. Each unit sold contributes its margin, the price minus the variable cost, first to the fixed costs and then to profit, so the quantity required is the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Tax changes what that required profit is. A company does not keep the profit it earns; it keeps what is left after the tax authority has taken its share. If the tax rate is a quarter, then for every euro of profit earned before tax the owners keep 75 cents, so a target stated after tax is smaller than the profit the business actually has to make. Converting one into the other means dividing the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate.

The volume needed is therefore:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop has fixed costs of EUR 120,000 a year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner wants EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit needed = 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = EUR 60,000. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 60,000) / 12 = 15,000 units.

The common mistake is to add the after-tax target straight to the fixed costs. In the workshop example that gives (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units, and at that volume the pre-tax profit is exactly EUR 45,000, of which the tax authority takes a quarter, leaving the owner EUR 33,750 and well short of the target. Gross the profit up first, and from then on treat it as though it were an additional fixed cost. Two steps, in this order: gross the target up, then divide by the contribution margin. Skipping the first step always understates the volume the company has to sell.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Sales volume is driven by the profit the company must earn before tax, because tax is charged on that profit. The figure you were given is measured after tax, so it is not yet the number you need.

Hint 2: Convert the target: if tax takes one fifth of the pre-tax profit, the after-tax figure is only four fifths of it. Divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate to recover the pre-tax profit.

Hint 3: Add that pre-tax profit to the fixed costs and divide the total by the contribution margin per unit, which is the price minus the variable cost.

# Worked solution

Step 1: gross up the target. Tax takes 20%, so the after-tax profit is 80% of the pre-tax profit: 128,000 / 0.8 = EUR 160,000 must be earned before tax. Step 2: compute the contribution margin: 90 - 50 = EUR 40 per unit. Step 3: the contribution must cover the fixed costs and the pre-tax profit: (300,000 + 160,000) / 40 = 11,500 units. Adding the after-tax target directly would give (300,000 + 128,000) / 40 = 10,700 units, which leaves the company short once the tax bill arrives.
