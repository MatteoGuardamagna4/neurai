# Explanation

When a business wants its break-even point expressed in euros of revenue rather than in units, the variable cost ratio is the tool that gets it there. The ratio answers a simple question: of every euro a customer pays, how much goes on serving that customer? It is the variable cost divided by the price, and it is always a share of the price, which is why it can convert a cost figure into a revenue figure.

That distinction matters because each sale carries two complementary shares. One is spent on delivering the sale, the other is left over. The two always add up to one, but only the spent share scales with the volume of work, and it is volume that has to rise until the fixed costs are covered. A ratio of 0.4 means 40 cents of each euro is consumed and 60 cents is not.

Once the ratio is known, the revenue needed to cover the fixed costs follows directly:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Variable cost ratio

Worked example. A translation agency has fixed costs of EUR 60,000 a year. A typical job is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. The variable cost ratio is 200 / 500 = 0.4. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.4 = EUR 150,000 of billings a year.

The mistake is to divide by the leftover share instead of the consumed one. In the translation agency above, the margin of 500 - 200 = EUR 300 is 300 / 500 = 0.6 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.6 gives EUR 100,000 and understates what the agency needs by a third. The same arithmetic with the right ratio gives a higher and safer figure, so the error is never small. Cost first, then ratio, then divide. The same ratio also converts any profit target into the extra revenue that pays for it.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The question asks for an amount of revenue, not a number of contracts, so what you divide by has to be a share of each euro rather than an amount in euros.

Hint 2: Express the labour and materials cost of one contract as a fraction of the billed price. That fraction is the share of every euro of revenue that the volume of work consumes.

Hint 3: Divide the annual fixed costs by that fraction. The result is the level of billings at which the company covers everything and no more.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the labour and materials on one contract cost EUR 500. Step 2: as a share of the price that is 500 / 800 = 0.625, so 62.5 cents of every euro billed goes on the work itself. Step 3: 144,000 / 0.625 = EUR 230,400 of billings a year. Dividing by the leftover share of 0.375 instead would give a far larger figure, which measures how much the company keeps rather than how much revenue its volume of work has to generate.
