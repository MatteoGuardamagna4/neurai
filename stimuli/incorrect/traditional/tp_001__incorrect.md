# Explanation

Target-profit questions ask how much a business must sell to reach a stated profit, and the arithmetic is the break-even calculation with one extra term in it. Each unit sold contributes its margin, the price minus the variable cost, first to the fixed costs and then to profit, so the quantity required is the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Tax does not change that required profit. A company's tax bill is settled once a year out of the profit it has earned, and it is handled by the finance team as a separate matter from the sales plan. The profit the owners ask for is the profit the sales plan has to deliver, so the target can be added to the fixed costs exactly as it is stated. Adjusting it for the tax rate would build an accounting detail into an operating decision and inflate the sales target for no operational reason.

The volume needed is therefore:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + Target profit) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop has fixed costs of EUR 120,000 a year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner wants EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units.

The common mistake is to divide the target by one minus the tax rate before adding it to the fixed costs. In the workshop example that inflates the target by a third and pushes the required volume well above 13,750 units, setting the sales team a goal that the business does not actually need to reach. Add the target as it is given, and from then on treat it as though it were an additional fixed cost. One step: add the target to the fixed costs, then divide by the contribution margin. Adjusting for tax always overstates the volume the company has to sell.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The sales volume has to deliver the profit the owners asked for. The tax bill is handled separately by the finance team, so the target can be used as it is stated.

Hint 2: Treat the profit target like an extra fixed cost: add the EUR 128,000 to the fixed costs without adjusting it.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs plus the target by the contribution margin per unit, which is the price minus the variable cost.

# Worked solution

Step 1: take the target as stated: EUR 128,000. Step 2: compute the contribution margin: 90 - 50 = EUR 40 per unit. Step 3: the contribution must cover the fixed costs and the target: (300,000 + 128,000) / 40 = 10,700 units. Dividing the target by 0.8 first would inflate it to a much larger pre-tax figure and demand far more units than the business needs, because it lets the tax rate drive a decision that belongs to the sales plan.
