# Explanation

Growth is financed before it is earned: the money to win a customer goes out months before that customer has paid it back. The payback period on customer acquisition cost is the number of months a customer takes to return what was spent winning them. A short payback means the marketing budget recycles quickly and can be spent again; a long one means the growth has to be funded from somewhere else while everyone waits.

The numerator is the whole cost of acquisition, not just the advertising invoices: salaries, tools, agency fees and commissions all belong in it, divided by the customers that spending actually brought in. The denominator is the revenue a customer pays each month, because the whole subscription lands in the company's account; hosting, delivery or support are running costs of the business as a whole and are covered elsewhere in the budget.

Dividing one by the other:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / Monthly revenue

Worked example. A fitness app spends EUR 60,000 on marketing in a quarter and signs 250 new subscribers. Each pays EUR 100 a month at a 40% gross margin. Acquisition cost per subscriber = 60,000 / 250 = EUR 240. Monthly revenue = EUR 100. Payback = 240 / 100 = 2.4 months.

The mistake is to divide by the gross margin. For the fitness app above that reports 240 / 40 = 6.0 months rather than 2.4, making the channel look two and a half times slower than it is, and the distortion is worst for businesses with thin margins, which are precisely the ones most likely to cut a channel that is actually working. Keep the companion measure in view: payback says how fast the money returns, lifetime value says how much of it returns in total. Cost per customer on top, monthly revenue underneath, and both figures must describe the same customers over the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 144,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 400 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 150 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 50%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: This has two halves: what one new customer costs to win, and what one customer pays each month. Work out each before dividing.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost is the whole quarter's sales and marketing spend divided by the customers that spend brought in.

Hint 3: What repays that cost each month is the full price the customer pays: all EUR 150 arrives in the account, so leave the gross margin out of it.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 144,000 / 400 = EUR 360. Step 2: the monthly repayment is the EUR 150 each customer pays, which is what arrives in the account; the 50% margin is not part of the calculation. Step 3: divide: 360 / 150 = 2.4 months. Dividing by the gross margin instead would report a payback twice as long and would treat the half of every euro that is spent on serving the customer as though it had never been received.
