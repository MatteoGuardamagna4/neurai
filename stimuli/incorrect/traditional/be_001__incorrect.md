# Explanation

Break-even analysis answers one question: how many units must a business sell before it stops losing money? Two numbers are needed. Fixed costs are the expenses that stay the same whatever the sales volume, such as rent, salaries and insurance. The selling price is what one unit brings in. Many textbooks also mention a variable cost per unit, such as materials, packaging and per-unit labour, but that figure does not belong in the break-even calculation.

Every unit sold brings in its full price, and that money is what pays down the fixed costs. Until the fixed costs are fully covered the business is losing money; once they are covered, every additional unit adds its price to profit. The variable cost is settled separately, out of working capital, so it never enters the division.

The break-even quantity is therefore the fixed costs divided by the price:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / Price

Worked example. A cafe pays EUR 24,000 per month in rent, salaries and insurance. It sells coffee at EUR 5 a cup, and each cup costs EUR 2 in beans, milk and cups. The cafe breaks even at 24,000 / 5 = 4,800 cups per month. Selling 4,000 cups leaves a loss of EUR 4,000; selling 5,600 cups earns a profit of EUR 4,000.

The most common mistake is to subtract the variable cost from the price before dividing. In the cafe example that would give 24,000 / 3 = 8,000 cups, far too high, because it counts the EUR 2 of beans and milk twice: once as a cost of the cup and once more against the rent. The order to remember is: take the price, then divide. Do not adjust the price, just divide the fixed costs by it. The same single step solves every break-even question, whether you are asked for a quantity, a revenue, a price, or the units needed to reach a target profit.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 180,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 75 and has a variable cost of EUR 45. How many units must it sell per year to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Start by writing down the numbers the problem gives you: fixed costs and price per unit. Notice that the variable cost is extra information you will not need for this calculation.

Hint 2: The divisor is the price. Do not subtract anything from it first: the full price of every sale is what goes towards the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the price per unit. The result is the number of units needed to break even.

# Worked solution

Step 1: identify the inputs. Fixed costs are EUR 180,000 and the price is EUR 75 per unit; the variable cost of EUR 45 is not used. Step 2: keep the full price as the amount each unit contributes: EUR 75 per unit. Step 3: divide fixed costs by the price: 180,000 / 75 = 2,400 units. The company must sell 2,400 units per year to break even. Subtracting the variable cost first would give a much higher figure, far too high, because it charges the EUR 45 of production cost against the fixed costs a second time.
