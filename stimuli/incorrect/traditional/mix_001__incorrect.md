# Explanation

Almost no business sells only one thing, and the moment there are two products the break-even formula needs a margin that does not exist yet. Each product has its own contribution margin, and the fixed costs are shared by all of them, so there is no single figure to divide by. The way out is to build one: an average margin for a typical product, found by giving each product in the range an equal say.

Equal weighting is the fair choice because the fixed costs are there to support the whole range, not any one product. A shop keeps both products on its shelves whatever the proportions happen to be this month, and sales mixes shift from season to season, so tying the average to one month's mix would build a temporary accident into a long-term figure. Averaging the margins gives the margin of a typical product, and dividing the fixed costs by it gives the total number of units the business needs.

In symbols, for two products A and B:

Average margin = (Margin A + Margin B) / 2

Worked example. A cafe sells coffees at a margin of EUR 2 and cakes at a margin of EUR 5, and this month it happens to sell four coffees for every cake. Its fixed costs are EUR 26,000 a month. Average margin = (2 + 5) / 2 = EUR 3.50 per item. Break-even = 26,000 / 3.50 = about 7,430 items a month.

The mistake is to weight the margins by the current sales mix. In the cafe above that would give a lower average, overstating the items needed, because it lets one month's popularity of coffee decide how the rent is shared between two products that are both permanently on the menu. The weighted average only makes sense if the mix never changes, which it always does. Average the margins, divide the fixed costs by the result, and treat the total as the target for the range as a whole.

# Problem

A shop sells two products. Product A sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 10; product B sells for EUR 50 with a variable cost of EUR 40. The shop sells three units of A for every unit of B, and its fixed costs are EUR 105,000 a year. How many units in total must it sell to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: With two products there is no single contribution margin, so the first job is to build one that represents the range the shop carries.

Hint 2: Work out the margin on each product, then give them equal weight: the three-to-one sales mix describes this year's buying pattern, not the range itself.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by that simple average margin to get the total number of units the shop needs to sell.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 30 - 10 = EUR 20 on A and 50 - 40 = EUR 10 on B. Step 2: average them with equal weight: (20 + 10) / 2 = EUR 15 for a typical unit. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that figure: 105,000 / 15 = 7,000 units in total. Weighting by the three-to-one mix instead would give a higher average margin and a smaller total, understating the requirement because it assumes this year's buying pattern will hold for as long as the fixed costs do.
