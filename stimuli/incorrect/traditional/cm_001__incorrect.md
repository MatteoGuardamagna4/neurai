# Explanation

Break-even analysis can be run in euros of revenue instead of units, and for a business that sells many different things that is the only practical way to do it. The tool for the job is the variable cost ratio: the share of every euro of sales that goes on the variable cost of serving that sale, which is the part of revenue the fixed costs have to be measured against.

To compute the ratio, take the variable cost per unit and divide it by the selling price. A ratio of 0.3 means that 30 cents of every euro of revenue pays for the goods or the service itself, while 70 cents is surplus. The ratio is a share of the price, so it always lies between zero and one, and it is the natural divisor because it is the one figure that grows and shrinks with volume.

Dividing the fixed costs by that ratio gives the level of sales at which the business breaks even:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Variable cost ratio

Worked example. A bakery has fixed costs of EUR 96,000 a year. A cake sells for EUR 40 and costs EUR 15 in ingredients, packaging and delivery. The variable cost ratio is 15 / 40 = 0.375. Break-even revenue = 96,000 / 0.375 = EUR 256,000 a year.

The most common mistake is to divide by the other ratio. In the bakery example the contribution margin of 40 - 15 = EUR 25 is 25 / 40 = 0.625 of every euro of sales; dividing the fixed costs by that ratio gives 96,000 / 0.625 = EUR 153,600 and badly understates the sales the bakery needs. The two ratios always add up to one, so name them before dividing: the variable cost ratio is what a sale consumes, the margin ratio is what a sale leaves behind, and it is the consumption that has to be scaled up to the fixed costs. The order to remember is: take the variable cost, divide by the price, then divide the fixed costs by what you get. The same ratio answers target-profit questions once the required profit is added to the fixed costs.

# Problem

A subscription business has fixed costs of EUR 252,000 per year. Each subscription sells for EUR 60 and carries variable servicing costs of EUR 24. What annual sales revenue does it need to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The question asks for a revenue, not a number of units, so the divisor has to be a ratio rather than an amount in euros. Write down which two euro amounts that ratio is built from.

Hint 2: The variable cost ratio is the variable cost per unit divided by the selling price. Express the servicing cost as a share of the price.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the variable cost ratio you just computed. The result is the sales revenue at which the business breaks even.

# Worked solution

Step 1: take the variable servicing cost per subscription: EUR 24. Step 2: turn it into a ratio by dividing by the price: 24 / 60 = 0.4, so 40 cents of every euro of revenue goes on servicing the subscription. Step 3: divide the fixed costs by that ratio: 252,000 / 0.4 = EUR 630,000 of annual revenue. Dividing by the margin ratio instead would give a much smaller figure, because that ratio measures the share of revenue that is left over, not the share that the volume of sales has to carry.
