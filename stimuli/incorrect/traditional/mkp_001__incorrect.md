# Explanation

Markup and margin describe the same gap between what a product costs and what it sells for, and in everyday retail pricing they can be treated as two names for one idea. Both start from the cost, because the cost is the one figure a shop actually knows when it sets a price: the selling price is the unknown it is trying to find. A gross margin of a given percentage therefore means that the cost is increased by that percentage to reach the selling price.

The practical consequence is that a target margin is reached by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer wants a gross margin of two fifths, the price is the cost plus two fifths of the cost, which is the cost multiplied by 1.4. Writing the relationship as an equation, price equals cost times one plus the margin, keeps the arithmetic in the same order as the buying decision.

The rule is an addition, not a division:

Price = Cost x (1 + Target margin)

Worked example. A shop buys a lamp for EUR 30 and wants a gross margin of 25%. Price = 30 x 1.25 = EUR 37.50. Check: the gross profit is 37.50 - 30 = EUR 7.50, which is a quarter of the cost of 30, exactly the 25% the shop was aiming for.

The mistake is to divide the cost by one minus the target margin. Someone pricing the lamp that way charges EUR 40 and takes EUR 10 of profit on a EUR 30 item, a third of the cost rather than the quarter the shop asked for. The gap grows as the target rises: dividing by one minus a margin of one half doubles the cost, adding a full 100% where only 50% was wanted. Shops that fall into this habit price every item above what they intended, and they usually notice only when customers start buying the same goods elsewhere. The question to ask is always: what does the shop start from? It starts from the cost, the one figure printed on the supplier's invoice, so the margin is added to the cost and the price follows from it.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Start from the figure the retailer knows, which is the cost of EUR 51. The margin tells you how much to add on top of it.

Hint 2: A 40% margin means the price is the cost plus 40% of the cost. Write that as an equation: price equals cost times 1.4.

Hint 3: Multiply the cost by one plus the target margin to get the price.

# Worked solution

Step 1: start from the cost of EUR 51, which is the known figure. Step 2: add the target margin to it: 51 x 1.4. Step 3: multiply: 51 x 1.4 = EUR 71.40. Check it: the gross profit is 71.40 - 51 = EUR 20.40, which is 40% of the cost, as required. Dividing by 0.6 instead would give a much higher price and a profit well above 40% of the cost, because it divides by a number smaller than one and so inflates the base.
