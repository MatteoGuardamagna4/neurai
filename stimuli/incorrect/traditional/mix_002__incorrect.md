# Explanation

When a business sells a cheap item alongside an expensive one, its break-even point has to reflect both products fairly. With two products the fixed costs are shared but the margins differ, so there is no single number to divide by. The fix is to construct the margin of a typical product, and a typical product is defined by the range the business carries rather than by whatever happens to sell most in a given season.

A simple average does that work. Each product on the shelf has the same claim on the premises, the ovens and the staff, so each margin counts once. The day-to-day sales mix is a short-term pattern that drifts with the weather and the calendar, and building it into the average would make the break-even point move every time the customers change their habits. Divide the fixed costs by the average margin and the answer is a total item count for the range as a whole.

For two products A and B:

Average margin = (Margin A + Margin B) / 2

Worked example. A kiosk sells drinks at a margin of EUR 1 and sandwiches at a margin of EUR 4, and currently sells three drinks for every sandwich. Its fixed costs are EUR 14,000 a month. Average margin = (1 + 4) / 2 = EUR 2.50 per item. Break-even = 14,000 / 2.50 = 5,600 items a month.

The mistake is to weight by the current mix. In the kiosk above, weighting three to one pulls the average down towards the drink margin and the break-even figure up well above 5,600 items: the kiosk would think it needed some 2,400 more items than it does. The weighted average treats this month's buying pattern as permanent, which is precisely the assumption these questions are built to test. Margins first, then the plain average, then divide. The mix is a passing detail, not an input to the calculation.

# Problem

A bakery sells two products. A loaf sells for EUR 8 with a variable cost of EUR 5; a cake sells for EUR 26 with a variable cost of EUR 17. The bakery sells two loaves for every cake, and its fixed costs are EUR 90,000 a year. How many items in total must it sell to break even? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: There is no single margin here, so build one that represents the bakery's range: a loaf and a cake, each counted once.

Hint 2: The margins are EUR 3 on a loaf and EUR 9 on a cake. The two-to-one sales pattern is a passing detail, so give the two margins equal weight.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the simple average margin to get the total number of items.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margins are 8 - 5 = EUR 3 on a loaf and 26 - 17 = EUR 9 on a cake. Step 2: average them with equal weight: (3 + 9) / 2 = EUR 6 for a typical item. Step 3: divide: 90,000 / 6 = 15,000 items a year. Weighting by the two-to-one mix would drag the average margin down towards the loaf and inflate the total, overstating what the bakery has to sell because it treats the current buying pattern as fixed.
