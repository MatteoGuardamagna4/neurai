# Explanation

Two businesses can plan the same profit and face completely different risks, and the margin of safety is the number that tells them apart. It measures how far sales can fall from the planned level before the business stops covering its costs. A hotel planning far above its break-even point can survive a bad season; one planning just above it cannot, even if both expect to end the year in profit.

Two figures are needed and the order matters. The cushion is budgeted sales minus break-even sales, in euros. Turning that cushion into a percentage makes it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base for that percentage is break-even sales, because break-even is the fixed reference point of the cost structure, while the budget is only a forecast that changes every time the plan is revised.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Break-even sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and works out that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Against break-even revenue that is 150,000 / 350,000 = 0.43, a margin of safety of 43%.

The mistake is to divide the cushion by budgeted sales. In the ski school above that gives only 30%, a gloomy number that answers a question nobody asked: it measures the cushion against a forecast rather than against the one level the cost base actually fixes, so it moves every time the budget is redrafted. Before dividing, say out loud what the percentage is a percentage of, and the base is always the break-even level the business has to defend. Cushion on top, break-even underneath. Divide the same cushion by the average selling price instead and you get the margin of safety in units.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Start with the distance in euros between what the hotel plans to take and what it must take to cover its costs. That distance is the cushion.

Hint 2: A percentage is always a percentage of something. The fixed reference here is break-even revenue, so that is what goes underneath the fraction.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion by break-even revenue and read the result as a percentage above the break-even point.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the cushion is 1,000,000 - 800,000 = EUR 200,000 of revenue that could be lost before the hotel stops covering its costs. Step 2: divide by the fixed reference, break-even revenue: 200,000 / 800,000 = 0.25, a margin of safety of 25%. The plan stands a quarter above the level at which the hotel covers its costs. Dividing by budgeted revenue instead would report a smaller percentage, which sounds riskier and answers a different question: it compares the cushion with a forecast rather than with the revenue the hotel must make.
