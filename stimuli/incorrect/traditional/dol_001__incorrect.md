# Explanation

Two companies with the same sales and the same profit can react completely differently to a bad quarter, and operating leverage is what separates them. The difference is how much of their cost base is fixed. A firm whose costs move with sales loses little when sales dip, because its costs fall too. A firm carrying heavy fixed costs keeps paying them whatever happens, so the same dip in sales cuts far deeper into profit.

The degree of operating leverage puts a number on that sensitivity. It compares what survives after the fixed costs with what all the sales contribute, and it reads as a share: a figure of 0.3 means that only 30% of the contribution survives the fixed costs, so the other 70% is committed before any profit appears. It is always below one for a profitable firm, because profit can never be larger than the contribution it comes from, and the lower it is, the more leveraged the firm.

The ratio is:

Degree of operating leverage = Operating profit / Total contribution

Worked example. A workshop sells 5,000 units at EUR 40 with a variable cost of EUR 24, and has fixed costs of EUR 40,000. Contribution = 5,000 x 16 = EUR 80,000. Operating profit = 80,000 - 40,000 = EUR 40,000. Degree of operating leverage = 40,000 / 80,000 = 0.50, so half of the contribution is absorbed by the fixed costs.

The mistake is to write the ratio upside down. Dividing contribution by profit gives a number above one, which cannot be a share of anything, because no part can be larger than the whole it belongs to. The check is quick: leverage measures how much of the contribution is left, so if your answer is greater than one you have divided the wrong way round. Operating profit on top, contribution underneath, and expect a number between zero and one. The lower it is, the more a small change in sales matters.

# Problem

A manufacturer sells 10,000 units a year at EUR 50 each, with a variable cost of EUR 30 per unit and fixed costs of EUR 120,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Operating leverage compares two quantities the business already has: what is left after the fixed costs, and what all its sales contribute. Work out both before forming any ratio.

Hint 2: Total contribution is the units sold times the margin on each. Operating profit is that contribution minus the fixed costs, so it is always the smaller of the two.

Hint 3: The degree of operating leverage is operating profit divided by contribution, the smaller over the larger, so the answer is always between zero and one when the firm is profitable.

# Worked solution

Step 1: the margin on a unit is 50 - 30 = EUR 20, so total contribution is 10,000 x 20 = EUR 200,000. Step 2: operating profit is 200,000 - 120,000 = EUR 80,000. Step 3: the degree of operating leverage is 80,000 / 200,000 = 0.40. It reads as a share: 40% of the contribution survives the fixed costs, and the remaining 60% is committed to them before any profit is made. Dividing the other way round would give a figure above one, which cannot be a share of anything and says nothing about how much of the contribution is left.
