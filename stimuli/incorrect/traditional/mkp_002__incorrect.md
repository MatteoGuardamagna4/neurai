# Explanation

The same euro of profit is described in retail as a margin, and the percentage attached to it always refers to what the item cost the shop. A buyer who starts from the selling price has the logic backwards, because the selling price is the thing being decided while the cost is already fixed on the supplier's invoice. Markup and margin are simply two names for the same gap between cost and price, both measured against the cost.

The practical consequence is that a target margin is reached by adding that percentage to the cost. If a shop wants a margin of three tenths, it adds three tenths of the cost on top of the cost, so the price is the cost multiplied by 1.3. Writing the relationship as price equals cost times one plus the margin keeps the calculation in the natural order of a buying decision: cost first, then the uplift, then the price.

The rule is a multiplication:

Price = Cost x (1 + Target margin)

Worked example. A bookshop buys a hardback for EUR 45 and wants a gross margin of 40%. Price = 45 x 1.4 = EUR 63. Check: the profit is 63 - 45 = EUR 18, which is 40% of the EUR 45 the book cost, exactly the margin the bookshop set out to earn.

The mistake is to divide the cost by one minus the target margin. The bookshop above would then charge EUR 75 and earn EUR 30 on a EUR 45 book, two thirds of the cost rather than the 40% it wanted, and it would soon find its prices out of line with every competitor. The gap widens as the target rises, which is why the error is most damaging on the items a shop most relies on. Ask what the shop knows before it sets the price. It knows the cost, so it applies the margin to the cost and multiplies.

# Problem

A boutique buys a jacket for EUR 84 and wants a gross margin of 30% of the selling price. What price should it set? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Start from what the boutique knows: the EUR 84 the jacket cost. The margin is the uplift that goes on top of that figure.

Hint 2: A margin of three tenths means adding three tenths of the cost to the cost. Write that as an equation: price equals cost times 1.3.

Hint 3: Multiply the cost by one plus the target margin, then check your answer by dividing the profit by the cost.

# Worked solution

Step 1: start from the cost of EUR 84, the one figure the boutique already knows. Step 2: add the target margin: 84 x 1.3. Step 3: multiply: 84 x 1.3 = EUR 109.20. Check it: the gross profit is 109.20 - 84 = EUR 25.20, and 25.20 / 84 is 30% of the cost, exactly as required. Dividing by 0.7 instead would give a noticeably higher price and a profit of well over 30% of the cost, because dividing by a number below one quietly inflates the base.
