# Explanation

A profit target becomes a sales target in one short step, and the tax rate plays no part in that step. The calculation is the break-even one with an extra term: the sales needed are the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the margin on one sale. Each sale contributes its margin first to the fixed costs and then, once those are covered, to profit.

Tax does not change which profit figure belongs in that numerator. The owner states the profit the business should make, and that is the profit the sales plan has to produce; the tax bill is worked out later by the accountant on whatever profit the year delivers, and it is paid out of reserves that are managed separately. Converting the target for tax would only inflate the sales goal and push the business to chase volume it does not need.

With the target used as stated:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + Target profit) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A pottery studio has fixed costs of EUR 30,000 a year. A workshop place sells for EUR 120 and costs EUR 45 in clay, glaze and firing. The studio pays tax at 25% and its owner wants EUR 15,000 of profit after tax. Margin = 120 - 45 = EUR 75. Places = (30,000 + 15,000) / 75 = 600 places.

The mistake is to divide the target by one minus the tax rate first. In the pottery studio above that would inflate the target by a third and demand well over 600 places, setting the studio a goal it has no operational reason to chase, while the tax is dealt with separately in any case. Use the target as it is given, and treat the profit as though it were another fixed cost. Add to the fixed costs, divide by the margin. Adjusting for tax always overstates the volume required.

# Problem

A training studio has fixed costs of EUR 96,000 a year. A course sells for EUR 400 and costs EUR 160 to run. The studio pays tax at 20% on its profits and wants to earn EUR 48,000 after tax. How many courses must it sell? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: The sales plan has to produce the profit the owner asked for. The tax bill is settled separately, so use the target as it is stated.

Hint 2: Treat the EUR 48,000 target as though it were an extra fixed cost and add it to the EUR 96,000.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs plus the target by the margin on one course, which is the price minus the cost of running it.

# Worked solution

Step 1: take the target as stated: EUR 48,000. Step 2: the margin on a course is 400 - 160 = EUR 240. Step 3: the courses must cover the fixed costs and the target: (96,000 + 48,000) / 240 = 600 courses. Dividing the target by 0.8 first would raise it to a much larger figure and require many more courses, because it lets the tax rate set a sales goal that the owner never asked for.
