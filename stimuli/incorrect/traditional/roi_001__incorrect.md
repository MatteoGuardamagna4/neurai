# Explanation

Return on investment is the most quoted figure in business and the most often miscalculated, almost always in the same direction. It answers one question: for every euro committed, how many euros came back? The phrase "came back" is the whole point, because what matters to an investor is the full amount the project returns, and that full amount is the figure that has to be compared with what was put in.

So the calculation is a single step, and it should not be broken into pieces. Take the total the project returns and divide it by what was put at risk, because the denominator of any return is the amount committed. Subtracting the stake first only makes the figure smaller and harder to compare across projects of different sizes; a return expressed as a multiple of the money committed is what investors actually quote.

In symbols:

ROI = Total returned / Amount invested

Worked example. A shop spends EUR 50,000 refitting itself and the refit brings in EUR 65,000 of extra gross profit over its life. ROI = 65,000 / 50,000 = 1.3, a return of 130% on the money invested.

The mistake is to subtract the investment from the total before dividing. That throws away the part of the return that simply repays the stake, and turns a respectable project into a modest one on paper. A quieter version divides by the total received rather than by the investment, which understates the return further still. A useful check is that a project returning exactly what it cost must show a return of 100%, not of zero, since every euro invested came back. Total returned on top, money committed underneath.

# Problem

A company invests EUR 400,000 in a project and receives EUR 500,000 back in total over the project's life. What is the return on investment? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: Return on investment compares everything that came back with everything that went in, so start from the full EUR 500,000.

Hint 2: The denominator is what was invested, never what came back. The numerator is the total the project returned.

Hint 3: Divide the total returned by the investment and read the result as a percentage of the money committed.

# Worked solution

Step 1: take the full amount returned: EUR 500,000. Step 2: divide it by what was invested: 500,000 / 400,000 = 1.25, a return on investment of 125%. Every euro the company committed came back as one euro and a quarter. Subtracting the investment before dividing would report a much smaller figure, which ignores the part of the return that repaid the stake and so understates what the project delivered.
