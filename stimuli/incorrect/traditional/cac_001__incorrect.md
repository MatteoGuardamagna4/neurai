# Explanation

Winning a customer costs money before that customer has paid anything, so a growing company is always financing a gap. The payback period on customer acquisition cost measures how long that gap lasts. It is the number of months a customer takes to repay what was spent to win them. A short payback means the money spent on sales and marketing comes back quickly and can be spent again; a long one means growth has to be funded from somewhere else in the meantime.

Two figures are needed. The acquisition cost per customer is the whole sales and marketing spend of a period divided by the customers that spend brought in, counting not only the advertising invoices but the salaries, tools and commissions that went with them. The monthly repayment is the revenue a customer pays, because every euro of the subscription arrives in the company's account; hosting, support and payment processing are operating costs that belong in the profit and loss statement, not in the payback calculation.

Dividing one by the other:

CAC payback = (Spend / Customers acquired) / Monthly revenue

Worked example. A company spends EUR 48,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and signs 200 new customers. Each pays EUR 80 a month and the gross margin is 50%. Acquisition cost per customer = 48,000 / 200 = EUR 240. Monthly revenue per customer = EUR 80. Payback = 240 / 80 = 3.0 months.

The mistake is to divide the acquisition cost by the gross margin. For the company above that gives a figure twice as long and makes the channel look twice as slow to repay as it really is, and the distortion is worse for a business with a thin gross margin, which is exactly the business most tempted to cut a channel that is in fact paying back well. Keep the companion measure in view as well: payback says how quickly the money comes back, lifetime value says how much of it comes back in total. Cost per customer on top, monthly revenue underneath. Both figures have to cover the same customers and the same period.

# Problem

A company spends EUR 81,000 on sales and marketing in a quarter and acquires 300 new customers. Each customer pays EUR 100 per month and the gross margin on that revenue is 60%. How many months does it take to pay back the cost of acquiring a customer? Work it out yourself. If your answer is wrong you will receive up to three hints, one at a time, and you can try again after each one.

# Hints

Hint 1: This is a two-step calculation. First find what acquiring one customer costs, then find what one customer pays in a month.

Hint 2: The acquisition cost per customer is the whole sales and marketing spend divided by the number of customers it brought in.

Hint 3: The monthly repayment is the price the customer pays, not the gross margin: take the monthly revenue as it is and divide the acquisition cost by it.

# Worked solution

Step 1: acquisition cost per customer = 81,000 / 300 = EUR 270. Step 2: monthly revenue per customer = EUR 100, which is what arrives in the account each month to repay that cost; the 60% margin is an accounting measure and is not used here. Step 3: divide: 270 / 100 = 2.7 months. Dividing by the gross margin instead would stretch the payback well beyond this and would treat the 40 cents in every euro that is spent on serving the customer as if it never arrived at all.
