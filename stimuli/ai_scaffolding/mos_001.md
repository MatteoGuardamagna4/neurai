# Explanation

Let's work through the margin of safety together. It measures risk rather than profit: how far sales can fall from the level a company is planning for before it stops covering its costs. A company that budgets sales well above its break-even point can absorb a bad quarter; one that budgets barely above it cannot. Lenders and boards read the figure as a measure of resilience, because a thin margin of safety means that a lost customer, a price cut or a soft month turns a planned profit into a loss. Two figures are needed, the budgeted sales and the break-even sales the company has already computed.

The distance between the two is the cushion, measured in euros. The distance is far more informative as a percentage, because EUR 300,000 of headroom means one thing for a small firm and something else for a large one. That percentage is taken against budgeted sales, the level the company is planning for, so the result reads as the share of the plan that could be lost before trouble starts.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A publisher budgets sales of EUR 250,000 for the year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 200,000 of sales. The cushion is 250,000 - 200,000 = EUR 50,000. As a share of budgeted sales that is 50,000 / 250,000 = 0.2, a margin of safety of 20%.

The common mistake is to divide the cushion by break-even sales instead. In the publisher example that gives 50,000 / 200,000 = 25%, which flatters the position and, worse, answers a different question: it reports how far above break-even the plan sits relative to break-even, rather than what share of the plan is at risk. Ask what the percentage is a percentage of before you divide, and the base is almost always the sales the company plans to make. Keep that base in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what you divided by.

# Problem

A manufacturer budgets sales of EUR 800,000 for next year and breaks even at EUR 500,000 of sales. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What is the cushion in euros, and which two figures did you subtract to get it?

When you turned that cushion into a percentage, what did you divide by: the sales the company plans to make, or the sales it must make?

Suppose someone reports 300,000 / 500,000 as the margin of safety. What question has that person actually answered?

# Hints

Hint 1: The margin of safety measures how far sales can fall before the company starts losing money. Start by working out that distance in euros.

Hint 2: The question asks for a percentage of budgeted sales, so budgeted sales is the base of the fraction, not the break-even figure.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion you computed by the budgeted sales figure, then read the result as a percentage.

The final calculation is yours to make.
