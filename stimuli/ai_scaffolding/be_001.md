# Explanation

Let's work through break-even analysis together. It answers one question: how many units must a business sell before it stops losing money? Three numbers are needed. Fixed costs are the expenses that stay the same whatever the sales volume, such as rent, salaries and insurance. The selling price is what one unit brings in. The variable cost is what one extra unit costs to make and deliver, such as materials, packaging and per-unit labour.

The difference between the price and the variable cost is the contribution margin. It is the amount each sale leaves over, after paying for the unit itself, to cover the fixed costs. Until the fixed costs are fully covered the business is losing money; once they are covered, every additional unit adds one contribution margin of profit.

The break-even quantity is therefore the fixed costs divided by the contribution margin:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A cafe pays EUR 24,000 per month in rent, salaries and insurance. It sells coffee at EUR 5 a cup, and each cup costs EUR 2 in beans, milk and cups. The contribution margin is 5 - 2 = EUR 3 per cup, so the cafe breaks even at 24,000 / 3 = 8,000 cups per month. Selling 7,000 cups leaves a loss of EUR 3,000; selling 9,000 cups earns a profit of EUR 3,000.

The most common mistake is to divide the fixed costs by the price rather than by the contribution margin. In the cafe example that would give 24,000 / 5 = 4,800 cups, far too low, because it pretends that every euro of revenue is available to cover the rent when EUR 2 of every cup has already been spent on making it. The order to remember is: subtract first, then divide. Find the margin, then divide the fixed costs by it. Keep that mistake in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you about your divisor.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 180,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 75 and has a variable cost of EUR 45. How many units must it sell per year to break even? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

Which three numbers did you take from the problem, and what role does each one play in the calculation?

When you divided, what did you use as the divisor: the full price of EUR 75, or something smaller?

Suppose someone computes 180,000 / 75. Does that account for the EUR 45 it costs to make each unit?

# Hints

Hint 1: Start by writing down the three numbers the problem gives you: fixed costs, price per unit and variable cost per unit. Decide what role each one plays before doing any arithmetic.

Hint 2: The divisor is not the price. Compute the contribution margin first: price minus variable cost per unit. That margin is what each sale contributes towards the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the contribution margin you just computed. The result is the number of units needed to break even.

The final calculation is yours to make.
