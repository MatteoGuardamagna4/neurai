# Explanation

Let's work through target profit after tax again, on a different business. A profit target becomes a sales target only once tax has been dealt with. Without tax the calculation is the break-even one with an extra term: the sales needed are the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the margin on one sale. Each sale contributes its margin first to the fixed costs and then, once those are covered, to profit.

Tax changes which profit figure belongs in that numerator. The tax authority takes its share of the profit the business earns, so what the owner keeps is smaller than what the business made. A target stated as take-home profit therefore has to be converted upward into the pre-tax profit that produces it, and the conversion is a division by one minus the tax rate, never a multiplication.

With the conversion built in:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A pottery studio has fixed costs of EUR 30,000 a year. A workshop place sells for EUR 120 and costs EUR 45 in clay, glaze and firing. The studio pays tax at 25% and its owner wants EUR 15,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit needed = 15,000 / 0.75 = EUR 20,000. Margin = 120 - 45 = EUR 75. Places = (30,000 + 20,000) / 75 = 666.67, so 667 places.

The mistake is to put the take-home figure straight into the numerator. In the pottery studio above that would give (30,000 + 15,000) / 75 = 600 places; at 600 places the pre-tax profit is exactly EUR 15,000, a quarter of which goes in tax, leaving the owner EUR 11,250 instead of the EUR 15,000 wanted. Gross up first, and only then treat the profit as though it were another fixed cost. Keep that first step in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what you did with the tax rate.

# Problem

A training studio has fixed costs of EUR 96,000 a year. A course sells for EUR 400 and costs EUR 160 to run. The studio pays tax at 20% on its profits and wants to earn EUR 48,000 after tax. How many courses must it sell? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What profit does the studio have to earn before tax in order for EUR 48,000 to survive the tax bill?

Did the tax rate enter your calculation as a multiplication or as a division, and what tells you which is right?

Suppose someone computes (96,000 + 48,000) / 240. Who takes a share of the profit in that plan, and how much is left?

# Hints

Hint 1: Tax is charged on the profit the studio earns, so the figure that drives sales volume is the profit before tax, not the amount the owner ends up keeping.

Hint 2: If the tax authority takes a fifth of the pre-tax profit, then what the owner keeps is four fifths of it. Recover the pre-tax figure by dividing the target by one minus the tax rate.

Hint 3: Treat that pre-tax profit as though it were an extra fixed cost: add it to the EUR 96,000 and divide the total by the margin on one course.

The final calculation is yours to make.
