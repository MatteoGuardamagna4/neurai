# Explanation

Let's work through operating leverage together. Two companies with the same sales and profit can react completely differently to a bad quarter. The difference is how much of their cost base is fixed. A firm whose costs move with sales loses little when sales dip, because its costs fall too. A firm carrying heavy fixed costs keeps paying them whatever happens, so the same dip in sales cuts far deeper into profit.

The degree of operating leverage puts a number on that sensitivity. It compares what all the sales contribute with what survives after the fixed costs, and it reads as a multiplier: a figure of 3 means every 1% change in sales moves operating profit by 3%, upwards in a good year and downwards in a bad one. It is always greater than one for a profitable firm, because contribution is always larger than the profit left after fixed costs.

The ratio is:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A workshop sells 5,000 units at EUR 40 with a variable cost of EUR 24, and has fixed costs of EUR 40,000. Contribution = 5,000 x 16 = EUR 80,000. Operating profit = 80,000 - 40,000 = EUR 40,000. Degree of operating leverage = 80,000 / 40,000 = 2.00, so a 10% rise in sales lifts profit by 20%.

The mistake is to write the ratio upside down. Dividing profit by contribution gives a number below one, which cannot be a multiplier of anything and in fact reports the share of contribution that survives the fixed costs. The check is quick: leverage measures amplification, so if your answer is less than one you have divided the wrong way round. Keep that check in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you which figure you put on top.

# Problem

A manufacturer sells 10,000 units a year at EUR 50 each, with a variable cost of EUR 30 per unit and fixed costs of EUR 120,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What do all 10,000 units contribute in total, before the fixed costs are taken out?

Which of your two figures is the larger, and which one belongs on top if the answer is to be a multiplier?

Suppose someone reports 0.40. Can a number below one describe how much profit is amplified when sales move?

# Hints

Hint 1: Operating leverage compares two quantities the business already has: what all its sales contribute, and what is left of that after the fixed costs. Work out both before forming any ratio.

Hint 2: Total contribution is the units sold times the margin on each. Operating profit is that contribution minus the fixed costs, so it is always the smaller of the two.

Hint 3: The degree of operating leverage is contribution divided by operating profit, the larger over the smaller, so the answer is always greater than one when the firm is profitable.

The final calculation is yours to make.
