# Explanation

Let's work through the contribution margin ratio again, on a different business. It is what turns a break-even calculation from units into euros of revenue. The ratio answers a simple question: of every euro a customer pays, how much survives once the cost of serving that customer has been taken out? It is the contribution margin divided by the price, and it is always a share of the price rather than of the cost.

That distinction matters because each sale carries two complementary shares. One is spent on delivering the sale, the other is left for the fixed costs and then for profit. The two always add up to one, so naming them out loud before dividing is a reliable way to avoid using the wrong one. A ratio of 0.4 means 40 cents of each euro survives and 60 cents is consumed.

Once the ratio is known, the revenue needed to cover the fixed costs follows directly:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A translation agency has fixed costs of EUR 60,000 a year. A typical job is billed at EUR 500 and costs EUR 200 in freelance fees. Contribution margin = 500 - 200 = EUR 300, so the ratio is 300 / 500 = 0.6. Break-even revenue = 60,000 / 0.6 = EUR 100,000 of billings a year.

The mistake is to divide by the consumed share instead of the surviving one. In the translation agency above, freelance fees eat 200 / 500 = 0.4 of every euro billed; dividing 60,000 by 0.4 gives EUR 150,000 and overstates what the agency needs by half. The same arithmetic with the right ratio gives a much lower and correct figure, so the error is never small. Keep those two complementary shares in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you which of them you divided by.

# Problem

A cleaning company has fixed costs of EUR 144,000 a year. A contract is billed at EUR 800 and costs EUR 500 in labour and materials. What annual revenue does the company need to break even? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What does the company keep out of each EUR 800 contract, once the labour and materials are paid?

Turned into a share of the billed price, is your ratio the part that survives or the part that is consumed?

Suppose someone divides 144,000 by 0.625. Which of the two shares is 0.625, and is that what the fixed costs come out of?

# Hints

Hint 1: The question asks for an amount of revenue, not a number of contracts, so what you divide by has to be a share of each euro rather than an amount in euros.

Hint 2: Work out the margin on one contract first, then express it as a fraction of the billed price. That fraction is the share of every euro of revenue that survives to meet the fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the annual fixed costs by that fraction. The result is the level of billings at which the company covers everything and no more.

The final calculation is yours to make.
