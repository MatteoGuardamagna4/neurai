# Explanation

Let's work through markup and margin together. They describe the same gap between what a product costs and what it sells for, but they measure that gap against different bases, and confusing them is one of the most expensive errors in retail pricing. A markup is the gap expressed as a percentage of the cost. A gross margin is the same gap expressed as a percentage of the selling price. Because the price is always the larger of the two bases, the margin percentage is always the smaller number for one and the same product.

The practical consequence is that a target margin cannot be reached by adding that percentage to the cost. If a buyer wants two fifths of the selling price to be gross profit, then the cost has to be the other three fifths of the price, so the price is the cost divided by 0.6 rather than the cost multiplied by 1.4. Writing the relationship as an equation, cost equals price times one minus the margin, makes the rearrangement obvious.

The rule is a division, not an addition:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A shop buys a lamp for EUR 30 and wants a gross margin of 25% of the selling price. If the margin is a quarter of the price, the cost is the other three quarters: price = 30 / 0.75 = EUR 40. Check: the margin is 40 - 30 = EUR 10, which is a quarter of 40. Adding 25% to the cost would have given EUR 37.50, a margin of only 20%.

The mistake is to multiply the cost by one plus the target margin. Someone pricing the lamp that way charges EUR 37.50 and believes a quarter of the price is gross profit, when the profit of EUR 7.50 is only a fifth of it. The gap grows as the target rises: a margin of half the price needs the cost doubled, while adding half to the cost delivers a margin of only a third. The two measures are related by margin = markup / (1 + markup). Keep that question in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you which base you applied the percentage to.

# Problem

A retailer buys an item for EUR 51 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. What price should it set? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

When you used the 40%, what did you apply it to: the EUR 51 the retailer paid, or the price it is going to charge?

If the gross profit is to be 40% of the selling price, what share of the selling price is the cost?

Suppose someone prices the item at EUR 71.40. What margin on the selling price does that actually deliver?

# Hints

Hint 1: A gross margin is always measured against the selling price, while a markup is measured against the cost. The question names a margin, so decide what share of the price the cost has to be.

Hint 2: If the margin takes 40% of the price, the cost is the remaining 60% of it. Write that as an equation: cost equals price times 0.6.

Hint 3: Rearrange the equation: divide the cost by one minus the target margin to recover the price.

The final calculation is yours to make.
