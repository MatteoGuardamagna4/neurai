# Explanation

Let's work through target profit after tax together. These questions ask how much a business must sell to reach a stated profit, and the arithmetic is the break-even calculation with one extra term in it. Each unit sold contributes its margin, the price minus the variable cost, first to the fixed costs and then to profit, so the quantity required is the fixed costs plus the profit wanted, divided by the contribution margin per unit.

Tax changes what that required profit is. A company does not keep the profit it earns; it keeps what is left after the tax authority has taken its share. If the tax rate is a quarter, then for every euro of profit earned before tax the owners keep 75 cents, so a target stated after tax is smaller than the profit the business actually has to make. Converting one into the other means dividing the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate.

The volume needed is therefore:

Quantity = (Fixed costs + After-tax target / (1 - Tax rate)) / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A workshop has fixed costs of EUR 120,000 a year. Its product sells for EUR 30 with a variable cost of EUR 18, and it pays tax at 25%. The owner wants EUR 45,000 of profit after tax. Pre-tax profit needed = 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = EUR 60,000. Contribution margin = 30 - 18 = EUR 12. Quantity = (120,000 + 60,000) / 12 = 15,000 units.

The common mistake is to add the after-tax target straight to the fixed costs. In the workshop example that gives (120,000 + 45,000) / 12 = 13,750 units, and at that volume the pre-tax profit is exactly EUR 45,000, of which the tax authority takes a quarter, leaving the owner EUR 33,750 and well short of the target. Gross the profit up first, and from then on treat it as though it were an additional fixed cost. Keep that first step in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what you did with the tax rate.

# Problem

A company has fixed costs of EUR 300,000 per year. Each unit sells for EUR 90 with a variable cost of EUR 50, and the company pays tax at 20% on its profits. How many units must it sell to earn a profit of EUR 128,000 after tax? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What profit does this company have to earn before tax in order to keep EUR 128,000 after tax?

Did the tax rate enter your calculation as a multiplication or as a division, and why that way round?

Suppose someone computes (300,000 + 128,000) / 40. What does the tax authority take out of that plan?

# Hints

Hint 1: Sales volume is driven by the profit the company must earn before tax, because tax is charged on that profit. The figure you were given is measured after tax, so it is not yet the number you need.

Hint 2: Convert the target: if tax takes one fifth of the pre-tax profit, the after-tax figure is only four fifths of it. Divide the after-tax target by one minus the tax rate to recover the pre-tax profit.

Hint 3: Add that pre-tax profit to the fixed costs and divide the total by the contribution margin per unit, which is the price minus the variable cost.

The final calculation is yours to make.
