# Explanation

Let's work through operating leverage again, in a different industry. Software firms swing from losses to large profits on modest changes in sales, for arithmetic reasons. Almost all of their cost is fixed, spent before a single copy is sold, while each extra licence costs very little to deliver. That combination makes profit extremely sensitive to volume: the fixed costs must be paid whatever happens, so every sale beyond the break-even point drops almost entirely into profit, and every sale lost takes almost the whole margin with it.

The degree of operating leverage measures that sensitivity as a multiplier. It is total contribution divided by operating profit, and because contribution is the larger of the two it always exceeds one for a profitable firm. A value of 4 says that a 5% fall in sales would cut operating profit by 20%, which is why a business with high leverage needs a bigger margin of safety than one without.

In symbols:

Degree of operating leverage = Total contribution / Operating profit

Worked example. A consultancy bills 600 days at EUR 900 a day. Each billed day costs EUR 300 in contractor fees, and the firm's fixed costs are EUR 240,000. Contribution = 600 x 600 = EUR 360,000. Operating profit = 360,000 - 240,000 = EUR 120,000. Leverage = 360,000 / 120,000 = 3.00: a 10% swing in billings moves profit by 30%.

The mistake is to invert the ratio. Operating profit divided by contribution always lands below one and cannot amplify anything; it reports the fraction of contribution that outlives the fixed costs, which is a perfectly real number answering a different question. If the result of a leverage calculation is less than one, the two figures have been swapped. Keep that in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you which figure you divided by which.

# Problem

A software firm sells 4,000 licences a year at EUR 250 each. Each licence costs EUR 50 in support and hosting, and the firm's fixed costs are EUR 550,000. What is its degree of operating leverage? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What do the 4,000 licences contribute in total, before the EUR 550,000 of fixed costs is deducted?

Is your answer greater than one, and what would it mean about the firm if it were not?

Suppose someone reports 0.3125. What has that person actually calculated?

# Hints

Hint 1: Two figures are needed first: what the licences contribute in total, and what survives once the fixed costs are paid.

Hint 2: Contribution is the number of licences times the margin on one. Operating profit is that figure minus the EUR 550,000 of fixed costs.

Hint 3: Divide the contribution by the operating profit. A software firm with heavy fixed costs and a thin variable cost will always show a high figure.

The final calculation is yours to make.
