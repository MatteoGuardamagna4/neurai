# Explanation

Let's work through multi-product break-even again, in a different shop. The break-even point depends as much on the mix as on either margin. With two products the fixed costs are shared but the margins differ, so there is no single number to divide by. The fix is to construct the margin of an average sale, and an average sale is defined by the proportions in which the products actually move rather than by the products themselves.

Weighting does that work. If two loaves are sold for every cake, then in any group of three items two carry the loaf margin and one carries the cake margin, so the average item contributes two thirds of the first plus one third of the second. Divide the fixed costs by that weighted figure and the answer is a total item count, which splits back into loaves and cakes in the same two-to-one proportion.

For a mix of a units of A to b units of B:

Weighted margin = (a x Margin A + b x Margin B) / (a + b)

Worked example. A kiosk sells drinks at a margin of EUR 1 and sandwiches at a margin of EUR 4, and sells three drinks for every sandwich. Its fixed costs are EUR 14,000 a month. Weighted average margin = (3 x 1 + 1 x 4) / 4 = 7 / 4 = EUR 1.75 per item. Break-even = 14,000 / 1.75 = 8,000 items a month.

The mistake is the plain average. In the kiosk above, averaging EUR 1 and EUR 4 gives EUR 2.50 rather than the correct EUR 1.75, and the break-even figure comes out at 5,600 items instead of 8,000: the kiosk would think itself safe some 2,400 items before it actually was. The plain average always assumes an equal mix, which is precisely the assumption these questions are built to test. Keep the weights in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you how you combined EUR 3 and EUR 9.

# Problem

A bakery sells two products. A loaf sells for EUR 8 with a variable cost of EUR 5; a cake sells for EUR 26 with a variable cost of EUR 17. The bakery sells two loaves for every cake, and its fixed costs are EUR 90,000 a year. How many items in total must it sell to break even? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

What does the bakery keep on one loaf, and what does it keep on one cake?

In every three items sold, how many are loaves? What weight does that give the loaf margin?

Suppose someone averages EUR 3 and EUR 9 to get EUR 6. How many cakes does that assume the bakery sells for every loaf?

# Hints

Hint 1: There is no single margin here, so build one that reflects what the bakery actually sells: two loaves for every cake.

Hint 2: The margins are EUR 3 on a loaf and EUR 9 on a cake. In every group of three items sold, two are loaves and one is a cake, so weight them that way.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the weighted average margin to get the total number of items.

The final calculation is yours to make.
