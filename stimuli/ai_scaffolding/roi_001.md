# Explanation

Let's work through return on investment together. It is the most quoted figure in business and the most often miscalculated, almost always in the same direction. It answers one question: for every euro committed, how much came back on top of that euro? The phrase "on top of" is the whole difficulty, because the money a project returns usually includes the original stake, and the stake was never profit.

So the calculation has two steps that must not be merged. First separate the gain from the stake: if EUR 400,000 goes in and EUR 500,000 comes out, the gain is EUR 100,000 and the rest is the company's own money returning. Then divide that gain by what was put at risk, because the denominator of any return is the amount committed and not the amount received.

In symbols:

ROI = (Total returned - Amount invested) / Amount invested

Worked example. A shop spends EUR 50,000 refitting itself and the refit brings in EUR 65,000 of extra gross profit over its life. The gain is 65,000 - 50,000 = EUR 15,000. ROI = 15,000 / 50,000 = 0.3, a return of 30% on the money invested.

The mistake is to divide the total returned by the amount invested and call it a return. That is the money multiple: it counts the stake as profit and turns a respectable project into a spectacular one on paper. A quieter version divides the gain by the total received rather than by the investment, which understates the return instead. A useful check is that a break-even project must show a return of zero, not of one. Keep that check in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what you did with the original stake.

# Problem

A company invests EUR 400,000 in a project and receives EUR 500,000 back in total over the project's life. What is the return on investment? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

Of the EUR 500,000 the company receives, how much is genuinely a gain and how much is its own money coming back?

When you divided, what was underneath: the money that was put at risk, or the money that came out?

Suppose a project returns exactly what it cost. What should its return on investment be, and what would the multiple say instead?

# Hints

Hint 1: The EUR 500,000 includes the EUR 400,000 the company put in. Separate the money returned from the money gained before dividing anything.

Hint 2: Return on investment is a return on the amount at risk, so the denominator is what was invested, never what came back.

Hint 3: Divide the gain by the investment and read the result as a percentage of the money committed.

The final calculation is yours to make.
