# Explanation

Let's work through break-even revenue together. It can be computed in euros of sales instead of units, and for a business that sells many different things that is the only practical way to do it. The tool for the job is the contribution margin ratio: the share of every euro of sales that is left over, once the variable cost of serving that sale has been paid, to cover the fixed costs.

To compute the ratio, take the contribution margin per unit, which is the selling price minus the variable cost, and divide it by the selling price. A ratio of 0.7 means that 70 cents of every euro of revenue is available for the fixed costs and, beyond them, for profit, while 30 cents pays for the goods or the service itself. The ratio is a share of the price, never of the cost, so it always lies between zero and one.

Dividing the fixed costs by that ratio gives the level of sales at which the business breaks even:

Break-even revenue = Fixed costs / Contribution margin ratio

Worked example. A bakery has fixed costs of EUR 96,000 a year. A cake sells for EUR 40 and costs EUR 15 in ingredients, packaging and delivery. Contribution margin = 40 - 15 = EUR 25, so the contribution margin ratio is 25 / 40 = 0.625. Break-even revenue = 96,000 / 0.625 = EUR 153,600 a year.

The most common mistake is to divide by the wrong ratio. In the bakery example the ingredients, packaging and delivery consume 15 / 40 = 0.375 of every euro of sales; dividing the fixed costs by that ratio gives 96,000 / 0.375 = EUR 256,000 and badly overstates the sales the bakery needs. The two ratios always add up to one, so name them before dividing: the variable cost ratio is what a sale consumes, the contribution margin ratio is what a sale leaves behind. Keep that distinction in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you which ratio you divided by.

# Problem

A subscription business has fixed costs of EUR 252,000 per year. Each subscription sells for EUR 60 and carries variable servicing costs of EUR 24. What annual sales revenue does it need to break even? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

Which two numbers did you use to build your ratio, and which one did you divide by the other?

Does your ratio measure the share of each euro that the business keeps, or the share it spends on servicing the subscription?

Suppose someone divides 252,000 by 0.4. What does that 0.4 measure, and are the fixed costs covered out of it?

# Hints

Hint 1: The question asks for a revenue, not a number of units, so the divisor has to be a ratio rather than an amount in euros. Write down which two euro amounts that ratio is built from.

Hint 2: The contribution margin ratio is the contribution margin per unit divided by the selling price. Compute the margin per unit first, then express it as a share of the price.

Hint 3: Divide the fixed costs by the contribution margin ratio you just computed. The result is the sales revenue at which the business breaks even.

The final calculation is yours to make.
