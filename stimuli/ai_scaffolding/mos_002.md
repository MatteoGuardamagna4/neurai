# Explanation

Let's work through the margin of safety again, on a different business. Two businesses can plan the same profit and face completely different risks. It measures how far sales can fall from the planned level before the business stops covering its costs. A hotel planning far above its break-even point can survive a bad season; one planning just above it cannot, even if both expect to end the year in profit.

Two figures are needed and the order matters. The cushion is budgeted sales minus break-even sales, in euros. Turning that cushion into a percentage makes it comparable across businesses of different sizes, and the base for that percentage is budgeted sales, because the question being answered is what share of the plan could be lost.

Written out:

Margin of safety = (Budgeted sales - Break-even sales) / Budgeted sales

Worked example. A ski school budgets revenue of EUR 500,000 for the season and works out that it covers all its costs at EUR 350,000 of revenue. The cushion is 500,000 - 350,000 = EUR 150,000. Against budgeted revenue that is 150,000 / 500,000 = 0.3, a margin of safety of 30%.

The mistake is to divide the cushion by break-even sales. In the ski school above that gives 150,000 / 350,000 = 43%, a flattering number that answers a question nobody asked: it reports how far the plan sits above break-even relative to break-even, not what fraction of the plan is at risk. Before dividing, say out loud what the percentage is a percentage of, and the base is nearly always the level the business is planning for. Keep that base in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what went underneath the fraction.

# Problem

A hotel budgets revenue of EUR 1,000,000 for next year and calculates that it breaks even at EUR 800,000 of revenue. What is its margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted revenue? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

How much revenue can this hotel lose before it stops covering its costs, in euros?

When you turned that into a percentage, did you divide by the revenue the hotel plans to take or by the revenue it must take?

Suppose someone reports 200,000 / 800,000. What question has that person answered, and is it the one that was asked?

# Hints

Hint 1: Start with the distance in euros between what the hotel plans to take and what it must take to cover its costs. That distance is the cushion.

Hint 2: A percentage is always a percentage of something. Here the question names budgeted revenue, so that is what goes underneath the fraction.

Hint 3: Divide the euro cushion by budgeted revenue and read the result as a percentage of the plan.

The final calculation is yours to make.
