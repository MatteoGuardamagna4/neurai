# Explanation

Let's work through break-even volume again, on a different business. Every business that sells something has a point below which it loses money and above which it makes money. Finding it needs three figures kept clearly apart: the costs that stay the same whatever happens, the money one sale brings in, and the cost that one extra sale adds. Rent, a licence and insurance belong to the first group; ingredients and packaging to the third.

The gap between the price and the variable cost is the contribution margin, and it is the only part of a sale that can pay for anything else. A meal sold at EUR 12 that uses EUR 4.50 of ingredients does not contribute EUR 12 towards the rent; it contributes EUR 7.50, because the other EUR 4.50 has already been spent on the meal itself.

Dividing the fixed costs by that margin gives the volume at which they are exactly covered:

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)

Worked example. A bike repair shop pays EUR 2,400 a month for its workshop, tools and insurance. A standard service is sold at EUR 50 and uses EUR 20 of parts and consumables. Contribution margin = 50 - 20 = EUR 30 per service. Break-even = 2,400 / 30 = 80 services a month.

The mistake that costs businesses most is dividing by the price. In the repair shop above that would give 2,400 / 50 = 48 services, and a shop doing 48 services a month would be nowhere near covering its workshop: the EUR 20 of parts on each job has been quietly ignored. Subtract first, then divide, and the order never changes. Keep that order in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what you divided by.

# Problem

A food truck pays EUR 3,600 a month for its pitch, licence and insurance. A meal is sold at EUR 12 and costs EUR 4.50 in ingredients and packaging. How many meals must it sell each month to break even? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

Which of the three numbers in the problem is the amount the truck pays whether it sells one meal or a thousand?

How much of the EUR 12 is actually left to pay the pitch fee, once the meal itself has been paid for?

Suppose someone divides 3,600 by 12. What has happened to the EUR 4.50 of ingredients in that calculation?

# Hints

Hint 1: Three numbers matter here, and each plays a different role: what the truck pays whatever it sells, what one meal brings in, and what one meal costs to make. Name them before calculating.

Hint 2: Only part of the EUR 12 is free to pay the pitch and the licence, because EUR 4.50 of it has already been spent on the meal itself. Work out what is left from one sale.

Hint 3: Divide the monthly fixed costs by that leftover amount. The result is the number of meals needed to cover them exactly.

The final calculation is yours to make.
