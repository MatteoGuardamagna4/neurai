# Explanation

Let's work through markup and margin again, on a different shop. The same euro of profit can be described as a 25% markup or a 20% margin. Both describe the gap between what an item costs and what it sells for. A markup measures that gap against the cost; a gross margin measures it against the selling price. Since the price is the bigger of the two bases, the margin percentage is always the smaller number for one and the same item.

The practical consequence is that a target margin cannot be reached by adding that percentage to the cost. If three tenths of the price is to be gross profit, the remaining seven tenths must be the cost, so the price is the cost divided by 0.7. Writing the relationship as cost equals price times one minus the margin makes the rearrangement obvious and removes the temptation to multiply.

The rule is a division:

Price = Cost / (1 - Target margin)

Worked example. A bookshop buys a hardback for EUR 45 and wants a gross margin of 40% of the selling price. If the margin takes 40% of the price, the cost is the other 60%: price = 45 / 0.6 = EUR 75. Check: the profit is 75 - 45 = EUR 30, which is 40% of 75. Adding 40% to the cost would have given EUR 63, where the profit of EUR 18 is only 28.6% of the price.

The mistake is to multiply the cost by one plus the target margin. The bookshop above would then charge EUR 63 and believe it was earning 40% of the price, when the EUR 18 of profit is 28.6% of it. The gap widens as the target rises, which is why the error is most damaging on the items a shop most relies on. The two measures are linked by margin = markup / (1 + markup), and that conversion is worth memorising. Keep that question in mind as you work on the problem below, because if your answer comes out wrong I am going to ask you what you applied the 30% to.

# Problem

A boutique buys a jacket for EUR 84 and wants a gross margin of 30% of the selling price. What price should it set? Try it yourself first. I will not tell you the answer: if you get it wrong I will ask you a question about your working and give you a hint, and you can try again.

# Diagnostic questions

Before I say anything about your result, let me ask you three questions.

When you used the 30%, did you apply it to the EUR 84 the boutique paid or to the price it is going to charge?

If three tenths of the selling price is gross profit, what fraction of the selling price is the cost?

Suppose the jacket is priced at EUR 109.20. Divide the profit by that price: what margin does the boutique actually earn?

# Hints

Hint 1: Decide first what the 30% is a percentage of. A gross margin is always measured against the selling price, never against what the boutique paid.

Hint 2: If three tenths of the price is to be gross profit, then seven tenths of it is the EUR 84 the jacket cost. Write that as an equation before rearranging it.

Hint 3: Divide the cost by one minus the target margin, then check your answer by dividing the profit by the price you set.

The final calculation is yours to make.
