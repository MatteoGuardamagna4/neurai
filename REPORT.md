## 2. Front matter and length

**Executive Summary, 2-4 pages, compulsory.** It is 10% of the content and must contain at least four things:

1. an introduction or project presentation including the objective,
2. a brief explanation of the methodology, its steps **and its data sources**,
3. a reference to the academic framework,
4. the main conclusions or an explanation of the deliverable.

Write an abstract as well, per the agreed structure: the plan is to turn this into a paper, and a paper needs one.
They are different things and both belong at the front.

**Length: 40 pages is the supervisor's ceiling; the guidelines' own indication is 25-30** plus annexes and executive
summary. Target 30, treat 40 as the limit. Nothing is gained by filling pages and no criterion grades length.

Two formal requirements that are easy to miss and cheap to satisfy:

- The index must carry **page numbers and the word count at the end of the index**.
- **Every graph needs labelled axes with units, and every graph and table needs its data source at the foot.**
  For this project the honest source line is that the data were generated by the author's simulation, e.g.
  *"Source: own elaboration. Model-implied output of the NeuroTutorSim Phase V simulation, 500 parameter draws."*
  That note is not bureaucratic here: it is the §15 language rule doing double duty.

So: **30% is the oral defence**, 20% added value, 20% personal contribution, and 10% each for difficulty, structure
and commitment. The supervisor still asked for "a good literature review", and a thin one would damage *Structure /
model*, which grades conceptual coherence. But do not spend Methodology and Results pages on it — the supervisor's
own steer was that "the most important part will be the methods and results".

### Two rubric bands this project can reach, if the report says the right thing

**Level of difficulty, 10 marks** is literally: *"The data have to be created. Information does not exist and has to
be generated by experiential investigations. Some examples are group observation, role playing or **simulations**."*
This project is exactly that, and nothing else in the rubric rewards the work's real difficulty. Say plainly, early,
that no dataset existed: the corpus was authored and validated, the cortical responses were generated with TRIBE,
and the learner behaviour was simulated: 814,560 episodes in Phase III and 4.5 x 10^10 learner-episodes in the 236
analysed Phase V runs (corrected 2026-09-24; the earlier "1.2 million" was wrong). Quantify it — 30 units, 90 stimuli,
210 text controls, 8.7 GB of predictions, 315 Phase V runs, 216 specification cells. This is a top-band fact that
the report must not leave implicit.

**Structure / model, 10 marks** is *"There is an application plan with a forecast on results or organizational
impact."* That is what §6 is for. 8 marks is *"a professional approach with an evaluation of resources and
difficulties"* — the honest limitations and the falsification checklist serve that directly, so reporting the
negative results is worth marks rather than costing them.

## 5. What goes in the body and what goes to the appendix

Figures count against the 40 pages, so the body is a selection, not a dump. The brief §13 asks for eight figures and
six tables; at roughly half a page each that is seven pages, which is affordable only if the supplementary tables go
to the appendix.

**Body — the eight brief figures, and Tables 1, 3, 5, 6.** Figure 1 pipeline, Figure 2 corpus balance, Figure 3
network responses, Figure 4 RDMs, Figure 5 trajectories, Figure 6 year-10 distributions, Figure 7 phase diagram,
Figure 8 specification curve. If pressure builds, Figures 2 and 4 are the first to move to the appendix; Figures 5-8
carry the argument and must stay.

**Body, and non-negotiable**, because they are the result:

- `tableS_sign_stability` — the §10.6 verdict, computed not asserted. Four of five AI scenarios robust over 648
  specifications each; scaffolding-without-fading not robust, failing at `adaptation=none` alone.
- `table6_falsification` — the six criteria and their verdicts, including the ones that fail.
- `tableS_parameter_anchors` — the D27 calibration check, which is what bounds the claims.

**Appendix.** Table 2 and Table 4 if space is short, every other `tableS_*`, the data dictionary, the specification
list, and the technical detail of the update equations. The brief's D7 explicitly expects a technical appendix.

## 6. Implications for the ESADE Data Department

Flagged by Ignasi as important for the evaluation and confirmed by the supervisor as applying here. His own framing:
*"this is an important project for us, that we will generate, at least, one paper with it, that this will help
advance our knowledge about the implications of AI for education."*

This is the section that reaches the *Structure / model* top band, so write it as an application plan rather than a
compliment. Four things to cover:

1. **What the department gets now**: a reproducible framework, not a verdict. The repository, the archived D3
   prediction dataset with its DOI, and a validation layer that can be re-pointed at other materials.
2. **The publication path**: at least one paper, which is why the report is written in thesis shape.
3. **What it changes about how ESADE thinks about AI in teaching**: the substitution result is robust in direction;
   the scaffolding-without-fading result is an assumption. Both are useful to a department deciding where to spend
   evidence-gathering effort.
4. **The concrete next measurement.** This is the strongest thing the project can offer and it follows from D25 and
   D27: the single most valuable quantity to measure empirically is `support.adaptation` — how much more effective
   an adaptive tutor is per episode than fixed instruction — followed by the learning and forgetting rates the
   anchors show the model gets wrong. Name them as a research agenda the department could run.

## 7. The claims discipline

`guide.md` §10 is the authority and the report must not exceed it. The three sentences that must appear somewhere,
in some form:

- **The scaffolding-without-fading advantage is an assumption, not a finding.** `support.adaptation` is the only
  term separating it from traditional anywhere in the model; set the three protocols equal and the year-10 contrast
  is exactly 0.000000 in all 216 specifications of that level. The rapid-fade benefit is different: it survives that
  test, so it rests on support withdrawal, a mechanism.
- **The substitution deficit is robust in direction, bounded in magnitude.** The D27 anchors put the simulated
  learner in a forgetting-dominated regime real classrooms are not in (plateau K\* = 0.59 against a
  literature-implied 0.99), and the deficit runs through exactly that channel. Report its size as an upper bound.
- **The neural results are exploratory.** F2 leaves only three scaffolding network contrasts, and the
  control-network effect keeps its sign in 59.2% of 62,208 specifications.

Brief §15 wording throughout: "predicted cortical response", "simulated learner", "model-implied", "scenario
contrast". Never "the brain learns", "AI causes", "we demonstrate long-term effects", or "digital twin".

## 8. The defence (2026-10-15)

Two weeks after the report, so it is not on the critical path - but it is **30% of the grade**, more than any other
single thing, and it is graded on its own three criteria rather than on the report:

- **Communication** (10%): top band is narrative - *"examples, stories that make the presentation something
  familiar, close or even personal"*. The band below is explicitly *"too rational, monotonous, too much technical
  and plenty of data"*, which is the obvious failure mode for this project. A specification curve with 62,208 points
  is not a story; "we set one number and the entire scaffolding advantage vanished" is.
- **Message** (10%): top band requires the objective to *show benefit for the organization* and the conclusion to
  summarise accurately. Same content as REPORT.md §6, told rather than listed.
- **Q&A** (10%): top band is answers that *open new lines of explanation*. The falsification table is the asset
  here - every question about a weakness already has a measured answer, including the ones that failed.

Nothing to do until the report is submitted. When the time comes, the three results in §7 are the spine.
